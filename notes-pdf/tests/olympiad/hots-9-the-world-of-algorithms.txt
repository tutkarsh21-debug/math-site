Q: Using Euclid's algorithm, the GCD of 48 and 18 is
- 3
* 6
- 9
- 12
W: $48 = 2 \times 18 + 12$, $18 = 1 \times 12 + 6$, $12 = 2 \times 6 + 0$.

Q: The GCD of 252 and 105 is
- 7
- 14
* 21
- 42
W: $252 = 2 \times 105 + 42$, $105 = 2 \times 42 + 21$, $42 = 2 \times 21 + 0$.

Q: The GCD of 91 and 35 is
- 5
* 7
- 13
- 35
W: $91 = 2 \times 35 + 21$, $35 = 1 \times 21 + 14$, $21 = 1 \times 14 + 7$, $14 = 2 \times 7 + 0$.

Q: An algorithm subtracts the smaller number from the larger until the two numbers are equal. Starting with 36 and 24, the final number is
- 6
* 12
- 24
- 36
W: $(36, 24) \to (12, 24) \to (12, 12)$.

Q: The output of the algorithm "$s = 0$; for $n = 1$ to 4 add $n$ to $s$; output $s$" is
- 4
- 6
* 10
- 24
W: $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10$.

Q: The remainder step of Euclid's algorithm for 100 and 4 gives remainder
* 0
- 1
- 4
- 25
W: $100 = 25 \times 4 + 0$, so the GCD is 4 after one step.

Q: Which property must every algorithm have?
- It must use only numbers
* It must end after a finite number of steps
- It must have exactly 10 steps
- It must use a computer
W: An algorithm has to give an answer, so it has to stop.

Q: When 8 and 7 are added in the units place of a long addition, the digit written and the carry are
- 5 and 0
- 1 and 5
* 5 and 1
- 15 and 0
W: $8 + 7 = 15$, so write 5 and carry 1.
