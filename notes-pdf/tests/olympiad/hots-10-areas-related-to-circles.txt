Q: The area of a circle of radius 14 cm is (take $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$)
- 88 square cm
- 308 square cm
* 616 square cm
- 1232 square cm
W: $\dfrac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = 616$.

Q: The circumference of a circle of diameter 21 cm is (take $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$)
- 33 cm
- 44 cm
* 66 cm
- 132 cm
W: $\dfrac{22}{7} \times 21 = 66$.

Q: The area of a quadrant (sector of $90^\circ$) of a circle of radius 14 cm is
- 77 square cm
* 154 square cm
- 308 square cm
- 616 square cm
W: $\dfrac{1}{4} \times 616 = 154$.

Q: The length of the arc of a sector of angle $120^\circ$ in a circle of radius 21 cm is
- 22 cm
* 44 cm
- 66 cm
- 132 cm
W: $\dfrac{120}{360} \times 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 21 = \dfrac{1}{3} \times 132 = 44$.

Q: The circumference of a circle is 44 cm. Its radius is
- 3.5 cm
* 7 cm
- 14 cm
- 22 cm
W: $2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times r = 44$ gives $r = 7$.

Q: The area of a ring between two concentric circles of radii 10 cm and 7 cm is
- $3\pi$ square cm
- $17\pi$ square cm
* $51\pi$ square cm
- $149\pi$ square cm
W: $\pi(10^2 - 7^2) = 51\pi$.

Q: The radius of the circle whose area equals the sum of the areas of two circles of radii 3 cm and 4 cm is
- 5.5 cm
- 7 cm
* 5 cm
- 3.5 cm
W: $\pi R^2 = \pi(9 + 16) = 25\pi$, so $R = 5$.

Q: A wheel of radius 21 cm makes 100 revolutions. The distance travelled is
- 66 m
* 132 m
- 264 m
- 1320 m
W: One revolution is $2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 21 = 132$ cm, so 100 revolutions are 13 200 cm, which is 132 m.
