Q: Which of these is a proposition?
- Close the door
- What is your name?
* 5 is greater than 3
- $x + 1 = 4$
W: A proposition is a statement that is true or false. "5 is greater than 3" is true.

Q: The converse of "If a figure is a square, then it is a rectangle" is
- If a figure is not a square, then it is not a rectangle
* If a figure is a rectangle, then it is a square
- If a figure is a rectangle, then it is not a square
- A square is always a rectangle
W: The converse swaps the two parts of the "if ... then" statement.

Q: A counterexample to "All prime numbers are odd" is
- 3
- 9
- 15
* 2
W: 2 is a prime number and it is even.

Q: Which statement is the converse of "If $x > 0$, then $x^2 > 0$", and is it true?
- "If $x^2 > 0$, then $x > 0$"; true
* "If $x^2 > 0$, then $x > 0$"; false
- "If $x \le 0$, then $x^2 \le 0$"; true
- "If $x > 0$, then $x^2 > 0$"; false
W: The converse is false: $x = -2$ gives $x^2 = 4 > 0$ but $x$ is not greater than 0.

Q: "The sum of two even numbers is even" is
* true, because $2a + 2b = 2(a + b)$
- false, because $2 + 4 = 6$
- true, only for small numbers
- not a proposition
W: The sum of any two even numbers is a multiple of 2.

Q: "If a number is a multiple of 4, then it is even" is true. Its converse is
- true
* false, because 6 is even but not a multiple of 4
- false, because 8 is even
- not a proposition
W: The converse is "If a number is even, then it is a multiple of 4", and 6 is a counterexample.

Q: To show that a statement is false, it is enough to give
* one counterexample
- five examples
- an example where it is true
- a long explanation
W: A single case where the statement fails shows that it is not always true.

Q: "If a number is divisible by 9, then it is divisible by 3" is
* true
- false, because 3 is smaller than 9
- false, because 12 is divisible by 3
- not a proposition
W: If a number is a multiple of 9 it is also a multiple of 3.
