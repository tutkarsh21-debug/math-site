Q: The area of a sector of radius 6 cm and angle 60° is
- $2\pi$ sq cm
* $6\pi$ sq cm
- $12\pi$ sq cm
- $36\pi$ sq cm
W: $\dfrac{60}{360} \times \pi \times 36$.

Q: The length of an arc of a circle of radius 14 cm that subtends 90° at the centre is (take $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$)
- 11 cm
* 22 cm
- 44 cm
- 88 cm
W: $\dfrac{90}{360} \times 2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14$.

Q: The area of a quadrant of a circle of radius 14 cm is (take $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$)
- 77 sq cm
* 154 sq cm
- 308 sq cm
- 616 sq cm
W: $\dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 196$.

Q: The minute hand of a clock is 21 cm long. The area it sweeps in 10 minutes is (take $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$)
- 115.5 sq cm
* 231 sq cm
- 462 sq cm
- 1386 sq cm
W: In 10 minutes it turns 60°: $\dfrac{60}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 441$.

Q: The perimeter of a sector of radius 21 cm and angle 60° is (take $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$)
- 22 cm
- 43 cm
* 64 cm
- 85 cm
W: The arc is 22 cm, and two radii add 42 cm.

Q: A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends a right angle at the centre. The area of the minor segment is (take $\pi = 3.14$)
- 78.5 sq cm
- 50 sq cm
* 28.5 sq cm
- 21.5 sq cm
W: Sector $= \dfrac{1}{4} \times 3.14 \times 100 = 78.5$, triangle $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 10 = 50$.

Q: The area of a sector is one-sixth of the area of the circle. The angle of the sector is
- 30°
- 45°
* 60°
- 90°
W: $\dfrac{\theta}{360°} = \dfrac{1}{6}$.

Q: A circle has area 300 sq cm. A minor sector has angle 120°. The area of the corresponding major sector is
- 100 sq cm
- 150 sq cm
* 200 sq cm
- 240 sq cm
W: The major sector has angle 240°, which is two-thirds of the circle.

Q: The circumference of a circle is 88 cm. Its area is (take $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$)
- 154 sq cm
- 308 sq cm
* 616 sq cm
- 1232 sq cm
W: The radius is 14 cm, and $\dfrac{22}{7} \times 196 = 616$.

Q: The length of an arc of a sector of radius $r$ and angle $\theta$ (in degrees) is
- $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$
* $\dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$
- $\dfrac{\theta}{180} \times 2\pi r$
- $\theta \times \pi r$
W: The arc is the same fraction of the circumference as the angle is of 360°.
