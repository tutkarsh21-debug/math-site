Q: A Rs 100 share is quoted at a premium of Rs 20. Its market value is
- Rs 80
- Rs 100
* Rs 120
- Rs 20
W: Market value = nominal value + premium.

Q: The dividend on a share is always calculated on its
- market value
* nominal (face) value
- premium
- selling price
W: The dividend rate is a percentage of the face value.

Q: A man holds 50 shares of nominal value Rs 100 each, and the dividend is 8%. His annual income is
- Rs 40
* Rs 400
- Rs 800
- Rs 4000
W: 8% of Rs 100 is Rs 8 a share, and $50 \times 8 = 400$.

Q: Rs 3600 is invested in Rs 100 shares quoted at Rs 120. The number of shares bought is
- 36
* 30
- 43
- 18
W: $3600 \div 120 = 30$.

Q: A company pays a dividend of 10% on Rs 100 shares quoted at Rs 125. The rate of return on the investment is
- 10%
- 12.5%
* 8%
- 7.5%
W: Rs 10 is earned on Rs 125 invested: $\dfrac{10}{125} \times 100$.

Q: A Rs 50 share is quoted at a discount of Rs 10. Its market value is
- Rs 60
* Rs 40
- Rs 45
- Rs 10
W: Market value = nominal value − discount.

Q: Rs 9000 is invested in 8% Rs 100 shares quoted at Rs 90. The annual income is
- Rs 720
* Rs 800
- Rs 900
- Rs 648
W: 100 shares are bought, each paying Rs 8.

Q: A man sells 60 shares at Rs 150 each. The sale proceeds are
- Rs 6000
* Rs 9000
- Rs 2500
- Rs 900
W: $60 \times 150$.

Q: Which is the better investment: 12% Rs 100 shares at Rs 120, or 8% Rs 100 shares at Rs 90?
* The first, with a return of 10%
- The second, with a return of 10%
- The second, with a return of about 8.9%
- Both give the same return
W: The first returns $\dfrac{12}{120} = 10\%$; the second returns $\dfrac{8}{90}$, about 8.9%.

Q: To get an annual income of Rs 600 from 10% Rs 100 shares quoted at Rs 110, the sum to be invested is
- Rs 6000
* Rs 6600
- Rs 5400
- Rs 660
W: 60 shares are needed, costing $60 \times 110$.
