Q: To draw a tangent to a circle at a point P on it, we draw
- a line through P and the centre
* the perpendicular at P to the radius OP
- the bisector of OP
- a chord through P
W: A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

Q: To draw tangents to a circle with centre O from an external point P, the first step is to
- join P to any point on the circle
* bisect OP and draw a circle on OP as diameter
- draw a perpendicular at P to OP
- draw a chord parallel to OP
W: This circle cuts the given circle at the two points of contact, because the angle in a semicircle is 90°.

Q: The centre of the circumscribed circle of a triangle is the point where
- the angle bisectors meet
* the perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet
- the medians meet
- the altitudes meet
W: That point is equidistant from the three vertices.

Q: The centre of the inscribed circle of a triangle is the point where
* the angle bisectors meet
- the perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet
- the medians meet
- the altitudes meet
W: That point is equidistant from the three sides.

Q: The radius of the inscribed circle of a triangle is
- the distance from the incentre to a vertex
* the perpendicular distance from the incentre to a side
- half of the longest side
- the distance between the incentre and the circumcentre
W: The circle touches each side, so the radius meets the side at right angles.

Q: A regular hexagon is inscribed in a circle by
- bisecting the diameter three times
* stepping off the radius six times round the circle
- drawing three perpendicular diameters
- drawing tangents at six points
W: The side of the hexagon equals the radius.

Q: The circumscribed circle of a triangle passes through
- the mid-points of its sides
* its three vertices
- its incentre
- the feet of its altitudes
W: It is the circle drawn around the triangle.

Q: A point P is 10 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 6 cm. When measured, each tangent from P should be
- 4 cm
* 8 cm
- 16 cm
- 11.7 cm
W: $\sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 8$. Measuring the tangents is a check on the construction.

Q: The circumcentre of an obtuse-angled triangle lies
- inside the triangle
* outside the triangle
- on a side of the triangle
- at a vertex
W: For an acute-angled triangle it lies inside, and for a right-angled triangle on the hypotenuse.

Q: The circumcentre of a right-angled triangle is
- the vertex of the right angle
* the mid-point of the hypotenuse
- the centroid
- outside the triangle
W: The hypotenuse is a diameter of the circumscribed circle.
