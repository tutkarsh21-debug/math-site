Q: The angle of elevation of the top of a tower 20 m high from a point on the ground is 45°. The distance of the point from the foot of the tower is
- 10 m
* 20 m
- $20\sqrt{3}$ m
- 40 m
W: $\tan 45° = 1$.

Q: From a point 15 m from the foot of a pole, the angle of elevation of its top is 60°. The height of the pole is
- 15 m
* $15\sqrt{3}$ m
- $5\sqrt{3}$ m
- 30 m
W: $h = 15\tan 60°$.

Q: From the top of a cliff 100 m high, the angle of depression of a boat is 30°. The distance of the boat from the foot of the cliff is
- 100 m
- 50 m
* $100\sqrt{3}$ m
- 200 m
W: $d = \dfrac{100}{\tan 30°}$.

Q: The length of the shadow of a pole is equal to its height. The angle of elevation of the Sun is
- 30°
* 45°
- 60°
- 90°
W: $\tan\theta = 1$.

Q: A ladder 10 m long makes an angle of 60° with the ground. The height it reaches on the wall is
- 5 m
* $5\sqrt{3}$ m
- 10 m
- $10\sqrt{3}$ m
W: $h = 10\sin 60°$.

Q: Two vertical poles are 12 m and 20 m high. A wire joining their tops makes an angle of 30° with the horizontal. The length of the wire is
- 8 m
* 16 m
- $8\sqrt{3}$ m
- 4 m
W: The difference in heights is 8 m, and $\dfrac{8}{\sin 30°} = 16$.

Q: An observer 1.5 m tall stands 28.5 m from a tower 30 m high. The angle of elevation of the top of the tower from his eye is
- 30°
* 45°
- 60°
- 90°
W: The height above eye level is 28.5 m, and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{28.5}{28.5}$.

Q: The angle of elevation of the top of a tower increases when the observer
- moves away from the tower
* moves towards the tower
- sits down far away
- stays where he is
W: A shorter horizontal distance gives a larger value of $\tan\theta$.

Q: An aeroplane flying at a height of 1 km is seen at an angle of elevation of 30°. Its horizontal distance from the observer is
- 1 km
* $\sqrt{3}$ km
- 2 km
- $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ km
W: $d = \dfrac{1}{\tan 30°}$.

Q: When the Sun's angle of elevation is 30°, a tower casts a shadow $10\sqrt{3}$ m long. The height of the tower is
- 30 m
* 10 m
- $10\sqrt{3}$ m
- 20 m
W: $h = 10\sqrt{3} \times \tan 30°$.
