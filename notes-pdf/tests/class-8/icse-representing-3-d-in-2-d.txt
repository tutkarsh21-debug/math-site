Q: For a polyhedron with F faces, V vertices and E edges, Euler's formula is
- $F + E - V = 2$
* $F + V - E = 2$
- $V + E - F = 2$
- $F + V + E = 2$
W: For a cube, $6 + 8 - 12 = 2$.

Q: A triangular prism has how many faces?
- 3
- 4
* 5
- 6
W: Two triangular ends and three rectangular faces.

Q: A polyhedron has 6 vertices and 12 edges. The number of its faces is
- 6
* 8
- 10
- 18
W: $F = 2 - V + E = 2 - 6 + 12 = 8$.

Q: A pentagonal pyramid has how many vertices?
- 5
* 6
- 7
- 10
W: Five on the base and one at the top.

Q: A hexagonal prism has how many edges?
- 8
- 12
* 18
- 24
W: Six on each hexagonal end and six joining the two ends.

Q: Can a polyhedron have 10 faces, 20 edges and 15 vertices?
- Yes, it is a prism
- Yes, it is a pyramid
* No, because $10 + 15 - 20$ is not 2
- No, because the number of faces must be even
W: Euler's formula must hold for every polyhedron, and here $F + V - E = 5$.

Q: Which of these solids is not a polyhedron?
- Cube
- Square pyramid
* Cylinder
- Triangular prism
W: A polyhedron has only flat faces. A cylinder has a curved surface.

Q: A net made of four equilateral triangles folds into a
- square pyramid
* tetrahedron
- triangular prism
- cube
W: A tetrahedron (triangular pyramid) has four triangular faces.

Q: A cone stands on its base. Its front view is a
- circle
* triangle
- rectangle
- semicircle
W: From the front, the outline of the cone is a triangle. Its top view is a circle.

Q: A cuboid has how many vertices?
- 4
- 6
* 8
- 12
W: A cuboid has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.
