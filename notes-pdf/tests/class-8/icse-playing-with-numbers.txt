Q: A three-digit number has digits $a$ (hundreds), $b$ (tens) and $c$ (units). Its generalised form is
- $a + b + c$
- $abc$
* $100a + 10b + c$
- $100c + 10b + a$
W: Each digit is multiplied by its place value.

Q: The sum of a two-digit number and the number obtained by reversing its digits is always divisible by
- 9
* 11
- 10
- 2
W: $(10a + b) + (10b + a) = 11(a + b)$.

Q: The difference between a three-digit number and the number formed by reversing its digits is always divisible by
- 11 only
- 101
* 99
- 100
W: $(100a + 10b + c) - (100c + 10b + a) = 99(a - c)$.

Q: In the addition 3A + 25 = B2, where A and B are digits, the value of A + B is
- 9
- 11
* 13
- 15
W: $A + 5$ ends in 2, so A = 7 with 1 carried over. Then $3 + 2 + 1 = 6$, so B = 6.

Q: The smallest digit $x$ for which the number 52x is divisible by 6 is
- 0
* 2
- 4
- 8
W: The number must be even and its digit sum $7 + x$ a multiple of 3. $x = 2$ gives 522, with digit sum 9.

Q: Which number is divisible by 11?
- 34567
- 20202
- 71345
* 61809
W: For 61809, $6 - 1 + 8 - 0 + 9 = 22$, which is a multiple of 11.

Q: A number is divisible by 8 if
- its last digit is 8
- its last two digits are divisible by 8
* the number formed by its last three digits is divisible by 8
- the sum of its digits is divisible by 8
W: 1000 is divisible by 8, so only the last three digits matter.

Q: The number 21y5 is a multiple of 9. The digit y is
- 0
* 1
- 4
- 7
W: The digit sum $8 + y$ must be a multiple of 9, so $y = 1$.

Q: A number divisible by both 2 and 5 always ends in
* 0
- 2
- 5
- 2 or 5
W: Such a number is divisible by 10.

Q: When the sum of the two-digit numbers $ab$ and $ba$ is divided by $a + b$, the quotient is
- 9
- 10
* 11
- $a - b$
W: $ab + ba = 11(a + b)$.
