Q: The mean of 4, 7, 9, 12 and 8 is
- 7
* 8
- 9
- 10
W: The sum is 40, and $40 \div 5 = 8$.

Q: The median of 3, 9, 5, 12 and 7 is
- 5
* 7
- 9
- 7.2
W: In order: 3, 5, 7, 9, 12. The middle value is 7.

Q: The median of 4, 8, 10 and 14 is
- 8
* 9
- 10
- 18
W: With an even number of values, take the mean of the two middle ones: $(8 + 10) \div 2 = 9$.

Q: The mean of 5 numbers is 12. A sixth number, 18, is added. The new mean is
- 12
- 12.5
* 13
- 15
W: The total becomes $60 + 18 = 78$, and $78 \div 6 = 13$.

Q: If 5 is added to every value in a data set, the mean
- stays the same
* increases by 5
- is multiplied by 5
- increases by 5 times the number of values
W: Every value moves up by 5, so the balance point moves up by 5.

Q: The mean of 6, $x$, 10 and 12 is 9. The value of $x$ is
- 7
* 8
- 9
- 10
W: The total must be $4 \times 9 = 36$, so $x = 36 - 28 = 8$.

Q: If every value in a data set is doubled, the median
- stays the same
- increases by 2
* is doubled
- is halved
W: The order of the values does not change, and the middle value is doubled.

Q: In a data set, the value 2 occurs 3 times, 4 occurs 2 times and 5 occurs 5 times. The mean is
- 3.5
- 3.67
* 3.9
- 4.1
W: $\dfrac{2 \times 3 + 4 \times 2 + 5 \times 5}{10} = \dfrac{39}{10} = 3.9$.

Q: A line graph is most suitable for showing
- the parts of a whole
* how a quantity changes over time
- the favourite colours of a class
- the number of students in each section
W: Joining the points shows rises and falls from one time to the next.

Q: For the data 2, 3, 3, 4, 48, which value describes a typical item better?
- The mean, 12
* The median, 3
- The largest value, 48
- The sum, 60
W: The single large value 48 pulls the mean up to 12, but the median is not affected by it.
