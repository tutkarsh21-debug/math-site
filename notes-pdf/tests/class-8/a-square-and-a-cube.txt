Q: Which of these is a perfect square?
- 1000
* 1024
- 1050
- 1200
W: $32^2 = 1024$. The others lie between consecutive squares.

Q: A perfect square can never end in the digit
- 4
- 5
- 6
* 7
W: Squares end only in 0, 1, 4, 5, 6 or 9.

Q: The sum of the first 8 odd numbers is
- 56
* 64
- 72
- 81
W: The sum of the first $n$ odd numbers is $n^2$, so the sum is $8^2 = 64$.

Q: $\sqrt{1764}$ is equal to
- 38
- 44
* 42
- 48
W: $1764 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7^2$, so the square root is $2 \times 3 \times 7 = 42$.

Q: How many whole numbers lie strictly between $15^2$ and $16^2$?
- 15
- 29
- 31
* 30
W: Between $n^2$ and $(n+1)^2$ there are $2n$ numbers. Here $2 \times 15 = 30$.

Q: The smallest number by which 180 must be multiplied to get a perfect square is
- 2
- 3
* 5
- 10
W: $180 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5$. Only 5 is unpaired, so multiply by 5 to get 900.

Q: $\sqrt{50}$ lies between
- 5 and 6
- 6 and 7
* 7 and 8
- 8 and 9
W: $7^2 = 49$ and $8^2 = 64$, and 50 lies between them.

Q: The cube of 12 is
- 144
* 1728
- 1228
- 1278
W: $12^3 = 144 \times 12 = 1728$.

Q: $\sqrt[3]{3375}$ is equal to
* 15
- 25
- 35
- 45
W: $3375 = 3^3 \times 5^3$, so the cube root is $3 \times 5 = 15$.

Q: The smallest number by which 108 must be multiplied to get a perfect cube is
* 2
- 3
- 4
- 6
W: $108 = 2^2 \times 3^3$. One more 2 completes the triple, giving $216 = 6^3$.
