Q: The class size of the class interval 20-30 is
- 5
* 10
- 25
- 50
W: Class size = upper limit − lower limit.

Q: The class mark of the class interval 40-50 is
- 40
* 45
- 50
- 10
W: The class mark is the midpoint: $\dfrac{40 + 50}{2} = 45$.

Q: The range of the data 12, 7, 25, 18, 9 is
- 7
- 13
* 18
- 25
W: Range = largest value − smallest value = $25 - 7$.

Q: In a histogram, the bars are drawn
- with equal gaps between them
* without gaps between them
- as sectors of a circle
- only for ungrouped data
W: The class intervals are continuous, so the bars touch.

Q: In a pie chart, an item of 60 out of a total of 240 has a central angle of
- 60°
* 90°
- 120°
- 25°
W: $\dfrac{60}{240} \times 360° = 90°$.

Q: With the class intervals 10-20, 20-30, 30-40, the value 20 is counted in
- 10-20
* 20-30
- both 10-20 and 20-30
- neither class
W: In continuous classes, the upper limit is excluded from a class and placed in the next one.

Q: The frequency of an observation is
- its value
- the largest value in the data
* the number of times it occurs
- the difference between the largest and smallest values
W: Tally marks are used to count frequencies.

Q: In a pie chart, a sector with a central angle of 120° stands for what fraction of the whole?
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
* $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
W: $\dfrac{120}{360} = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

Q: Which graph is best for comparing a student's marks in five subjects?
- Histogram
* Bar graph
- Pie chart
- Tally chart
W: The subjects are separate categories, so bars with gaps are suitable.

Q: A family spends Rs 18,000 a month. In the pie chart of its spending, food has a central angle of 100°. The amount spent on food is
- Rs 1800
- Rs 4500
* Rs 5000
- Rs 6000
W: $\dfrac{100}{360} \times 18000 = 5000$.
