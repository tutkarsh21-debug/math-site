Q: The graphical solution of a pair of linear equations is given by
- the $y$-intercepts of the lines
* the point where the two lines meet
- the slopes of the lines
- the origin
W: The point of intersection satisfies both equations.

Q: If the graphs of two linear equations are parallel lines, the equations have
* no solution
- one solution
- two solutions
- infinitely many solutions
W: Parallel lines never meet.

Q: If the graphs of two linear equations coincide, the equations have
- no solution
- one solution
- two solutions
* infinitely many solutions
W: Every point of the line is a common point.

Q: The lines $x = 3$ and $y = 2$ meet at
- $(2, 3)$
* $(3, 2)$
- $(3, 0)$
- $(0, 2)$
W: The point has $x$-co-ordinate 3 and $y$-co-ordinate 2.

Q: The lines $x + y = 6$ and $x - y = 2$ meet at
- $(3, 3)$
* $(4, 2)$
- $(2, 4)$
- $(5, 1)$
W: $4 + 2 = 6$ and $4 - 2 = 2$.

Q: The area of the triangle formed by the lines $x = 0$, $y = 0$ and $x + y = 4$ is
- 4 sq units
* 8 sq units
- 16 sq units
- 2 sq units
W: The line cuts the axes at $(4, 0)$ and $(0, 4)$: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 4$.

Q: The line $2x + 3y = 6$ meets the axes at
- $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$
* $(3, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$
- $(6, 0)$ and $(0, 6)$
- $(3, 2)$ only
W: Put $y = 0$ to get $x = 3$, and $x = 0$ to get $y = 2$.

Q: The least number of points needed to draw the graph of a linear equation is
- 1
* 2
- 3
- 4
W: Two points fix a straight line. A third point is plotted as a check.

Q: The area of the triangle formed by the lines $y = x$, $x = 6$ and the $x$-axis is
- 6 sq units
- 12 sq units
* 18 sq units
- 36 sq units
W: The vertices are $(0, 0)$, $(6, 0)$ and $(6, 6)$: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 6$.

Q: The graph of $y = -2$ is a line
- parallel to the $y$-axis
* parallel to the $x$-axis and below it
- parallel to the $x$-axis and above it
- through the origin
W: Every point on it is 2 units below the $x$-axis.
