Q: The $n$th term of a sequence is $2n + 3$. Its 5th term is
- 10
* 13
- 15
- 25
W: $2 \times 5 + 3 = 13$.

Q: The common difference of the AP 3, 7, 11, 15, ... is
- 3
* 4
- 7
- 10
W: $7 - 3 = 4$.

Q: The 10th term of the AP 5, 8, 11, ... is
- 30
* 32
- 35
- 50
W: $a + 9d = 5 + 27 = 32$.

Q: The sum of the first 100 natural numbers is
- 5000
* 5050
- 5100
- 10100
W: $\dfrac{100 \times 101}{2} = 5050$.

Q: The common ratio of the GP 2, 6, 18, 54, ... is
- 2
* 3
- 4
- 12
W: $6 \div 2 = 3$.

Q: The 5th term of the GP 3, 6, 12, ... is
- 24
- 30
* 48
- 96
W: $3 \times 2^4 = 48$.

Q: A sequence is given by $a_1 = 2$ and $a_n = a_{n-1} + 5$. The term $a_4$ is
- 12
* 17
- 20
- 22
W: The terms are 2, 7, 12, 17.

Q: Which of these is an arithmetic progression?
- 1, 2, 4, 8
- 1, 4, 9, 16
* 1, 4, 7, 10
- 1, 1, 2, 3
W: Only here is the difference between consecutive terms the same (3).

Q: The sum of the first $n$ natural numbers is
- $n^2$
* $\dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2}$
- $n(n + 1)$
- $\dfrac{n(n - 1)}{2}$
W: Pair the first and last terms: there are $\dfrac{n}{2}$ pairs, each adding to $n + 1$.

Q: Sequence P starts 100, 200, 300, ... and sequence Q starts 1, 2, 4, 8, ... Which statement is true?
- P is always ahead of Q
* Q overtakes P after enough terms
- They are equal at every term
- Q is an arithmetic progression
W: A GP with ratio greater than 1 grows faster than any AP in the long run. Here the 12th term of Q is 2048, while the 12th term of P is only 1200.
