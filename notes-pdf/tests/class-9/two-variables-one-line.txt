Q: Which pair is a solution of $2x + y = 7$?
- $(1, 3)$
* $(2, 3)$
- $(3, 2)$
- $(0, 5)$
W: $2 \times 2 + 3 = 7$.

Q: The slope of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 8)$ is
- 2
* 3
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- 6
W: $\dfrac{8 - 2}{3 - 1} = 3$.

Q: For the line $y = -2x + 5$, the slope and the $y$-intercept are
- 2 and 5
- 5 and $-2$
* $-2$ and 5
- $-2$ and $-5$
W: In $y = mx + c$, $m$ is the slope and $c$ the $y$-intercept.

Q: The solution of $x + y = 10$ and $x - y = 4$ is
- $x = 6$, $y = 4$
* $x = 7$, $y = 3$
- $x = 8$, $y = 2$
- $x = 3$, $y = 7$
W: Adding gives $2x = 14$.

Q: The pair $2x + 3y = 5$ and $4x + 6y = 10$ has
- no solution
- exactly one solution
- exactly two solutions
* infinitely many solutions
W: The second equation is twice the first, so the two lines coincide.

Q: The pair $x + 2y = 4$ and $2x + 4y = 12$ has
* no solution
- exactly one solution
- exactly two solutions
- infinitely many solutions
W: The lines have the same slope but different intercepts, so they are parallel.

Q: Which line is parallel to the $x$-axis?
- $x = 3$
* $y = 3$
- $y = x$
- $x + y = 3$
W: On $y = 3$ every point is at height 3.

Q: If $y = 2x$ and $x + y = 12$, then
- $x = 3$, $y = 6$
* $x = 4$, $y = 8$
- $x = 6$, $y = 12$
- $x = 8$, $y = 4$
W: Substituting, $x + 2x = 12$.

Q: The line $3x + 2y = 12$ meets the $x$-axis at
- $(0, 6)$
- $(6, 0)$
* $(4, 0)$
- $(0, 4)$
W: Put $y = 0$: $3x = 12$.

Q: The sum of two numbers is 25 and their difference is 7. The larger number is
- 9
- 14
* 16
- 18
W: $x + y = 25$ and $x - y = 7$ give $2x = 32$.
