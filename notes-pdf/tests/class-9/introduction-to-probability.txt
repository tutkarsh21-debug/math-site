Q: The probability of an impossible event is
* 0
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- 1
- $-1$
W: On the probability scale, 0 stands for impossible and 1 for certain.

Q: A die is thrown once. The probability of getting a multiple of 3 is
- $\dfrac{1}{6}$
* $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
W: The multiples of 3 are 3 and 6: two outcomes out of six.

Q: A coin is tossed 50 times and shows heads 28 times. The experimental probability of heads is
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
* $\dfrac{14}{25}$
- $\dfrac{11}{25}$
- $\dfrac{28}{22}$
W: $\dfrac{28}{50} = \dfrac{14}{25}$.

Q: When two coins are tossed together, the number of outcomes in the sample space is
- 2
- 3
* 4
- 8
W: The outcomes are HH, HT, TH and TT.

Q: Two coins are tossed together. The probability of getting exactly one head is
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
* $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{3}$
W: HT and TH are favourable: two out of four.

Q: A bag has 4 red and 6 blue balls. One ball is drawn at random. The probability that it is red is
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
* $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
W: $\dfrac{4}{10} = \dfrac{2}{5}$.

Q: If the probability of an event is 0.35, the probability that it does not happen is
- 0.35
- 0.5
* 0.65
- 1.35
W: The two probabilities add up to 1.

Q: In a survey of 200 students, 50 walk to school. The probability that a student chosen at random walks to school is
- $\dfrac{1}{2}$
* $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- $\dfrac{1}{5}$
- $\dfrac{3}{4}$
W: $\dfrac{50}{200} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

Q: A coin is tossed three times. A tree diagram for this shows how many outcomes?
- 3
- 6
* 8
- 9
W: Each toss doubles the number of branches: $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$.

Q: As the number of trials of an experiment becomes very large, the experimental probability of an event
- moves away from the theoretical probability
* gets closer to the theoretical probability
- becomes 1
- becomes 0
W: With more trials the chance variations even out.
