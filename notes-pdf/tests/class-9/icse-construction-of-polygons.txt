Q: The side of a regular hexagon inscribed in a circle is equal to
- the diameter of the circle
* the radius of the circle
- half the radius
- $\sqrt{3}$ times the radius
W: The hexagon is made of six equilateral triangles with the centre as a common vertex.

Q: Each interior angle of a regular hexagon is
- 60°
- 108°
* 120°
- 135°
W: $\dfrac{(6 - 2) \times 180°}{6} = 120°$.

Q: A unique quadrilateral can be constructed when the number of independent measurements given is
- 3
- 4
* 5
- 2
W: A diagonal divides it into two triangles: three measurements for the first and two more for the second.

Q: In constructing trapezium ABCD with AB parallel to DC, we use the fact that
- $\angle A = \angle D$
* $\angle A + \angle D = 180°$
- AD = BC
- AC = BD
W: A and D are co-interior angles between the parallel sides.

Q: A rectangle has one side 5 cm and a diagonal 13 cm. The other side is
- 8 cm
* 12 cm
- 18 cm
- 14 cm
W: $\sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = 12$.

Q: To construct a square with diagonal 6 cm, we draw the diagonal, then its perpendicular bisector, and mark on the bisector points at a distance from the mid-point of
- 6 cm
* 3 cm
- 1.5 cm
- 12 cm
W: The diagonals of a square are equal and bisect each other at right angles.

Q: A rhombus has side 5 cm and one angle 60°. Its shorter diagonal is
- 2.5 cm
* 5 cm
- $5\sqrt{3}$ cm
- 10 cm
W: The shorter diagonal and two sides form an equilateral triangle.

Q: A parallelogram can be constructed from its two diagonals and the angle between them because the diagonals
- are equal
* bisect each other
- are perpendicular
- bisect the angles
W: Each half-diagonal can be marked from the point of intersection.

Q: Joining the centre of a regular hexagon to its vertices gives
- 3 equilateral triangles
* 6 equilateral triangles
- 6 right-angled triangles
- 12 equilateral triangles
W: The angle at the centre is $360° \div 6 = 60°$, and the two radii are equal.

Q: An angle of 120° is constructed with compasses by
- bisecting 90°
* marking two successive arcs of 60°
- bisecting 60°
- adding 90° and 45°
W: Two steps of 60° along the same arc give 120°.
