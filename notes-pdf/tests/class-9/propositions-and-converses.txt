Q: The converse of "If P, then Q" is
- If not P, then not Q
* If Q, then P
- If P, then not Q
- P and Q
W: The converse swaps the condition and the conclusion.

Q: The converse of "If a number is divisible by 4, then it is even" is
- If a number is not divisible by 4, then it is odd
- If a number is odd, then it is not divisible by 4
* If a number is even, then it is divisible by 4
- Every number is divisible by 4
W: Swap the two parts of the statement.

Q: The statement "If a number is even, then it is divisible by 4" is
- true
* false, and 6 is a counterexample
- false, and 8 is a counterexample
- false, and 3 is a counterexample
W: 6 is even but is not divisible by 4.

Q: Which number is a counterexample to "All prime numbers are odd"?
- 1
* 2
- 9
- 15
W: 2 is prime and even. (1 is not prime, and 9 and 15 are not prime.)

Q: For which statement is the converse also true?
- If a number is a multiple of 10, then it is a multiple of 5
- If a shape is a square, then it is a rectangle
* If a triangle has two equal sides, then it has two equal angles
- If $x = 2$, then $x^2 = 4$
W: A triangle with two equal angles also has two equal sides. The other converses fail (15, a non-square rectangle, and $x = -2$).

Q: Which of these is a proposition?
- Close the door.
- How old are you?
* 7 is a prime number.
- What a lovely day!
W: A proposition is a sentence that is either true or false.

Q: The words "if and only if" are used when
- a statement is false
* a statement and its converse are both true
- only the converse is true
- a statement has no converse
W: "P if and only if Q" means P gives Q and Q gives P.

Q: To show that a statement about all numbers is false, it is enough to give
- many examples where it is true
* one counterexample
- a proof of its converse
- two counterexamples
W: A single case where the statement fails disproves it.

Q: Which number is a counterexample to "The square of a number is always greater than the number"?
- 2
- 3
- $-2$
* $\dfrac{1}{2}$
W: $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$, which is smaller than $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Q: The converse of the Baudhayana-Pythagoras theorem says that if $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ for the sides of a triangle, then
- the triangle is isosceles
* the triangle is right-angled
- the triangle is equilateral
- no such triangle exists
W: The angle opposite the side $c$ is a right angle.
