Q: In triangle ABC, $\angle A = 70°$, $\angle B = 60°$ and $\angle C = 50°$. The longest side is
- AB
* BC
- CA
- all are equal
W: The longest side is opposite the greatest angle, A.

Q: In triangle ABC, AB = 5 cm, BC = 7 cm and CA = 6 cm. The greatest angle is
* $\angle A$
- $\angle B$
- $\angle C$
- none, as all are equal
W: The greatest angle is opposite the longest side, BC.

Q: Which set of lengths can be the sides of a triangle?
- 3 cm, 4 cm, 8 cm
- 2 cm, 3 cm, 5 cm
* 5 cm, 7 cm, 11 cm
- 1 cm, 2 cm, 4 cm
W: $5 + 7 > 11$. In the others the two smaller lengths do not add up to more than the largest.

Q: Two sides of a triangle are 6 cm and 9 cm. The third side $x$ satisfies
- $x > 15$
* $3 < x < 15$
- $x < 3$
- $6 < x < 9$
W: The third side lies between the difference and the sum of the other two.

Q: Of all the segments from a point to a line, the shortest is
- the longest one drawn
* the perpendicular
- the one making 45° with the line
- the one to the end of the line
W: In each right triangle formed, the perpendicular is a leg and the other segment is the hypotenuse.

Q: In a right-angled triangle, the longest side is
- the base
- the perpendicular
* the hypotenuse
- any of the three
W: It is opposite the greatest angle, 90°.

Q: The sum of any two sides of a triangle is
- equal to the third side
- less than the third side
* greater than the third side
- twice the third side
W: This is the triangle inequality.

Q: In triangle PQR, PQ > PR. Then
- $\angle Q > \angle R$
* $\angle R > \angle Q$
- $\angle Q = \angle R$
- $\angle P > \angle R$
W: The angle opposite the longer side is greater. PQ is opposite R and PR is opposite Q.

Q: Two sides of a triangle are 4 cm and 7 cm. How many whole-number values (in cm) can the third side take?
- 5
- 6
* 7
- 8
W: $3 < x < 11$, so $x$ can be 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 or 10.

Q: In triangle ABC, $\angle A = 100°$ and $\angle B = 50°$. The shortest side is
* AB
- BC
- CA
- BC and CA together
W: $\angle C = 30°$ is the smallest angle, and AB is opposite C.
