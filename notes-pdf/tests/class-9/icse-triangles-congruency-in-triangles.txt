Q: Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 60°. The exterior angle at the third vertex is
- 70°
* 110°
- 120°
- 130°
W: An exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

Q: Which of these is not a condition for congruency of triangles?
- SSS
- SAS
- ASA
* AAA
W: Triangles with equal angles have the same shape but can have different sizes.

Q: The RHS condition applies only to
- equilateral triangles
- isosceles triangles
* right-angled triangles
- obtuse-angled triangles
W: R stands for right angle, H for hypotenuse and S for side.

Q: $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$, with AB = 5 cm, BC = 7 cm and $\angle B = 60°$. Then PQ is
* 5 cm
- 7 cm
- 6 cm
- 12 cm
W: PQ corresponds to AB, because the order of the letters shows the matching vertices.

Q: C.P.C.T. stands for
- common parts of congruent triangles
* corresponding parts of congruent triangles
- corresponding points of common triangles
- congruent parts of corresponding triangles
W: Once two triangles are proved congruent, their matching sides and angles are equal.

Q: The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. The largest angle is
- 60°
* 80°
- 90°
- 100°
W: 9 parts make 180°, so one part is 20°.

Q: In triangles ABC and DEF, AB = DE, $\angle B = \angle E$ and BC = EF. The triangles are congruent by
- SSS
* SAS
- ASA
- RHS
W: The equal angle lies between the two pairs of equal sides.

Q: If $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FED$, then $\angle A$ is equal to
- $\angle D$
- $\angle E$
* $\angle F$
- $\angle B$
W: A is the first letter and matches F, the first letter of the other triangle.

Q: Two congruent triangles always have
- different perimeters
* equal areas
- different angles
- one side in common
W: Congruent figures coincide exactly, so their areas are equal.

Q: The angles of a triangle are $(x + 10)°$, $(2x - 30)°$ and $x°$. The value of $x$ is
- 40
* 50
- 55
- 60
W: $4x - 20 = 180$.
