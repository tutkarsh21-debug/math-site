Q: Which of these is an irrational number?
- $\sqrt{9}$
- 0.25
- $\dfrac{22}{7}$
* $\sqrt{7}$
W: 7 is not a perfect square, so $\sqrt{7}$ cannot be written as a fraction.

Q: The decimal expansion of $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is
- terminating
* non-terminating and recurring
- non-terminating and non-recurring
- not possible
W: $\dfrac{1}{3} = 0.333\ldots$, with the digit 3 repeating.

Q: $0.\overline{6}$ written as a fraction is
- $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- $\dfrac{6}{10}$
* $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\dfrac{6}{7}$
W: If $x = 0.666\ldots$ then $10x - x = 6$, so $x = \dfrac{6}{9} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

Q: $0.\overline{27}$ written as a fraction is
- $\dfrac{27}{100}$
* $\dfrac{3}{11}$
- $\dfrac{27}{90}$
- $\dfrac{9}{11}$
W: $100x - x = 27$, so $x = \dfrac{27}{99} = \dfrac{3}{11}$.

Q: The value of $(-7) \times (-4) - (-5)$ is
- 23
- $-33$
* 33
- $-23$
W: $28 + 5 = 33$. The product of two negatives is positive, and subtracting a negative adds.

Q: Which rational number lies between $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$?
- $\dfrac{1}{4}$
* $\dfrac{5}{12}$
- $\dfrac{7}{12}$
- $\dfrac{2}{3}$
W: $\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{4}{12}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{6}{12}$.

Q: Which of these has a terminating decimal expansion?
- $\dfrac{5}{12}$
- $\dfrac{4}{15}$
* $\dfrac{7}{40}$
- $\dfrac{2}{7}$
W: $40 = 2^3 \times 5$ has only 2 and 5 as prime factors. The other denominators contain 3 or 7.

Q: To mark $\sqrt{2}$ on the number line, we use the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs
- 1 and 2
* 1 and 1
- 2 and 2
- 1 and $\sqrt{3}$
W: $\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$.

Q: The sum of a rational number and an irrational number is
- always rational
* always irrational
- always an integer
- sometimes rational
W: If the sum were rational, subtracting the rational number would make the irrational number rational.

Q: Which of these is not defined?
- $0 \div 5$
- $0 \times 5$
- $0 - 5$
* $5 \div 0$
W: Division by zero has no meaning.
