Q: An algorithm is
- a very difficult problem
* a finite list of clear steps for solving a problem
- a computer
- a formula that works only once
W: Each step must be unambiguous, and the steps must come to an end.

Q: The number of divisors of 12 is
- 4
- 5
* 6
- 12
W: The divisors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12.

Q: The gcd of 48 and 18 is
- 2
- 3
* 6
- 9
W: $48 = 2^4 \times 3$ and $18 = 2 \times 3^2$, with common part $2 \times 3$.

Q: Using Euclid's subtraction algorithm on 21 and 15, the first step replaces the pair by
- 21 and 6
* 15 and 6
- 36 and 15
- 7 and 5
W: Replace the larger number by the difference: $21 - 15 = 6$, giving the pair 15 and 6.

Q: The division algorithm for the gcd uses the fact that, if $r$ is the remainder when $a$ is divided by $b$, then
- $\gcd(a, b) = r$
* $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, r)$
- $\gcd(a, b) = a - b$
- $\gcd(a, b) = b$
W: Any common divisor of $a$ and $b$ also divides $r$, and the other way round.

Q: The remainder when 100 is divided by 7 is
- 1
* 2
- 3
- 6
W: $100 = 7 \times 14 + 2$.

Q: The gcd of 17 and 5 is
* 1
- 5
- 17
- 85
W: 17 is prime and does not divide 5, so the only common divisor is 1.

Q: To list all the divisors of a number $n$, it is enough to test the numbers up to
- $n$
- $\dfrac{n}{2}$
* $\sqrt{n}$
- $n^2$
W: Divisors come in pairs whose product is $n$, and one of each pair is at most $\sqrt{n}$.

Q: The gcd of 56 and 20 is
- 2
* 4
- 8
- 10
W: $56 = 2 \times 20 + 16$, $20 = 1 \times 16 + 4$, $16 = 4 \times 4 + 0$. The last non-zero remainder is 4.

Q: Compared with repeated subtraction, the division algorithm for the gcd usually needs
* fewer steps
- more steps
- exactly the same number of steps
- twice as many steps
W: One division does the work of many subtractions.
