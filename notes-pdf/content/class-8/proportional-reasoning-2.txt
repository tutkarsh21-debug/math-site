# Recap of proportion
- Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when they change by the same factor. Their quotient stays constant: $\dfrac{x_1}{y_1} = \dfrac{x_2}{y_2} = k$.
- $a : b :: c : d$ exactly when $a \times d = b \times c$.
- Rule of Three: if $a : b :: c : d$ then $d = \dfrac{bc}{a}$.
?eg One cook mixes 6 cups of rice with 3 cups of dal, and another mixes 4 cups of rice with 2 cups of dal. Are the mixtures the same?
= $6 \times 2 = 12$ and $3 \times 4 = 12$
=> Yes, both are $2 : 1$

# Ratios in maps
- The **Representative Fraction (RF)** of a map is the ratio of a distance on the map to the actual distance on the ground, in the same unit.
- RF $1 : 60{,}00{,}000$ means 1 cm on the map stands for 60,00,000 cm, which is 60 km.
- Useful conversion: 1 km $= 1000$ m $= 1{,}00{,}000$ cm.
- A map gives the straight-line distance, not the road distance.
?eg A map has RF $1 : 25{,}00{,}000$. Two towns are 6.4 cm apart on the map. Find the actual distance.
= 1 cm stands for 25,00,000 cm $= 25$ km.
= $6.4 \times 25$
=> 160 km
?eg A classroom 8 m long is drawn to the scale $1 : 50$. How long is it in the drawing?
= $800 \text{ cm} \div 50$
=> 16 cm

# Ratios with more than two terms
- A ratio $a : b : c : d$ says that for every $a$ units of the first quantity there are $b$ of the second, $c$ of the third and $d$ of the fourth.
- Two such ratios are proportional, $a : b : c : d :: p : q : r : s$, when $\dfrac{a}{p} = \dfrac{b}{q} = \dfrac{c}{r} = \dfrac{d}{s}$.
- Simplest form: divide every term by the HCF of all the terms.
- If one term is known, find the value of **one part** and multiply.
?eg A spice mix uses coriander, chillies, dal and fenugreek in the ratio $8 : 4 : 2 : 1$. If only 2 chillies are available, how much of each of the others is needed?
= Chillies drop from 4 to 2, a factor of $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Apply it to every term.
=> 4 spoons of coriander, 1 spoon of dal and half a spoon of fenugreek
?eg Red, blue and white paint are mixed in the ratio $2 : 3 : 5$. With 10 litres of white, how much red and blue are needed, and how much paint is made?
= 5 parts $= 10$ litres, so 1 part $= 2$ litres.
=> 4 litres of red and 6 litres of blue; 20 litres in all
?eg A library has Odia, Hindi and English books in the ratio $3 : 2 : 1$. There are 288 Odia books. Find the others.
= 3 parts $= 288$, so 1 part $= 96$.
=> 192 Hindi books and 96 English books

# Dividing a whole in a given ratio
- To divide $x$ in the ratio $a : b : c : \ldots$, the parts are
$$\frac{a}{a + b + c + \ldots} \times x, \quad \frac{b}{a + b + c + \ldots} \times x, \quad \frac{c}{a + b + c + \ldots} \times x, \ \ldots$$
?eg Concrete is made from cement, sand and gravel in the ratio $1 : 1.5 : 3$. How much of each is needed for 110 units of concrete?
= Total parts $= 5.5$, and $110 \div 5.5 = 20$.
=> 20 units of cement, 30 of sand and 60 of gravel
?eg The angles of a triangle are in the ratio $1 : 3 : 5$. Find them.
= The sum is $180^\circ$ and there are 9 parts, so one part is $20^\circ$.
=> $20^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $100^\circ$
?eg A 150-minute practice session is split among warm-up, batting, bowling and fielding in the ratio $3 : 4 : 3 : 5$. Find the time for each.
= Total parts $= 15$; one part $= 10$ minutes.
=> 30, 40, 30 and 50 minutes
?eg There are 100 coins of ₹10, ₹5, ₹2 and ₹1 in the ratio $4 : 3 : 2 : 1$. Find the total value.
= The numbers of coins are 40, 30, 20 and 10.
= Value $= 400 + 150 + 40 + 10$
=> ₹600
?eg Can a triangle have sides in the ratio $1 : 3 : 5$?
= The sides would be $k$, $3k$, $5k$, and $k + 3k = 4k$ is less than $5k$.
=> No. The sum of two sides must be greater than the third.

# Pie charts
- A **pie chart** shows how a whole is divided into parts. Each part is a sector (slice) of a circle.
- The angle of a sector is proportional to the quantity it stands for. The angles add up to $360^\circ$.
$$\text{Angle of a sector} = \frac{\text{value of the part}}{\text{total value}} \times 360^\circ$$
- If the data are percentages: angle $=$ percentage $\times\ 3.6^\circ$.
- **To draw**: draw a circle and one radius; measure the first angle from it and draw the next radius; continue from each new radius; label the sectors.
| Grade | A | B | C | D | E | Total |
| Students | 12 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 40 |
| Angle | $108^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | $72^\circ$ | $54^\circ$ | $36^\circ$ | $360^\circ$ |

?eg 360 people chose a favourite season: 90 summer, 120 rainy and the rest winter. Find the angles for a pie chart.
= Winter $= 360 - 210 = 150$ people. Here each person is $1^\circ$.
=> Summer $90^\circ$, rainy $120^\circ$, winter $150^\circ$
?eg Favourite TV channels: entertainment 50%, sports 25%, news 15%, information 10%. Find the angles.
= Multiply each percentage by $3.6^\circ$.
=> $180^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $54^\circ$, $36^\circ$
?eg In a pie chart the sector for "car" has an angle of $60^\circ$ and stands for 18 children. How many children were surveyed? How many does a $90^\circ$ sector stand for?
= $60^\circ$ is $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of the circle, so the total is $18 \times 6 = 108$.
= $90^\circ$ is $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of 108.
=> 108 children; 27 children

# Inverse proportion
- Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when one is multiplied by a factor $n$ and the other is multiplied by $\dfrac{1}{n}$. Their **product stays constant**: $xy = k$.
- If $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ are two pairs of values, then $x_1y_1 = x_2y_2$, and so $\dfrac{x_1}{x_2} = \dfrac{y_2}{y_1}$.
| Speed (km/h) | 5 | 15 | 30 | 60 |
| Time (hours) | 18 | 6 | 3 | 1.5 |
| Speed $\times$ time | 90 | 90 | 90 | 90 |
- Examples: speed and time for a fixed distance; number of workers and days for a fixed job; number of pumps and time to fill a tank; number of people and days a stock of food lasts.
- **Direct or inverse?** Ask: if one quantity doubles, does the other double (direct) or halve (inverse)?
- These problems assume that every worker, pump or machine works at the same steady rate.
?eg Are $x$ and $y$ in inverse proportion? $x$: 40, 80, 25, 16 and $y$: 20, 10, 32, 50.
= The products are $800, 800, 800, 800$.
=> Yes
?eg 20 workers lay a road in 4 days. How long will 10 workers take?
= $20 \times 4 = 10 \times d$
=> 8 days
?eg 2 pumps fill a tank in 18 hours. How long do 4 pumps take?
= $2 \times 18 = 4 \times t$
=> 9 hours
?eg Food for 80 students lasts 15 days. If 20 more students join, how long will it last?
= $80 \times 15 = 100 \times d$
=> 12 days
?eg 42 machines make a batch of toys in 63 days. How many machines are needed to make it in 54 days?
= $42 \times 63 = m \times 54$
=> 49 machines
?eg A car takes 2 hours at 60 km/h. How long does it take at 80 km/h?
= $60 \times 2 = 80 \times t$
=> 1.5 hours

# Working together
- Take the whole job as **1 unit of work**. If a person finishes it in $t$ hours, the work done in 1 hour is $\dfrac{1}{t}$.
- Add the rates of all the workers to get the work done together in 1 hour. The time taken together is the reciprocal of that sum.
- If two workers take $a$ and $b$ hours alone, together they take $\dfrac{ab}{a + b}$ hours.
?eg Ram cuts a basket of vegetables in 1 hour and Shyam takes 1.5 hours. How long do they take together?
= Rates: 1 and $\dfrac{1}{1.5} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ of the job per hour. Together: $\dfrac{5}{3}$ per hour.
=> $\dfrac{3}{5}$ hour, that is 36 minutes
?eg A small pump fills a tank in 3 hours and a large pump in 2 hours. How long do both take together?
= Rates: $\dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{6}$ of the tank per hour
=> $\dfrac{6}{5}$ hours, that is 1 hour 12 minutes

# Common mistakes
- Using the Rule of Three for an inverse proportion. More workers mean **fewer** days.
- Treating every "more means more" situation as a proportion. Check that the factor is the same.
- Forgetting to change centimetres to kilometres when reading a map.
- Dividing by the number of terms instead of the **sum** of the terms when sharing in a ratio.
- Drawing pie-chart angles that do not add up to $360^\circ$.
- Adding the times when two people work together. Add the **rates**.
