# Curves and polygons
- A **simple curve** does not cross itself. A **closed curve** begins and ends at the same point.
- A **polygon** is a simple closed curve made only of line segments. The segments are its **sides** and their end points its **vertices**.
- A **diagonal** joins two vertices that are not next to each other.
- **Adjacent sides** share a vertex. **Adjacent vertices** are the ends of one side.

| Name | Triangle | Quadrilateral | Pentagon | Hexagon | Heptagon | Octagon | Nonagon | Decagon |

# Convex and concave polygons
- **Convex polygon**: every interior angle is less than $180^\circ$. All its diagonals lie inside it.
- **Concave polygon**: at least one interior angle is greater than $180^\circ$ (a reflex angle). At least one diagonal lies partly outside.
- Unless told otherwise, "polygon" means a convex polygon.

# Regular polygons
- A **regular polygon** has **all sides equal** and **all angles equal**. Examples: equilateral triangle, square, regular hexagon.
- A rhombus has equal sides but is not regular (its angles differ). A rectangle has equal angles but is not regular (its sides differ).

# Interior angles
- A polygon of $n$ sides can be divided into $n - 2$ triangles by the diagonals from one vertex.
$$\text{Sum of the interior angles} = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ = (2n - 4) \text{ right angles}$$
| Polygon | Sides | Sum of interior angles | Each angle if regular |
| Triangle | 3 | $180^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ |
| Quadrilateral | 4 | $360^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ |
| Pentagon | 5 | $540^\circ$ | $108^\circ$ |
| Hexagon | 6 | $720^\circ$ | $120^\circ$ |
| Octagon | 8 | $1080^\circ$ | $135^\circ$ |
| Decagon | 10 | $1440^\circ$ | $144^\circ$ |
- Each interior angle of a regular polygon $= \dfrac{(n - 2) \times 180^\circ}{n}$.
?eg Find the sum of the interior angles of a polygon with 12 sides.
= $(12 - 2) \times 180^\circ$
=> $1800^\circ$
?eg The sum of the interior angles of a polygon is $1260^\circ$. How many sides does it have?
= $(n - 2) \times 180 = 1260$, so $n - 2 = 7$
=> 9 sides
?eg Four angles of a pentagon are $100^\circ$, $110^\circ$, $95^\circ$ and $125^\circ$. Find the fifth.
= $540^\circ - 430^\circ$
=> $110^\circ$
?eg The angles of a hexagon are $x$, $x + 10^\circ$, $x + 20^\circ$, $x + 30^\circ$, $x + 40^\circ$ and $x + 50^\circ$. Find $x$.
= $6x + 150 = 720$
=> $x = 95^\circ$

# Exterior angles
- An **exterior angle** is formed when a side is produced. At each vertex, interior angle $+$ exterior angle $= 180^\circ$.
- **The sum of the exterior angles of any convex polygon, one at each vertex, is $360^\circ$.**
- For a regular polygon of $n$ sides: each exterior angle $= \dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$, so $n = \dfrac{360^\circ}{\text{exterior angle}}$.
- Each interior angle of a regular polygon $= 180^\circ - \dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$.
!fig w=300 h=190; P A 70 150 bl; P B 210 150 b; P C 250 60 tr; P D 140 20 t; P E 50 70 l; Q X 280 150; L A B; L B C; L C D; L D E; L E A; D B X; A X B C ext; cap The exterior angle at B is formed by producing AB
?eg Find each exterior angle and each interior angle of a regular polygon of 15 sides.
= Exterior: $360^\circ \div 15$. Interior: $180^\circ - 24^\circ$.
=> $24^\circ$ and $156^\circ$
?eg Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $40^\circ$. How many sides does it have?
= $360 \div 40$
=> 9
?eg Each interior angle of a regular polygon is $140^\circ$. Find the number of sides.
= Exterior angle $= 40^\circ$
=> 9
?eg Can a regular polygon have each exterior angle equal to $50^\circ$?
= $360 \div 50 = 7.2$, which is not a whole number.
=> No
?eg The ratio of each interior angle to each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $4 : 1$. Find the number of sides.
= $4x + x = 180^\circ$, so the exterior angle is $36^\circ$.
=> 10 sides

# Diagonals
- From one vertex of an $n$-sided polygon, $n - 3$ diagonals can be drawn.
$$\text{Number of diagonals} = \frac{n(n - 3)}{2}$$
?eg How many diagonals does an octagon have?
= $\dfrac{8 \times 5}{2}$
=> 20
?eg A polygon has 35 diagonals. How many sides does it have?
= $n(n - 3) = 70 = 10 \times 7$
=> 10 sides

# Common mistakes
- Using $(n - 2) \times 180^\circ$ for the sum of the exterior angles. That sum is always $360^\circ$.
- Taking $n \times 180^\circ$ as the interior angle sum.
- Calling a rhombus or a rectangle a regular polygon.
- Finding the number of sides from the interior angle by dividing $360^\circ$ by it. Use the exterior angle.
- Counting sides as diagonals.
- Accepting a number of sides that is not a whole number.
