# Triangles and rectangles
| Figure | Perimeter | Area |
| Rectangle, $l$ by $b$ | $2(l + b)$ | $l \times b$ |
| Square, side $a$ | $4a$ | $a^2$, also $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{diagonal}^2$ |
| Triangle, base $b$ and height $h$ | Sum of the sides | $\dfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h$ |
| Triangle with sides $a$, $b$, $c$ | $a + b + c$ | $\sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$, $s = \dfrac{a + b + c}{2}$ |
| Equilateral triangle, side $a$ | $3a$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2$ |
?eg Find the area of a triangle with sides 10 cm, 17 cm and 21 cm.
= $s = 24$. Area $= \sqrt{24 \times 14 \times 7 \times 3} = \sqrt{7056}$.
=> 84 sq cm
?eg The base of a triangle is 16 cm and its area is 96 sq cm. Find its height.
= $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 16 \times h = 96$
=> 12 cm

# Parallelogram and rhombus
- **Parallelogram**: area $=$ base $\times$ height, where the height is the perpendicular distance between the base and the opposite side.
- **Rhombus**: area $=$ base $\times$ height, and also $\dfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2$.
?eg A parallelogram has sides 20 cm and 12 cm. The height on the 20 cm side is 9 cm. Find the area and the height on the 12 cm side.
= Area $= 20 \times 9 = 180$ sq cm. Then $12 \times h = 180$.
=> 180 sq cm; 15 cm
?eg The diagonals of a rhombus are 30 cm and 16 cm. Find its area, side and height.
= Area $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 30 \times 16 = 240$ sq cm. Side $= \sqrt{15^2 + 8^2} = 17$ cm. Height $= 240 \div 17$.
=> 240 sq cm; 17 cm; about 14.1 cm

# Trapezium
- A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides. If their lengths are $a$ and $b$ and the perpendicular distance between them is $h$:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times (a + b) \times h$$
!fig w=330 h=180; P A 40 140 bl; P B 290 140 br; P C 220 40 tr; P D 100 40 tl; Q M 100 140; L A B; L B C; L C D; L D A; D D M; R D M B; M D C a; N A B b; T 113 95 h; cap Area of a trapezium = ½ (a + b) × h
- Reason: a diagonal splits the trapezium into two triangles with bases $a$ and $b$ and the same height $h$.
?eg The parallel sides of a trapezium are 18 cm and 12 cm and the distance between them is 8 cm. Find its area.
= $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 30 \times 8$
=> 120 sq cm
?eg The area of a trapezium is 180 sq cm and its height is 9 cm. One parallel side is 6 cm longer than the other. Find the parallel sides.
= $\dfrac{1}{2}(x + x + 6) \times 9 = 180$, so $2x + 6 = 40$
=> 17 cm and 23 cm
?eg The parallel sides of an isosceles trapezium are 20 cm and 10 cm and each of the other sides is 13 cm. Find its area.
= Each end triangle has base $\dfrac{20 - 10}{2} = 5$ cm and hypotenuse 13 cm, so the height is 12 cm.
= $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 30 \times 12$
=> 180 sq cm

# Quadrilateral
- If a diagonal has length $d$ and the perpendiculars on it from the other two vertices are $h_1$ and $h_2$:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times d \times (h_1 + h_2)$$
?eg In quadrilateral ABCD, diagonal $AC = 24$ cm and the perpendiculars from B and D on AC are 8 cm and 13 cm. Find the area.
= $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 24 \times 21$
=> 252 sq cm

# Polygon and field book
- The area of any polygon is found by dividing it into triangles, rectangles and trapeziums and adding their areas.
- **Regular hexagon** of side $a$: six equilateral triangles, area $= 6 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2 = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}a^2$.
- **Field book**: a surveyor measures along a base line and records the perpendicular offsets to the corners on each side. The field is then a set of right triangles and trapeziums.
?eg In a field book, base line AD $= 100$ m. At 30 m from A there is an offset of 20 m to B on the left; at 60 m there is an offset of 30 m to E on the right; at 80 m there is an offset of 40 m to C on the left. Find the area of the field ABCDE.
= Left side: triangle (base 30, height 20) $= 300$; trapezium between 30 m and 80 m with parallel sides 20 and 40 $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 60 \times 50 = 1500$; triangle (base 20, height 40) $= 400$.
= Right side: triangle AED with base 100 and height 30 $= 1500$.
=> $300 + 1500 + 400 + 1500 = 3700$ sq m
?eg Find the area of a regular hexagon of side 6 cm.
= $\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} \times 36$
=> $54\sqrt{3}$ sq cm, about 93.5 sq cm

# Circle
- Circumference $= 2\pi r = \pi d$. Area $= \pi r^2$. Take $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$ unless told otherwise.
- **Ring** between two concentric circles of radii $R$ and $r$: area $= \pi(R^2 - r^2)$.
- A wheel covers a distance equal to its circumference in one revolution.
?eg Find the circumference and the area of a circle of radius 14 cm.
= $2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 14$ and $\dfrac{22}{7} \times 196$
=> 88 cm and 616 sq cm
?eg The area of a circle is 154 sq cm. Find its radius and circumference.
= $r^2 = 154 \times \dfrac{7}{22} = 49$
=> 7 cm; 44 cm
?eg A circular path 3.5 m wide runs round a circular garden of radius 10.5 m. Find the area of the path.
= $\dfrac{22}{7}(14^2 - 10.5^2) = \dfrac{22}{7} \times 85.75$
=> 269.5 sq m
?eg The wheel of a cart has diameter 1.4 m. How many revolutions does it make in travelling 1.1 km?
= Circumference $= 4.4$ m. $1100 \div 4.4$
=> 250

# Paths
- Path of width $w$ outside a rectangle $l$ by $b$: area $= (l + 2w)(b + 2w) - lb$.
- Path of width $w$ inside: area $= lb - (l - 2w)(b - 2w)$.
?eg A room 12 m by 9 m has a carpet that leaves a border 0.5 m wide all round. Find the area of the border.
= Carpet: 11 m by 8 m $= 88$ sq m. Room: 108 sq m.
=> 20 sq m

# Common mistakes
- Using a slant side as the height.
- Forgetting the $\dfrac{1}{2}$ in the triangle, rhombus and trapezium formulas.
- Using the diameter instead of the radius in $\pi r^2$.
- Writing the area of a ring as $\pi(R - r)^2$.
- In field-book problems, using distances from A as the heights of the trapeziums instead of the differences between them.
- Mixing units such as centimetres and metres.
