# Simple interest
- **Principal** $P$: the money borrowed or deposited. **Rate** $R$: the interest on ₹100 for one year, written as a percentage per annum. **Time** $T$: the period in years.
- **Interest** $I$: the extra money paid for using the principal. **Amount** $A = P + I$.
$$I = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$$
- Time must be in **years**: 6 months $= \dfrac{1}{2}$ year, 73 days $= \dfrac{73}{365} = \dfrac{1}{5}$ year.
- When counting days between two dates, leave out the first day and include the last.
?eg Find the simple interest and the amount on ₹6000 at 8% per annum for 3 years.
= $I = \dfrac{6000 \times 8 \times 3}{100} = 1440$
=> Interest ₹1440; amount ₹7440
?eg Find the simple interest on ₹7300 at 10% per annum for 146 days.
= $T = \dfrac{146}{365} = \dfrac{2}{5}$ year. $I = \dfrac{7300 \times 10 \times 2}{100 \times 5}$.
=> ₹292

# Finding P, R or T
| To find | Formula |
| Principal | $P = \dfrac{100 \times I}{R \times T}$ |
| Rate | $R = \dfrac{100 \times I}{P \times T}$ |
| Time | $T = \dfrac{100 \times I}{P \times R}$ |
| Principal from the amount | $P = \dfrac{100 \times A}{100 + R \times T}$ |
?eg At what rate will ₹4000 earn ₹1200 as simple interest in 5 years?
= $R = \dfrac{100 \times 1200}{4000 \times 5}$
=> 6% per annum
?eg In how many years will ₹2500 amount to ₹3250 at 6% per annum simple interest?
= $I = 750$. $T = \dfrac{100 \times 750}{2500 \times 6}$.
=> 5 years
?eg What sum amounts to ₹5800 in 4 years at 4% per annum simple interest?
= $P = \dfrac{100 \times 5800}{100 + 16}$
=> ₹5000
?eg A sum doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. Find the rate.
= Interest equals the principal: $P = \dfrac{P \times R \times 8}{100}$.
=> 12.5% per annum
?eg A sum amounts to ₹5600 in 2 years and to ₹6400 in 3 years at simple interest. Find the sum and the rate.
= Interest for 1 year $= 800$. Interest for 2 years $= 1600$, so the sum is $5600 - 1600 = 4000$.
= $R = \dfrac{100 \times 800}{4000 \times 1}$
=> ₹4000; 20% per annum

# Compound interest
- In **compound interest** the interest of each year is added to the principal, and the next year's interest is calculated on this larger sum.
- **Amount at the end of a year becomes the principal for the next year.**
- Compound interest (C.I.) $=$ final amount $-$ original principal.
- For the first year, C.I. and S.I. are equal. After that, C.I. is greater.
| Year | Principal (₹) | Interest at 10% (₹) | Amount (₹) |
| 1 | 8000 | 800 | 8800 |
| 2 | 8800 | 880 | 9680 |
| 3 | 9680 | 968 | 10648 |
?eg Find the compound interest on ₹8000 for 3 years at 10% per annum.
= From the table the amount is ₹10,648.
=> ₹2648
?eg Find the amount on ₹5000 for 2 years when the rates for the two years are 8% and 10%.
= After year 1: $5000 + 400 = 5400$. After year 2: $5400 + 540$.
=> ₹5940

# Compound interest by formula
$$A = P\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n, \qquad \text{C.I.} = A - P$$
- Here $n$ is the number of years and the interest is compounded yearly.
- **Different rates**: $A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{R_1}{100}\right)\left(1 + \dfrac{R_2}{100}\right)$.
- **Compounded half-yearly**: use rate $\dfrac{R}{2}\%$ and $2n$ periods.
?eg Find the compound interest on ₹12,000 for 2 years at 5% per annum.
= $A = 12000 \times \left(\dfrac{21}{20}\right)^2 = 12000 \times \dfrac{441}{400} = 13230$
=> ₹1230
?eg Find the amount on ₹10,000 for 1 year at 10% per annum, compounded half-yearly.
= Two half-years at 5%: $10000 \times (1.05)^2$
=> ₹11,025
?eg What sum amounts to ₹6050 in 2 years at 10% per annum compound interest?
= $P \times 1.21 = 6050$
=> ₹5000

# Comparing C.I. and S.I.
- For 2 years: C.I. $-$ S.I. $= P\left(\dfrac{R}{100}\right)^2$. It equals one year's interest on the first year's interest.
?eg Find the difference between the C.I. and the S.I. on ₹5000 for 2 years at 6% per annum.
= $5000 \times 0.06 \times 0.06$
=> ₹18
?eg The difference between the C.I. and the S.I. on a sum for 2 years at 5% is ₹15. Find the sum.
= $P \times \dfrac{1}{400} = 15$
=> ₹6000

# Growth and depreciation
- A quantity growing by $R\%$ a year becomes $P\left(1 + \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n$ after $n$ years.
- A quantity falling by $R\%$ a year becomes $P\left(1 - \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n$.
?eg A town of 20,000 people grows by 5% a year. Find its population after 2 years.
= $20000 \times 1.05^2$
=> 22,050
?eg A machine worth ₹50,000 loses 10% of its value each year. Find its value after 2 years.
= $50000 \times 0.9^2$
=> ₹40,500

# Common mistakes
- Using months or days as $T$ without converting to years.
- Giving the amount when the interest is asked, or the reverse.
- Calculating every year's compound interest on the original principal.
- Writing $\left(1 + R\right)^n$ instead of $\left(1 + \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n$.
- For half-yearly compounding, halving the rate but not doubling the number of periods.
- Using a plus sign for depreciation.

Simple interest | $I = \dfrac{PRT}{100}$

Principal | $P = \dfrac{100I}{RT}$
Rate | $R = \dfrac{100I}{PT}$
Time | $T = \dfrac{100I}{PR}$
Principal from amount (S.I.) | $P = \dfrac{100A}{100 + RT}$

Different rates | $A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{R_1}{100}\right)\left(1 + \dfrac{R_2}{100}\right)$

C.I. $-$ S.I. for 2 years | $P\left(\dfrac{R}{100}\right)^2$
Depreciation | $P\left(1 - \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n$
