# Basic terms
- **Cost price (C.P.)**: the price at which an article is bought. **Selling price (S.P.)**: the price at which it is sold.
- **Overheads**: extra expenses such as transport, repairs and labour. They are **added to the cost price**.
- **Profit (gain)** $=$ S.P. $-$ C.P., when S.P. $>$ C.P.
- **Loss** $=$ C.P. $-$ S.P., when C.P. $>$ S.P.
- Profit and loss percentages are always calculated **on the cost price**.
$$\text{Profit}\% = \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{C.P.}} \times 100, \qquad \text{Loss}\% = \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{C.P.}} \times 100$$
?eg A table bought for ₹1600 is sold for ₹1840. Find the profit per cent.
= Profit $= 240$. $\dfrac{240}{1600} \times 100$
=> 15%
?eg A man buys a second-hand cycle for ₹2400, spends ₹300 on repairs and sells it for ₹2430. Find his loss per cent.
= Total C.P. $= 2700$. Loss $= 270$. $\dfrac{270}{2700} \times 100$
=> 10%

# Finding S.P. and C.P.
| To find | Given a profit of $p\%$ | Given a loss of $l\%$ |
| S.P. | C.P. $\times \dfrac{100 + p}{100}$ | C.P. $\times \dfrac{100 - l}{100}$ |
| C.P. | S.P. $\times \dfrac{100}{100 + p}$ | S.P. $\times \dfrac{100}{100 - l}$ |
?eg A fan costs ₹1500. At what price must it be sold to gain 12%?
= $1500 \times \dfrac{112}{100}$
=> ₹1680
?eg By selling a watch for ₹1140 a shopkeeper loses 5%. Find the cost price.
= $1140 \times \dfrac{100}{95}$
=> ₹1200
?eg By selling an article for ₹720 a man loses 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 5%?
= C.P. $= 720 \times \dfrac{100}{90} = 800$. New S.P. $= 800 \times \dfrac{105}{100}$.
=> ₹840

# Several articles
- When the C.P. of $x$ articles equals the S.P. of $y$ articles, take the C.P. of one article as ₹1.
- When articles are bought at one rate and sold at another, find the C.P. and S.P. of the **same number** of articles (use the LCM).
- Two articles sold at the **same price**, one at a gain of $x\%$ and the other at a loss of $x\%$: there is always a **loss of $\dfrac{x^2}{100}\%$** overall.
?eg The cost price of 12 pens equals the selling price of 10 pens. Find the profit per cent.
= Let the C.P. of one pen be ₹1. S.P. of 10 pens $= 12$, C.P. of 10 pens $= 10$.
=> $\dfrac{2}{10} \times 100 = 20\%$ profit
?eg Lemons are bought at 5 for ₹20 and sold at 4 for ₹20. Find the gain per cent.
= C.P. of one $= 4$; S.P. of one $= 5$.
=> 25%
?eg A man sells two radios for ₹1980 each, gaining 10% on one and losing 10% on the other. Find his overall gain or loss.
= C.P. of the first $= 1980 \times \dfrac{100}{110} = 1800$; of the second $= 1980 \times \dfrac{100}{90} = 2200$.
= Total C.P. $= 4000$; total S.P. $= 3960$.
=> A loss of ₹40, that is 1%

# Discount
- **Marked price (M.P.)** or list price: the price printed on the article.
- **Discount**: a reduction offered on the marked price. Discount is always calculated **on the marked price**.
- S.P. $=$ M.P. $-$ discount $=$ M.P. $\times \dfrac{100 - d}{100}$, where $d$ is the discount per cent.
$$\text{Discount}\% = \frac{\text{Discount}}{\text{M.P.}} \times 100$$
?eg A shirt marked ₹800 is sold at a discount of 15%. Find the selling price.
= $800 \times \dfrac{85}{100}$
=> ₹680
?eg After a discount of 20%, a bag is sold for ₹960. Find its marked price.
= $960 \times \dfrac{100}{80}$
=> ₹1200
?eg An article marked ₹1250 is sold for ₹1100. Find the discount per cent.
= $\dfrac{150}{1250} \times 100$
=> 12%

# Marked price, discount and profit together
- First get the S.P. from the M.P. and the discount; then compare the S.P. with the C.P.
- To fix the marked price: find the required S.P. from the C.P. and profit, then divide by $\dfrac{100 - d}{100}$.
?eg A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹600 and marks it 30% above the cost price. He allows a discount of 10%. Find his profit per cent.
= M.P. $= 780$. S.P. $= 780 \times 0.9 = 702$. Profit $= 102$.
=> 17%
?eg How much above the cost price must a trader mark his goods so that after allowing a discount of 20% he still gains 12%?
= Let C.P. $= 100$. Required S.P. $= 112$. M.P. $= 112 \times \dfrac{100}{80} = 140$.
=> 40% above the cost price

# Successive discounts
- Two discounts of $d_1\%$ and $d_2\%$ one after the other: S.P. $=$ M.P. $\times \dfrac{100 - d_1}{100} \times \dfrac{100 - d_2}{100}$.
- They are equivalent to a single discount of $\left(d_1 + d_2 - \dfrac{d_1d_2}{100}\right)\%$, which is less than $d_1 + d_2$.
?eg Find the single discount equivalent to successive discounts of 20% and 10%.
= $20 + 10 - \dfrac{200}{100}$
=> 28%
?eg A washing machine marked ₹15,000 is sold with successive discounts of 10% and 5%. Find the selling price.
= $15000 \times 0.9 \times 0.95$
=> ₹12,825

# Tax on sales
- A tax such as GST is charged as a percentage of the **selling price** and added to the bill.
- Amount paid $=$ S.P. $\times \left(1 + \dfrac{\text{tax}\%}{100}\right)$.
- If the price **includes** tax, the price before tax $=$ amount paid $\div \left(1 + \dfrac{\text{tax}\%}{100}\right)$.
?eg A pair of shoes is priced at ₹1500 and the tax is 12%. Find the amount to be paid.
= $1500 \times 1.12$
=> ₹1680
?eg A fan is bought for ₹2360 including tax at 18%. Find its price before tax.
= $2360 \div 1.18$
=> ₹2000

# Common mistakes
- Calculating profit% or loss% on the selling price.
- Calculating discount on the cost price instead of the marked price.
- Leaving out the overheads from the cost price.
- Adding successive discounts: 20% and 10% make 28%, not 30%.
- Using $100 + l$ for a loss or $100 - p$ for a profit.
- Finding the C.P. by taking the percentage of the S.P.
