# Mean and median
- **Mean** (average) $= \dfrac{\text{sum of all the values}}{\text{number of values}}$.
- **Median**: the middle value when the data are arranged in order. With an even number of values, it is the mean of the two middle values.
- Sum of the values $=$ mean $\times$ number of values. This is used again and again.
?eg Find the mean and median of 8, 3, 10, 13, 4, 6, 7, 7, 8, 8, 5.
= Sum $= 79$ and there are 11 values, so the mean is $\dfrac{79}{11} \approx 7.18$.
= In order: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 10, 13. The 6th value is 7.
=> Mean about 7.18; median 7

# The mean as a balance point
- On a dot plot, the **total distance** of the values below the mean from the mean equals the total distance of the values above it. The mean is the point where the data balance.
- The mean is not always midway between the smallest and largest values.
- There is only one such balance point.
!fig w=330 h=135; Q a 30 85; Q b 310 85; L a b; P p1 60 70 -; P p2 60 55 -; P p3 90 70 -; P p4 270 70 -; Q m1 120 60; Q m2 120 95; H m1 m2; T 60 105 10; T 90 105 11; T 120 122 mean 12; T 270 105 17; cap Data 10, 10, 11, 17: distances on the left 2 + 2 + 1 = 5, on the right 5
?eg Check the balance property for the data 4, 6, 7, 11, 12.
= Mean $= \dfrac{40}{5} = 8$.
= Below the mean: $4 + 2 + 1 = 7$. Above the mean: $3 + 4 = 7$.
=> The distances balance

# Adding or removing values
- Including a value **greater than the mean** increases the mean. Including a value **less than the mean** decreases it. Including a value **equal to the mean** leaves it unchanged.
- Removing a value has the opposite effect: removing a value above the mean lowers the mean.
- Several values can be added without changing the mean if their distances above and below the mean balance, for example $m - 3$ and $m + 3$, or $m - 1$, $m - 2$ and $m + 3$.
- The **median** behaves in a similar way: including a value greater than the median cannot lower it, and including a value smaller than the median cannot raise it. The median may also stay the same.
?eg The mean of 9 numbers is 20. A tenth number, 40, is included. Find the new mean.
= New sum $= 180 + 40 = 220$
=> 22
?eg The data 3, 5, 8, 8, 9, 12, 14 have median 8. What is the median after 11 is included?
= In order: 3, 5, 8, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14. The two middle values are 8 and 9.
=> 8.5

# Changing every value
- If the same number $k$ is **added** to every value, the mean increases by $k$. If $k$ is subtracted, the mean decreases by $k$.
- If every value is **multiplied** by $k$, the mean is multiplied by $k$.
- Reason: $\dfrac{(x_1 + k) + (x_2 + k) + \ldots + (x_n + k)}{n} = \dfrac{x_1 + x_2 + \ldots + x_n}{n} + \dfrac{nk}{n} = a + k$.
- The median changes in exactly the same way.
?eg The mean height of 24 students, measured with shoes on, is 150.2 cm. The shoes add 1 cm. Find the correct mean height.
= Every value is 1 cm too large, so the mean is 1 cm too large.
=> 149.2 cm
?eg The mean of a data set is 12. Every value is doubled and then 5 is added. Find the new mean.
= $2 \times 12 + 5$
=> 29

# Finding an unknown value
- Use: sum $=$ mean $\times$ number of values.
?eg The mean weight of 10 players is 39.2 kg. Nine of the weights add up to 349 kg. Find the tenth.
= Total $= 39.2 \times 10 = 392$
=> 43 kg
?eg The mean of 15 values is 25.6. One value was recorded as 3 more than it should be. Find the correct mean.
= Recorded sum $= 25.6 \times 15 = 384$. Correct sum $= 381$.
=> $\dfrac{381}{15} = 25.4$
?eg The mean of 8, 13, 10, 4, 5, 20, $y$, 10 is 10.375. Find $y$.
= Sum $= 10.375 \times 8 = 83$, and the known values add up to 70.
=> $y = 13$

# Mean and median with frequencies
- When each value occurs several times, multiply each value by its **frequency** before adding.
$$\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{sum of (value} \times \text{frequency)}}{\text{sum of the frequencies}}$$
- Do **not** just average the different values.
- **Median**: add the frequencies one after another (running totals) until you reach the middle position(s).
| Family size | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | Total |
| Number of students | 4 | 10 | 8 | 5 | 3 | 30 |
| Size $\times$ number | 12 | 40 | 40 | 30 | 21 | 143 |
?eg Find the mean and median family size from the table above.
= Mean $= \dfrac{143}{30} \approx 4.77$.
= There are 30 values, so the median is the mean of the 15th and 16th values. Running totals: 4, 14, 22. Positions 15 to 22 all hold the value 5.
=> Mean about 4.77; median 5

# Spreadsheets
- A **spreadsheet** is a table of **cells** on a computer. Columns are labelled A, B, C, ... and rows 1, 2, 3, ... The cell in column E and row 5 is **E5**.
- A cell can hold text, a number or a **formula**. A formula begins with $=$.
- A row or column of cells is written Start:End. B3:G3 means the cells from B3 to G3; D2:D6 means the cells from D2 to D6.
| Formula | What it gives |
| =SUM(B3:G3) | The total of cells B3 to G3 |
| =AVERAGE(B2:B23) | The mean of cells B2 to B23 |
| =MEDIAN(B2:B23) | The median of cells B2 to B23 |
- When a number in the table is changed, every formula that uses it updates automatically.

# Line graphs
- A **line graph** joins data points by line segments. It is best for showing how a quantity **changes over time**.
- The horizontal axis usually shows time; the vertical axis shows the quantity. Always read the titles, the scale and the key first.
- **Reading a graph in two steps**: (1) identify what is given: axes, scale, units, which line is which; (2) infer and interpret: highest and lowest points, rises and falls, comparisons.
- A **steeper** segment means a faster change. A flat segment means no change.
- Two or more lines on the same axes let us compare trends. With many time points, a line graph is much clearer than a clustered bar graph.
- Ask how the data were collected. This shows what the graph can and cannot tell us.
| Month | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun |
| Maximum temperature ($^\circ$C) | 20 | 24 | 30 | 36 | 34 | 28 |

?eg From the graph above: (i) in which month is the temperature highest? (ii) Between which two months is the rise greatest? (iii) What is the mean for the six months?
= (i) The highest point is April, at 36.
= (ii) The steepest rising segments are Feb to Mar and Mar to Apr, each a rise of 6.
= (iii) $\dfrac{20 + 24 + 30 + 36 + 34 + 28}{6} = \dfrac{172}{6}$
=> April; Feb to Mar and Mar to Apr; about 28.7$^\circ$C
?eg A line graph shows a shop's visitors over a week: 16, 19, 10, 14, 20, 22, 35 from Monday to Sunday. Describe the trend.
= The lowest point is Wednesday (10). From Wednesday the line rises every day.
=> Visitors dip mid-week and climb to a peak of 35 on Sunday; the steepest rise is from Saturday to Sunday.

# Other ways of showing data
- **Infographics** combine pictures, maps, colours and numbers to tell a story quickly. Read the colour scale or key before drawing conclusions.
- An **activity strip** shows how a day is spent: a strip of 48 boxes, one for each half hour, coloured by activity.
- A graph can support only what its data show. "No power cuts" cannot be concluded from a graph of how many homes use electricity for lighting.

# Common mistakes
- Averaging the distinct values and ignoring their frequencies.
- Finding the median without arranging the data in order.
- Assuming the mean is midway between the smallest and largest values.
- Thinking that adding any new value raises the mean. It depends on whether the value is above or below the mean.
- Reading a line graph without checking the scale on the vertical axis.
- Concluding things about individuals from an average.

@@formulas
Mean | $\dfrac{\text{sum of the values}}{\text{number of values}}$
Sum from the mean | Mean $\times$ number of values
Median, odd number $n$ of values | The $\dfrac{n + 1}{2}$th value in order
Median, even number $n$ of values | Mean of the $\dfrac{n}{2}$th and $\left(\dfrac{n}{2} + 1\right)$th values
Balance property | Total distance below the mean $=$ total distance above
Add $k$ to every value | Mean and median increase by $k$
Multiply every value by $k$ | Mean and median are multiplied by $k$
Mean with frequencies | $\dfrac{\text{sum of (value} \times \text{frequency)}}{\text{sum of frequencies}}$
New mean after including $v$ | $\dfrac{an + v}{n + 1}$
Spreadsheet total | =SUM(Start:End)
Spreadsheet mean | =AVERAGE(Start:End)
Line graph | Shows change over time; steeper means faster change
