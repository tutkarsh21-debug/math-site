# Data
- **Data** are numerical facts collected for a purpose. Data as first collected are **raw data**.
- Each entry is an **observation**. The number of times an observation occurs is its **frequency**.
- **Range** $=$ highest observation $-$ lowest observation.
- **Tally marks** make counting easy: four upright strokes and a fifth across them make a bundle of five.
- A **frequency distribution table** lists the observations with their frequencies.
?eg The marks of 20 students are: 5, 7, 6, 8, 5, 9, 7, 6, 7, 8, 5, 7, 9, 6, 7, 8, 10, 7, 6, 8. Make a frequency table.
= Count each mark: 5 occurs 3 times, 6 four times, 7 six times, 8 four times, 9 twice, 10 once.
=> Frequencies 3, 4, 6, 4, 2, 1 (total 20)
| Marks | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
| Frequency | 3 | 4 | 6 | 4 | 2 | 1 |

# Grouped data
- When there are many different observations, they are put into groups called **class intervals**.
- **Class limits**: in the class 10–20, the lower limit is 10 and the upper limit is 20.
- **Exclusive form**: 0–10, 10–20, 20–30. The upper limit is **not** included, so 10 goes in 10–20.
- **Inclusive form**: 1–10, 11–20, 21–30. Both limits are included.
- **Class size (width)** $=$ upper limit $-$ lower limit (in exclusive form).
- **Class mark** $= \dfrac{\text{upper limit} + \text{lower limit}}{2}$.
?eg For the class 40–50, find the class size and the class mark.
=> 10 and 45
?eg The weights (in kg) of 15 students are: 32, 41, 38, 45, 36, 49, 43, 37, 40, 52, 34, 47, 39, 44, 50. Group them into classes 30–35, 35–40, 40–45, 45–50, 50–55.
= 30–35: 32, 34. 35–40: 38, 36, 37, 39. 40–45: 41, 43, 40, 44. 45–50: 45, 49, 47. 50–55: 52, 50.
=> Frequencies 2, 4, 4, 3, 2

# Bar graph
- A **bar graph** shows ungrouped data with bars of **equal width** and **equal gaps** between them. The height of a bar shows the value.
- Choose a suitable scale, label both axes and give the graph a title.
- A **double bar graph** shows two sets of data side by side for comparison.

# Histogram
- A **histogram** shows **grouped continuous data**. Class intervals are on the horizontal axis and frequencies on the vertical axis.
- The bars have **no gaps**, because the classes are continuous.
- With equal class sizes, the heights of the bars are proportional to the frequencies.
- If the first class does not start at 0, a **kink** (zig-zag) is drawn on the horizontal axis.

| Number of students | 4 | 8 | 12 | 10 | 6 |

# Pie chart
- A **pie chart** (circle graph) shows how a whole is divided into parts. Each part is a **sector**.
- The angle of each sector is proportional to the value it represents:
$$\text{Central angle} = \frac{\text{value of the component}}{\text{total value}} \times 360^\circ$$
- The central angles add up to $360^\circ$.
- To read a pie chart: value $= \dfrac{\text{central angle}}{360^\circ} \times$ total.
| Item | Food | Rent | Education | Savings | Others | Total |
| Amount (₹) | 6000 | 4000 | 3000 | 2000 | 3000 | 18000 |
| Central angle | $120^\circ$ | $80^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $40^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $360^\circ$ |
!fig w=320 h=205; Q O 160 100; Q p0 230 100; Q p1 125 39.4; Q p2 94.2 124; Q p3 147.8 168.9; Q p4 195 160.6; C O 70; L O p0; L O p1; L O p2; L O p3; L O p4; T 185 68 Food; T 118 88 Rent; T 124 136 Edu.; T 171 150 Sav.; T 198 128 Others; cap Pie chart of a family's monthly spending
?eg Find the central angles for the family budget in the table.
= Each ₹18,000 corresponds to $360^\circ$, so ₹1000 corresponds to $20^\circ$.
=> $120^\circ$, $80^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $40^\circ$, $60^\circ$
?eg In a pie chart of 720 students, the sector for "cricket" has an angle of $150^\circ$. How many students chose cricket?
= $\dfrac{150}{360} \times 720$
=> 300
?eg A sector of $72^\circ$ stands for ₹2400. Find the total amount shown by the pie chart.
= $72^\circ$ is $\dfrac{1}{5}$ of the circle.
=> ₹12,000
?eg In a pie chart, 35% of the students walk to school. Find the angle of that sector.
= $0.35 \times 360^\circ$
=> $126^\circ$

# Choosing a graph
| Data | Suitable graph |
| Separate items to compare | Bar graph |
| Two related sets to compare | Double bar graph |
| Grouped continuous data | Histogram |
| Parts of a whole | Pie chart |

# Common mistakes
- Putting an observation equal to an upper limit in that class (exclusive form). It belongs to the next class.
- Leaving gaps between the bars of a histogram, or drawing a bar graph without gaps.
- Central angles that do not add up to $360^\circ$.
- Using percentages as angles: 25% is $90^\circ$, not $25^\circ$.
- Forgetting to check that the frequencies add up to the number of observations.
- Not stating the scale.

@@formulas
Range | Highest value $-$ lowest value
Frequency | Number of times an observation occurs
Class size | Upper limit $-$ lower limit
Class mark | $\dfrac{\text{upper limit} + \text{lower limit}}{2}$
Exclusive class $a$–$b$ | Includes $a$, excludes $b$
Central angle | $\dfrac{\text{component}}{\text{total}} \times 360^\circ$
Value from an angle | $\dfrac{\text{angle}}{360^\circ} \times$ total
Percentage to angle | Percentage $\times\ 3.6^\circ$
Sum of central angles | $360^\circ$
Histogram | Bars without gaps, for continuous classes
Bar graph | Bars of equal width with equal gaps
Total frequency | Number of observations
