# Direct variation
- Two quantities are in **direct variation** if an increase (or decrease) in one causes a **proportional** increase (or decrease) in the other.
- If $x$ and $y$ vary directly, the ratio $\dfrac{x}{y}$ is **constant**: $\dfrac{x_1}{y_1} = \dfrac{x_2}{y_2}$.
- Examples: number of articles and their cost; distance and time at a fixed speed; work done and wages.
| Number of pens ($x$) | 2 | 5 | 8 | 12 |
| Cost in ₹ ($y$) | 30 | 75 | 120 | 180 |
- In the table $\dfrac{x}{y} = \dfrac{1}{15}$ throughout.
?eg If 15 notebooks cost ₹675, find the cost of 22 notebooks.
= $\dfrac{15}{675} = \dfrac{22}{y}$, so $y = \dfrac{675 \times 22}{15}$
=> ₹990
?eg A car uses 12 litres of petrol for 180 km. How far can it go on 20 litres?
= $\dfrac{12}{180} = \dfrac{20}{d}$
=> 300 km

# Inverse variation
- Two quantities are in **inverse variation** if an increase in one causes a **proportional decrease** in the other.
- If $x$ and $y$ vary inversely, the product $xy$ is **constant**: $x_1y_1 = x_2y_2$.
- Examples: number of workers and days for a job; speed and time for a fixed distance; number of animals and days a stock of food lasts.
| Speed in km/h ($x$) | 20 | 30 | 40 | 60 |
| Time in hours ($y$) | 6 | 4 | 3 | 2 |
- In the table $xy = 120$ throughout.
?eg 12 workers can build a wall in 15 days. How many days will 20 workers take?
= $12 \times 15 = 20 \times d$
=> 9 days
?eg A car takes 4 hours at 60 km/h. How long will it take at 80 km/h?
= $60 \times 4 = 80 \times t$
=> 3 hours
?eg A hostel has food for 120 students for 30 days. If 30 more students join, how long will the food last?
= $120 \times 30 = 150 \times d$
=> 24 days

# Unitary method
- Find the value for **one unit** first, then for the required number of units.
- In direct variation, divide to get one unit and then multiply. In inverse variation, multiply to get one unit and then divide.
?eg 8 men earn ₹4800 in a day. How much do 11 men earn?
= One man earns ₹600.
=> ₹6600
?eg 6 pipes fill a tank in 40 minutes. How long do 8 pipes take?
= One pipe takes $6 \times 40 = 240$ minutes.
=> 30 minutes

# Time and work
- If a person does a piece of work in $n$ days, the work done in **one day** is $\dfrac{1}{n}$.
- If the work done in one day is $\dfrac{1}{n}$, the whole work takes $n$ days.
- If A takes $a$ days and B takes $b$ days, together they do $\dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b}$ of the work in a day and finish in $\dfrac{ab}{a + b}$ days.
- Wages are shared in the ratio of the **work done**.
?eg A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B in 18 days. How long do they take together?
= One day's work $= \dfrac{1}{12} + \dfrac{1}{18} = \dfrac{5}{36}$
=> $\dfrac{36}{5} = 7\tfrac{1}{5}$ days
?eg A and B together finish a job in 6 days. A alone takes 10 days. How long does B alone take?
= B's one day's work $= \dfrac{1}{6} - \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{1}{15}$
=> 15 days
?eg A can do a job in 20 days and B in 30 days. They work together for 6 days and then A leaves. How long does B take to finish the rest?
= Work done in 6 days $= 6 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{20} + \dfrac{1}{30}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Remaining work $= \dfrac{1}{2}$.
=> $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 30 = 15$ days
?eg A, B and C can do a job in 10, 12 and 15 days. How long do all three take together?
= $\dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{1}{12} + \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{6 + 5 + 4}{60} = \dfrac{1}{4}$
=> 4 days
?eg A and B do a job for ₹900. A alone can do it in 12 days and B alone in 6 days. How should the money be shared?
= One day's work is in the ratio $\dfrac{1}{12} : \dfrac{1}{6} = 1 : 2$.
=> A ₹300, B ₹600

# Pipes and cisterns
- A pipe that fills a tank in $n$ hours fills $\dfrac{1}{n}$ of it in one hour. This is treated as positive work.
- An outlet that empties a tank in $m$ hours empties $\dfrac{1}{m}$ in one hour. This is treated as **negative** work.
?eg Two taps fill a tank in 10 hours and 15 hours. How long do they take together?
= $\dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{1}{6}$
=> 6 hours
?eg A tap fills a tank in 8 hours and a leak empties it in 12 hours. With both working, how long does the tank take to fill?
= $\dfrac{1}{8} - \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{1}{24}$
=> 24 hours

# More than two quantities
- When three quantities are involved (men, hours a day, days), decide for each one whether it varies directly or inversely with the unknown.
- For the same work: men $\times$ days $\times$ hours a day is constant. In general $\dfrac{M_1D_1H_1}{W_1} = \dfrac{M_2D_2H_2}{W_2}$.
?eg 15 men working 8 hours a day finish a job in 12 days. How many days do 20 men take working 6 hours a day?
= $15 \times 8 \times 12 = 20 \times 6 \times d$
=> 12 days
?eg 10 men dig a trench 50 m long in 4 days. How many men are needed to dig 150 m in 6 days?
= $\dfrac{10 \times 4}{50} = \dfrac{m \times 6}{150}$
=> 20 men

# Common mistakes
- Using a direct proportion for men and days. More men need fewer days.
- Adding the times of two workers. Add their one-day work instead.
- Forgetting to subtract the work of a leak or outlet.
- Sharing wages in the ratio of the days taken. Use the ratio of work done.
- Not checking units: hours with hours, days with days.

@@formulas
Direct variation | $\dfrac{x_1}{y_1} = \dfrac{x_2}{y_2}$
Inverse variation | $x_1y_1 = x_2y_2$
One day's work | $\dfrac{1}{\text{number of days for the whole work}}$
Two workers together | $\dfrac{ab}{a + b}$ days
Three workers together | $1 \div \left(\dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b} + \dfrac{1}{c}\right)$ days
B alone | $\dfrac{1}{b} = \dfrac{1}{t} - \dfrac{1}{a}$
Remaining work | $1 -$ work done
Wages | Shared in the ratio of work done
Filling and emptying | Net rate $= \dfrac{1}{n} - \dfrac{1}{m}$
Men, days and hours | $\dfrac{M_1D_1H_1}{W_1} = \dfrac{M_2D_2H_2}{W_2}$
Speed and time | Speed $\times$ time $=$ distance (constant)
Unitary method | Find the value for one unit first
