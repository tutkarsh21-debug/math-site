# Per cent
- **Per cent** means "per hundred" or "out of a hundred". The symbol is %.
- $x\% = \dfrac{x}{100}$. For example $35\% = \dfrac{35}{100} = \dfrac{7}{20} = 0.35$.
| To convert | Do this | Example |
| Per cent to fraction | Divide by 100 and simplify | $45\% = \dfrac{9}{20}$ |
| Fraction to per cent | Multiply by 100 | $\dfrac{3}{8} = 37.5\%$ |
| Per cent to decimal | Move the decimal point two places left | $6.5\% = 0.065$ |
| Decimal to per cent | Move the decimal point two places right | $0.48 = 48\%$ |
| Per cent to ratio | Write over 100 and simplify | $25\% = 1 : 4$ |
| Ratio to per cent | Write as a fraction and multiply by 100 | $3 : 5 = 60\%$ |
- Useful values: $50\% = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $25\% = \dfrac{1}{4}$, $20\% = \dfrac{1}{5}$, $12\tfrac{1}{2}\% = \dfrac{1}{8}$, $33\tfrac{1}{3}\% = \dfrac{1}{3}$, $66\tfrac{2}{3}\% = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
?eg Express $\dfrac{7}{25}$, 0.085 and $2 : 5$ as percentages.
=> 28%, 8.5% and 40%
?eg Express $16\tfrac{2}{3}\%$ as a fraction.
= $\dfrac{50}{3} \times \dfrac{1}{100}$
=> $\dfrac{1}{6}$

# Percentage of a quantity
- $x\%$ of a quantity $= \dfrac{x}{100} \times$ the quantity.
- To express one quantity as a percentage of another (same units): $\dfrac{\text{first}}{\text{second}} \times 100\%$.
- To find the whole when a percentage of it is known: whole $=$ part $\times \dfrac{100}{\text{percentage}}$.
?eg Find 15% of ₹840 and $12\tfrac{1}{2}\%$ of 64 kg.
= $\dfrac{15}{100} \times 840$ and $\dfrac{1}{8} \times 64$
=> ₹126 and 8 kg
?eg What per cent of 2 hours is 45 minutes?
= $\dfrac{45}{120} \times 100$
=> 37.5%
?eg 30% of a number is 72. Find the number.
= $72 \times \dfrac{100}{30}$
=> 240
?eg A number exceeds 20% of itself by 40. Find the number.
= $x - 0.2x = 40$, so $0.8x = 40$
=> 50

# Increase and decrease
$$\text{Percentage change} = \frac{\text{change}}{\text{original value}} \times 100\%$$
- After an **increase** of $x\%$, new value $=$ original $\times \left(1 + \dfrac{x}{100}\right)$.
- After a **decrease** of $x\%$, new value $=$ original $\times \left(1 - \dfrac{x}{100}\right)$.
- To find the **original** value: divide the new value by the same multiplier.
?eg The price of sugar rises from ₹40 to ₹46 per kg. Find the percentage increase.
= $\dfrac{6}{40} \times 100$
=> 15%
?eg A salary of ₹24,000 is increased by 12.5%. Find the new salary.
= $24000 \times \dfrac{9}{8}$
=> ₹27,000
?eg After a 20% decrease, the price of a book is ₹160. Find the original price.
= $160 \div 0.8$
=> ₹200
?eg A number is increased by 20% and the result is then decreased by 20%. Find the net change.
= $1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$
=> A decrease of 4%

# Comparisons
- If A is $x\%$ **more** than B, then B is $\dfrac{x}{100 + x} \times 100\%$ **less** than A.
- If A is $x\%$ **less** than B, then B is $\dfrac{x}{100 - x} \times 100\%$ **more** than A.
- If a price rises by $x\%$, consumption must fall by $\dfrac{x}{100 + x} \times 100\%$ to keep the expenditure unchanged.
?eg A's income is 25% more than B's. By what per cent is B's income less than A's?
= $\dfrac{25}{125} \times 100$
=> 20%
?eg The price of rice rises by 25%. By what per cent must a family reduce its consumption so that its expenditure on rice does not change?
=> 20%

# Word problems
?eg In an examination a student scored 432 marks out of 600. Find the percentage. If the pass mark is 40%, how many marks are needed to pass?
= $\dfrac{432}{600} \times 100 = 72$ and $0.4 \times 600 = 240$
=> 72%; 240 marks
?eg A candidate scores 35% and fails by 30 marks. Another scores 42% and gets 12 marks more than the pass mark. Find the maximum marks and the pass mark.
= $0.35M + 30 = 0.42M - 12$, so $0.07M = 42$ and $M = 600$.
=> Maximum marks 600; pass mark 240
?eg In an election between two candidates, the winner got 58% of the votes and won by 2400 votes. Find the total number of votes.
= The loser got 42%. The difference is 16% of the votes: $0.16T = 2400$.
=> 15,000
?eg An alloy contains 36% zinc, 40% copper and the rest nickel. Find the mass of nickel in 500 g of the alloy.
= Nickel is 24%: $0.24 \times 500$
=> 120 g
?eg A man spends 75% of his income and saves ₹4500. Find his income.
= Savings are 25% of the income.
=> ₹18,000
?eg The population of a town increases by 10% every year. If it is 50,000 now, find it after 2 years.
= $50000 \times 1.1 \times 1.1$
=> 60,500

# Common mistakes
- Using the new value as the base for a percentage change. The base is the original value.
- Thinking a 20% rise followed by a 20% fall leaves the value unchanged.
- Comparing quantities in different units (minutes with hours) without converting.
- Finding the original value by subtracting the percentage from the new value.
- Writing $0.5\%$ as 0.5 instead of 0.005.
- Treating "A is 25% more than B" as "B is 25% less than A".

@@formulas
Per cent | $x\% = \dfrac{x}{100}$
Fraction to per cent | Multiply by 100
Percentage of a quantity | $\dfrac{x}{100} \times$ quantity
One quantity as a percentage of another | $\dfrac{\text{first}}{\text{second}} \times 100$
Whole from a part | Part $\times \dfrac{100}{\text{percentage}}$
Percentage change | $\dfrac{\text{change}}{\text{original}} \times 100$
After an increase of $x\%$ | Original $\times \left(1 + \dfrac{x}{100}\right)$
After a decrease of $x\%$ | Original $\times \left(1 - \dfrac{x}{100}\right)$
A is $x\%$ more than B | B is $\dfrac{100x}{100 + x}\%$ less than A
A is $x\%$ less than B | B is $\dfrac{100x}{100 - x}\%$ more than A
Increase then decrease by $x\%$ | Net decrease of $\dfrac{x^2}{100}\%$
Common values | $12\tfrac{1}{2}\% = \dfrac{1}{8}$, $33\tfrac{1}{3}\% = \dfrac{1}{3}$
