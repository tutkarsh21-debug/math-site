# Circles show groups
- In a **Venn diagram** each group is drawn as a circle. A thing **inside** the circle belongs to the group.
- Two circles that **overlap** have some members in common. Two circles that are **apart** have no member in common.
- A circle **inside** another circle means every member of the small group is also in the big group.
| What the words say | The circles |
| All A are B | circle A inside circle B |
| Some A are B | circles A and B overlap |
| No A is B | circles A and B are separate |
| A and B are both kinds of C | A and B separate, both inside C |
- The same thing can belong to more than one group, so look at the real world: some doctors are women, but not all.
!fig w=300 h=200; Q o 150 100; Q p 150 122; Q q 150 142; C o 90; C p 62; C q 36; T 150 26 Animals; T 150 80 Mammals; T 150 148 Dogs; cap Dogs are mammals, and mammals are animals
?eg Which diagram shows Pen, Pencil and Stationery?
= A pen is stationery, and a pencil is stationery. A pen is not a pencil.
= So there is one big circle for Stationery, with two separate small circles inside it.
=> Two separate circles inside a larger circle
?eg Which diagram shows Rose, Flower and Plant?
= Every rose is a flower, and every flower is a plant.
=> Three circles, each inside the next

# Counting with two groups
- For two groups $A$ and $B$, the number in at least one of them is $n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$.
- The overlap $A \cap B$ is counted twice when you add $n(A)$ and $n(B)$, so it is subtracted once.
- The number that belong to $A$ only is $n(A) - n(A \cap B)$.
- Begin by filling the **overlap**, then the other regions, then the outside.
?eg In a class of 40, 25 like tea, 20 like coffee and 5 like neither. How many like both?
= The number who like at least one is $40 - 5 = 35$.
= $35 = 25 + 20 - n(\text{both})$, so $n(\text{both}) = 45 - 35$.
=> 10
?eg Of 100 people, 60 read paper A, 50 read paper B and 20 read both. How many read exactly one paper?
= Only A: $60 - 20 = 40$. Only B: $50 - 20 = 30$.
=> 70

# Counting with three groups
- Three circles overlap in a pattern that gives seven regions. Start from the centre (all three) and work outwards.
!fig w=300 h=210; Q a 115 100; Q b 185 100; Q c 150 150; C a 62; C b 62; C c 62; T 90 78 A; T 210 78 B; T 150 190 C; cap Three overlapping groups: seven regions
?eg In a survey of 100 students, 40 like maths and 35 like science. 10 like both and 35 like neither. Check that these numbers fit, and find how many like only maths.
= At least one: $40 + 35 - 10 = 65$, and $100 - 35 = 65$, so the numbers fit.
= Only maths: $40 - 10$.
=> 30

# Statements and circles
- Draw the circles for the statements only. A conclusion is true only if it is true in **every** possible drawing.
?eg All A are B. All B are C. Does it follow that all A are C?
= The circle for A is inside the circle for B, and B is inside C, so A is inside C.
=> Yes
?eg All roses are flowers. Some flowers are red. Does it follow that some roses are red?
= The red flowers may all be other flowers, so the roses may not touch the red part.
=> No, it does not follow
!tip Draw the least overlap that the statements allow. A conclusion that is not true in that drawing does not follow.

@@formulas
At least one of two groups | $n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$
Only $A$ | $n(A) - n(A \cap B)$
Exactly one of two groups | $n(A) + n(B) - 2\,n(A \cap B)$
Neither | total $- n(A \cup B)$

Q: Which diagram shows Rose, Flower and Plant?
A: Three circles, each inside the next (every rose is a flower, every flower is a plant).

Q: Which diagram shows Pen, Pencil and Stationery?
A: Two separate small circles inside a larger circle.

Q: In a class of 50, 30 play cricket and 25 play football. 10 play both. How many play neither?
A: 5 ($30 + 25 - 10 = 45$ play at least one, and $50 - 45 = 5$).

Q: Of 60 students, 35 like maths, 28 like science and 13 like both. How many like only maths?
A: 22.

Q: $n(A) = 45$, $n(B) = 30$ and $n(A \cap B) = 12$. Find $n(A \cup B)$.
A: 63.

Q: Of 70 people who drink tea or coffee (everyone drinks at least one), 40 drink tea and 45 drink coffee. How many drink both?
A: 15 ($40 + 45 - 70$).

Q: All squares are rectangles. All rectangles are quadrilaterals. How are the three circles drawn?
A: The circle of squares is inside the circle of rectangles, which is inside the circle of quadrilaterals.

Q: All A are B. All B are C. Does it follow that all A are C?
A: Yes.

Q: In a Venn diagram of two circles, the left-only region has 14 members, the overlap has 6, the right-only region has 9, and 3 members are outside both circles. How many members are there in all?
A: 32.

Q: Of 100 people, 60 read paper A, 50 read paper B and 20 read both. How many read exactly one paper?
A: 70.
