# Solved HOTS questions
- Try each question for five minutes before reading the solution. The hidden first step is usually "turn the story into an HCF or an LCM".
?eg Three bells ring at intervals of 12, 15 and 20 minutes. They ring together at 8:00 am. When will they next ring together?
= They ring together again after a time that is a multiple of 12, 15 and 20 minutes, so after the LCM.
= $12 = 2^2 \times 3$, $15 = 3 \times 5$, $20 = 2^2 \times 5$, so $\text{LCM} = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5 = 60$ minutes.
=> At 9:00 am
?eg Show that $5 - 2\sqrt{3}$ is irrational.
= Suppose $5 - 2\sqrt{3} = r$ is rational.
= Then $\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{5 - r}{2}$, which is rational, because $r$ is rational.
= But $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational. This contradiction shows that the assumption was wrong.
=> $5 - 2\sqrt{3}$ is irrational
?eg Use Euclid's algorithm to find the HCF of 1260 and 7344.
= $7344 = 5 \times 1260 + 1044$.
= $1260 = 1 \times 1044 + 216$.
= $1044 = 4 \times 216 + 180$.
= $216 = 1 \times 180 + 36$.
= $180 = 5 \times 36 + 0$.
=> 36
?eg Find the smallest number which leaves remainder 5 when divided by 12, 15 and 18.
= The number minus 5 must be a multiple of 12, 15 and 18, so find their LCM.
= $12 = 2^2 \times 3$, $15 = 3 \times 5$, $18 = 2 \times 3^2$, so $\text{LCM} = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 180$.
= The smallest such number is $180 + 5$.
=> 185
?eg Without long division, say whether $\dfrac{129}{2^2 \times 5^7 \times 7^5}$ has a terminating decimal expansion.
= A fraction in lowest terms terminates only if its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5.
= The denominator contains $7^5$, and 129 $= 3 \times 43$ has no factor 7 to cancel it.
=> It does not terminate. It is a non-terminating repeating decimal.

Q: Three bells ring at intervals of 8, 12 and 18 minutes. After how many minutes do they ring together again?
A: 72 minutes.

Q: Is $3\sqrt{2} + 5$ rational or irrational?
A: Irrational (if it were rational, $\sqrt{2}$ would be rational).

Q: Find the smallest number which leaves remainder 3 when divided by 8, 12 and 20.
A: 123 ($\text{LCM} = 120$).

Q: Use Euclid's algorithm to find the HCF of 867 and 255.
A: 51.

Q: Write the decimal expansion of $\dfrac{13}{200}$ without long division by first making the denominator a power of 10.
A: 0.065.

Q: If $a = 2^3 \times 3^2$ and $b = 2^2 \times 3^3 \times 5$, find $\text{HCF}(a, b)$ and $\text{LCM}(a, b)$.
A: HCF = 36 and LCM = 1080.

Q: Find the largest number which divides 70 and 125 leaving remainders 5 and 8 respectively.
A: 13 (the HCF of 65 and 117).

Q: The product of two numbers is 2160 and their HCF is 12. Find their LCM.
A: 180.
