@title Simple and Compound Interest
@summary Interest on borrowed money, simple interest by the formula, compound interest by repeated growth, and the quick shortcut for the difference in two years.

# Simple interest
- **Interest** is the extra money paid for borrowing. The money borrowed is the **principal** $P$, the yearly **rate** is $R\%$ and the **time** is $T$ years.
- In **simple interest** the interest is always worked out on the original principal.
$$\text{SI} = \dfrac{P \times R \times T}{100} \qquad A = P + \text{SI}$$
- $A$ is the **amount**: what has to be paid back in all.
- If any three of $P$, $R$, $T$ and SI are known, the fourth can be found by rearranging the formula.
?eg Find the simple interest on Rs 5000 at 8% per year for 3 years.
= $\text{SI} = \dfrac{5000 \times 8 \times 3}{100}$.
=> Rs 1200
?eg A sum of Rs 2000 earns a simple interest of Rs 600 in 3 years. Find the rate of interest.
= $600 = \dfrac{2000 \times R \times 3}{100}$, so $R = \dfrac{600 \times 100}{6000}$.
=> 10% per year

# Compound interest
- In **compound interest** the interest of each year is added to the principal, and the next year's interest is worked out on the larger amount.
- After $n$ years: $A = P \left(1 + \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n$ and $\text{CI} = A - P$.
- If the interest is added **half-yearly**, use half the rate and double the number of periods: $A = P \left(1 + \dfrac{R/2}{100}\right)^{2n}$.
- For depreciation (the value falls each year) use $\left(1 - \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n$.
?eg Find the compound interest on Rs 10 000 at 10% per year for 2 years.
= $A = 10\,000 \times 1.1 \times 1.1 = 12\,100$.
= $\text{CI} = 12\,100 - 10\,000$.
=> Rs 2100
?eg Find the amount when Rs 8000 is invested for 1 year at 10% per year, interest added half-yearly.
= Rate per half-year $= 5\%$ and the number of half-years $= 2$.
= $A = 8000 \times 1.05 \times 1.05$.
=> Rs 8820
?eg A machine worth Rs 50 000 loses 10% of its value every year. Find its value after 2 years.
= $50\,000 \times 0.9 \times 0.9$.
=> Rs 40 500

# Comparing the two
- In the **first** year simple interest and compound interest are equal. After that, compound interest is larger.
- For **2 years**, $\text{CI} - \text{SI} = P \left(\dfrac{R}{100}\right)^2$.
?eg Find the difference between the compound interest and the simple interest on Rs 5000 at 10% per year for 2 years.
= $5000 \times \left(\dfrac{10}{100}\right)^2 = 5000 \times 0.01$.
=> Rs 50
!tip For compound interest over 2 or 3 years, multiply year by year. You can see each step, and mistakes with powers are avoided.

@@formulas
Simple interest | $\dfrac{P \times R \times T}{100}$
Amount (simple) | $P + \text{SI}$
Amount (compound, yearly) | $P \left(1 + \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n$
Compound interest | $A - P$
Half-yearly compounding | rate $\dfrac{R}{2}$, periods $2n$
Depreciation | $P \left(1 - \dfrac{R}{100}\right)^n$
Difference in 2 years | $\text{CI} - \text{SI} = P \left(\dfrac{R}{100}\right)^2$
