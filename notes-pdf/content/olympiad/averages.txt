@title Averages
@summary The mean of a set of numbers, how the average changes when numbers are added or removed, and the weighted average of two groups.

# The average
- The **average** (mean) of $n$ numbers is $\dfrac{\text{sum of the numbers}}{n}$.
- So $\text{sum} = \text{average} \times n$. This is the most useful form: from an average you can always recover the **sum**.
- The average always lies between the smallest and the largest number.
- For equally spaced numbers (like 3, 5, 7, 9, 11) the average is the **middle** number, or the mean of the first and last.
?eg Find the average of 12, 15, 18, 21 and 24.
= The numbers are equally spaced, so the average is the middle number. Check: $\dfrac{12 + 15 + 18 + 21 + 24}{5} = \dfrac{90}{5}$.
=> 18
?eg Find the average of the first 20 natural numbers.
= The sum is $\dfrac{20 \times 21}{2} = 210$. The average is $\dfrac{210}{20}$.
=> 10.5

# When a number is added, removed or replaced
- Work with **sums**. Find the old sum and the new sum, then take the difference.
?eg The average of 5 numbers is 20. A sixth number is added and the average becomes 22. Find the sixth number.
= Old sum $= 5 \times 20 = 100$. New sum $= 6 \times 22 = 132$.
= The sixth number is $132 - 100$.
=> 32
?eg The average age of 8 people is 40 years. A person aged 32 leaves and a new person joins, and the average becomes 41. Find the age of the new person.
= Old sum $= 320$. New sum $= 8 \times 41 = 328$. The sum has gone up by 8.
= New person $= 32 + 8$.
=> 40 years
?eg A student scored 70, 75, 80 and 85 in four tests. What must the student score in the fifth test to have an average of 80?
= Needed total $= 5 \times 80 = 400$. Present total $= 310$.
=> 90

# Average of two groups
- If group A has $a$ members with average $m_A$ and group B has $b$ members with average $m_B$, the combined average is
$$\dfrac{a\,m_A + b\,m_B}{a + b}$$
- This is a **weighted average**. It lies closer to the average of the **larger** group.
?eg A class of 30 students has an average mark of 60 and another class of 20 students has an average mark of 70. Find the average of all 50 students.
= Total marks $= 30 \times 60 + 20 \times 70 = 1800 + 1400 = 3200$.
= Average $= \dfrac{3200}{50}$.
=> 64
!tip Never average two averages directly when the groups are of different sizes. Convert each to a total first.

@@formulas
Average | $\dfrac{\text{sum}}{n}$
Sum | average $\times\ n$
Average of the first $n$ natural numbers | $\dfrac{n + 1}{2}$
Average of equally spaced numbers | the middle number
Two groups combined | $\dfrac{a\,m_A + b\,m_B}{a + b}$
Number added or removed | find the change in the sum

@@dpp
Q: Find the average of 12, 15, 18, 21 and 24.
A: 18.

Q: The average of 5 numbers is 20. A sixth number is added and the average becomes 22. Find the sixth number.
A: 32.

Q: A class of 30 students has average 60 marks and another class of 20 students has average 70. Find the average of all 50 students.
A: 64.

Q: Find the average of the first 20 natural numbers.
A: 10.5.

Q: The average age of 8 people is 40. A person aged 32 leaves and a new person joins. The average becomes 41. Find the age of the new person.
A: 40 years.

Q: A student scored 70, 75, 80 and 85 in four tests. What is needed in the fifth test for an average of 80?
A: 90.

Q: The average of six numbers is 15. The average of four of them is 12. Find the average of the other two.
A: 21 ($90 - 48 = 42$, and $42 \div 2 = 21$).

Q: Find the average of 7 consecutive numbers beginning with 10.
A: 13.

Q: The average age of 3 friends is 14 years. A fourth friend joins and the average becomes 15 years. Find the age of the fourth friend.
A: 18 years.

Q: The average temperature on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday was $30^\circ$C and on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday was $32^\circ$C. If Thursday was $33^\circ$C, find the temperature on Monday.
A: $27^\circ$C (the sums are 90 and 96, so Thursday $-$ Monday $= 6$).
