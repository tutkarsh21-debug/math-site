# Cost price, selling price and profit
- **Cost price (CP)** is what the seller pays. **Selling price (SP)** is what the buyer pays.
- If $\text{SP} > \text{CP}$ there is a **profit**: $\text{profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP}$.
- If $\text{SP} < \text{CP}$ there is a **loss**: $\text{loss} = \text{CP} - \text{SP}$.
- **Profit percent and loss percent are always calculated on the cost price.**
$$\text{profit}\% = \dfrac{\text{profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \qquad \text{loss}\% = \dfrac{\text{loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$$
- To find SP from CP: $\text{SP} = \text{CP} \times \left(1 + \dfrac{\text{profit}\%}{100}\right)$ for a profit, or $\text{CP} \times \left(1 - \dfrac{\text{loss}\%}{100}\right)$ for a loss.
?eg A shopkeeper buys a watch for Rs 250 and sells it for Rs 300. Find the profit percent.
= Profit $= 300 - 250 = 50$. Profit percent $= \dfrac{50}{250} \times 100$.
=> 20%
?eg A bicycle bought for Rs 400 is sold for Rs 340. Find the loss percent.
= Loss $= 400 - 340 = 60$. Loss percent $= \dfrac{60}{400} \times 100$.
=> 15%
?eg Find the selling price needed to gain 20% on an article that costs Rs 450.
= $\text{SP} = 450 \times 1.2$.
=> Rs 540

# Finding the cost price from the selling price
- If you know the SP and the profit or loss percent, divide: $\text{CP} = \dfrac{\text{SP}}{1 \pm \text{percent}/100}$.
?eg An article is sold at a 10% profit for Rs 1320. Find its cost price.
= $\text{CP} = \dfrac{1320}{1.1}$.
=> Rs 1200
?eg An article is sold at a 20% loss for Rs 720. Find its cost price.
= $\text{CP} = \dfrac{720}{0.8}$.
=> Rs 900

# Marked price and discount
- The **marked price (MP)** is the price written on the article. A **discount** is a reduction in the marked price.
- $\text{discount} = \text{MP} - \text{SP}$, and $\text{discount}\% = \dfrac{\text{discount}}{\text{MP}} \times 100$. **Discount is calculated on the marked price.**
- Two discounts of $a\%$ and $b\%$ given one after the other are equal to **one** discount of $\left(a + b - \dfrac{ab}{100}\right)\%$.
?eg A table marked at Rs 800 is sold at a discount of 15%. Find the selling price.
= Discount $= 0.15 \times 800 = 120$.
=> Rs 680
?eg Find the single discount equal to two successive discounts of 20% and 10%.
= Factors: $0.8 \times 0.9 = 0.72$. So the buyer pays $72\%$ of the marked price.
=> 28%
?eg An article costs Rs 500. The shopkeeper marks it 40% above the cost and gives a 10% discount. Find the profit percent.
= $\text{MP} = 500 \times 1.4 = 700$. $\text{SP} = 700 \times 0.9 = 630$.
= Profit $= 130$, and $\dfrac{130}{500} \times 100 = 26$.
=> 26%
!tip Profit and loss use the cost price as the base. Discount uses the marked price as the base. Mixing them up is the most common mistake.

@@formulas
Profit | $\text{SP} - \text{CP}$
Loss | $\text{CP} - \text{SP}$
Profit percent | $\dfrac{\text{profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$
Loss percent | $\dfrac{\text{loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$
SP when there is a profit of $p\%$ | $\text{CP} \left(1 + \dfrac{p}{100}\right)$
SP when there is a loss of $p\%$ | $\text{CP} \left(1 - \dfrac{p}{100}\right)$
Discount | $\text{MP} - \text{SP}$
Discount percent | $\dfrac{\text{discount}}{\text{MP}} \times 100$
Two discounts $a\%$ and $b\%$ | $a + b - \dfrac{ab}{100}$
