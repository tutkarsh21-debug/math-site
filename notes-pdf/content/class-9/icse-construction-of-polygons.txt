@summary Ruler-and-compass constructions of quadrilaterals, parallelograms, rectangles, rhombuses, squares, trapeziums and regular hexagons from given measurements.

# Before you construct
- Draw a **rough sketch** first and mark the given measurements on it. It shows which triangle can be drawn first.
- A quadrilateral needs **five** independent measurements. Special quadrilaterals need fewer, because their properties supply the rest.
| Figure | Measurements needed |
| Quadrilateral | 5 |
| Trapezium | 4 |
| Parallelogram | 3 |
| Rectangle | 2 |
| Rhombus | 2 |
| Square | 1 |
- Use only a ruler and compasses unless a protractor is allowed. Leave all construction arcs visible.
- Standard angles by compasses: $60^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $90^\circ$ (bisect a straight angle or use $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$), $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $75^\circ$, $105^\circ$, $135^\circ$.

# Quadrilaterals
| Given | Method |
| 4 sides and 1 diagonal | Draw the triangle on the diagonal with two of the sides; draw the second triangle on the other side of the diagonal |
| 3 sides and 2 diagonals | Draw the triangle formed by one side and parts fixed by the two diagonals from its ends; locate the fourth vertex by arcs |
| 4 sides and 1 angle | Draw the two sides containing the angle; cut arcs for the other two sides from their free ends |
| 3 sides and the 2 included angles | Draw the middle side; draw the angles at its ends; cut off the other two sides |
| 2 adjacent sides and 3 angles | Draw the sides with their included angle; draw the other two angles at the free ends; the rays meet at the fourth vertex |
?eg Construct quadrilateral ABCD with $AB = 4$ cm, $BC = 5$ cm, $CD = 4.5$ cm, $DA = 3.5$ cm and $AC = 6$ cm.
= Draw $AC = 6$ cm. With centres A and C and radii 4 cm and 5 cm, draw arcs meeting at B.
= On the other side of AC, with centres A and C and radii 3.5 cm and 4.5 cm, draw arcs meeting at D.
=> Join AB, BC, CD and DA
?eg Can a quadrilateral be constructed with $AB = 3$ cm, $BC = 4$ cm and $AC = 8$ cm as one of its diagonals?
= In triangle ABC, $AB + BC = 7$ cm, which is less than AC.
=> No; triangle ABC cannot be drawn

# Parallelograms
- Use: opposite sides are equal and parallel; the diagonals bisect each other.
| Given | Method |
| 2 adjacent sides and the included angle | Draw the sides with the angle; complete with arcs equal to the opposite sides |
| 2 adjacent sides and 1 diagonal | Draw the triangle with the two sides and the diagonal; complete with arcs |
| 2 diagonals and the angle between them | Draw one diagonal, mark its mid-point O; draw the other through O at the given angle, half on each side |
| 1 side and 2 diagonals | Draw the triangle with the side and the two half-diagonals; produce each half-diagonal an equal length beyond O |
| 2 adjacent sides and the height | Draw one side; draw a parallel at the given height; cut it with an arc equal to the other side |
?eg Construct parallelogram ABCD with $AB = 6$ cm, $AC = 8$ cm and $BD = 6$ cm.
= The diagonals bisect each other at O, so $OA = 4$ cm and $OB = 3$ cm.
= Draw $AB = 6$ cm. Draw triangle OAB with $OA = 4$ cm and $OB = 3$ cm.
= Produce AO to C with $OC = 4$ cm and BO to D with $OD = 3$ cm.
=> Join BC, CD and DA

# Rectangles
- Use: every angle is $90^\circ$; the diagonals are equal and bisect each other.
| Given | Method |
| Length and breadth | Draw the length; draw perpendiculars at both ends; cut off the breadth |
| One side and a diagonal | Draw the side AB; draw a perpendicular at B; cut it from A with the diagonal as radius |
| Diagonal and the angle between the diagonals | Draw one diagonal, mark O; draw the other through O at the given angle, equal and bisected |
?eg Construct rectangle ABCD with $AB = 5$ cm and diagonal $AC = 6.5$ cm.
= Draw $AB = 5$ cm. At B construct $\angle ABX = 90^\circ$.
= With centre A and radius 6.5 cm, cut BX at C.
=> With centres A and C and radii BC and AB, draw arcs meeting at D; join AD and CD

# Rhombuses and squares
- **Rhombus**: all sides equal; the diagonals bisect each other at $90^\circ$.
| Given | Method |
| Side and one angle | Draw the two sides with the angle; complete with arcs equal to the side |
| Side and one diagonal | Draw the diagonal; draw arcs equal to the side from both ends, on both sides |
| Two diagonals | Draw one diagonal; draw its perpendicular bisector; mark half of the other diagonal on each side |
- **Square**: one measurement is enough.
| Given | Method |
| Side | Draw the side; draw perpendiculars at both ends; cut off the side |
| Diagonal | Draw the diagonal; draw its perpendicular bisector; mark half the diagonal on each side of the mid-point |
?eg Construct a rhombus with diagonals 6 cm and 8 cm. What is its side?
= Draw $AC = 8$ cm and its perpendicular bisector, meeting AC at O. Mark $OB = OD = 3$ cm on the bisector.
= Join AB, BC, CD, DA. Side$^2 = 4^2 + 3^2$.
=> The side measures 5 cm
?eg Construct a square with diagonal 5 cm.
= Draw $AC = 5$ cm and its perpendicular bisector through O.
=> Mark $OB = OD = 2.5$ cm on it and join AB, BC, CD, DA

# Trapeziums
- A trapezium needs four measurements together with the fact that one pair of sides is parallel.
- **Method**: in trapezium ABCD with $AB \parallel DC$, draw CE parallel to DA to meet AB at E. Then AECD is a parallelogram, $AE = DC$, $CE = DA$ and $EB = AB - DC$. Construct triangle EBC first, then complete the parallelogram.
?eg Construct trapezium ABCD with $AB \parallel DC$, $AB = 7$ cm, $BC = 4$ cm, $CD = 4$ cm and $DA = 3.5$ cm.
= Draw $AB = 7$ cm and mark E on it with $AE = 4$ cm, so $EB = 3$ cm.
= Draw triangle EBC with $EC = 3.5$ cm and $BC = 4$ cm.
=> With centres A and C and radii 3.5 cm and 4 cm, draw arcs meeting at D; join AD and CD

# Regular hexagon
- Each interior angle of a regular hexagon is $120^\circ$. Its side equals the radius of its circumscribed circle.
- **Method 1 (using the circle)**: draw a circle with radius equal to the side. Starting from any point on it, step off the radius six times round the circle. Join the points in order.
- **Method 2 (using angles)**: draw the side AB. At A and B construct angles of $120^\circ$ and cut off the side on each arm. Repeat at the new vertices.
!fig w=260 h=215; Q O 130 105; C O 80; P A 210 105 r; P B 170 35.7 tr; P C 90 35.7 tl; P D 50 105 l; P E 90 174.3 bl; P F 170 174.3 br; L A B; L B C; L C D; L D E; L E F; L F A; D O A; D O B; cap The side of a regular hexagon equals the radius of its circumcircle
?eg Construct a regular hexagon of side 3.5 cm.
= Draw a circle of radius 3.5 cm. Mark a point A on it.
= With radius 3.5 cm, cut the circle at B from A, at C from B, and so on up to F.
=> Join AB, BC, CD, DE, EF and FA

# Common mistakes
- Starting without a rough sketch and then finding that the first triangle cannot be drawn.
- Using the full diagonal instead of half when the diagonals bisect each other.
- Rubbing out the construction arcs.
- Measuring an angle with a protractor when a compass construction is required.
- Not checking the triangle inequality for the first triangle.
- Drawing the second triangle on the same side of the diagonal as the first.

@@formulas
Quadrilateral | 5 measurements
Parallelogram | 3 measurements
Rectangle, rhombus | 2 measurements
Square | 1 measurement
Parallelogram | Opposite sides equal; diagonals bisect each other
Rectangle | Angles $90^\circ$; diagonals equal
Rhombus | All sides equal; diagonals perpendicular bisectors
Rhombus side from diagonals | $\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{d_1}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{d_2}{2}\right)^2}$
Square | Side $= \dfrac{\text{diagonal}}{\sqrt{2}}$
Trapezium | Draw CE parallel to DA; $EB = AB - DC$
Regular hexagon | Interior angle $120^\circ$; side $=$ circumradius
Regular polygon, interior angle | $\dfrac{(2n - 4) \times 90^\circ}{n}$

@@dpp
Q: How many measurements are needed to construct (i) a parallelogram (ii) a rhombus (iii) a square?
A: 3, 2 and 1
Q: Quadrilateral ABCD has $AB = 5$ cm, $BC = 4$ cm, $CD = 6$ cm, $DA = 4.5$ cm and $BD = 7$ cm. Which triangle is drawn first?
A: Triangle ABD (or BCD), on the diagonal BD
Q: A parallelogram has diagonals 10 cm and 6 cm meeting at $60^\circ$. What lengths are marked from the point of intersection?
A: 5 cm on each side along one diagonal and 3 cm on each side along the other
Q: A rectangle has a side 8 cm and a diagonal 10 cm. What is the other side?
A: 6 cm
Q: A rhombus has diagonals 10 cm and 24 cm. What will its side measure?
A: 13 cm
Q: A square has a diagonal 6 cm. What will its side measure, to one decimal place?
A: 4.2 cm
Q: In trapezium ABCD with $AB \parallel DC$, $AB = 8$ cm and $DC = 5$ cm. CE is drawn parallel to DA to meet AB at E. Find EB.
A: 3 cm
Q: What is the radius of the circle used to construct a regular hexagon of side 4 cm?
A: 4 cm
Q: Can a quadrilateral have sides 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm and 13 cm?
A: No; the longest side must be less than the sum of the other three, and $3 + 4 + 5 = 12 < 13$
Q: Which compass angles are combined to construct $105^\circ$?
A: $90^\circ$ and $15^\circ$ (or $60^\circ$ and $45^\circ$)
