@summary The mid-point theorem, its converse and the equal intercept theorem, with their use in triangles, trapeziums and other quadrilaterals.

# Mid-point theorem
- **Theorem**: the line segment joining the **mid-points of two sides** of a triangle is **parallel to the third side** and **equal to half of it**.
- If D and E are the mid-points of AB and AC, then $DE \parallel BC$ and $DE = \dfrac{1}{2}BC$.
!fig w=320 h=205; P A 150 22 t; P B 40 180 bl; P C 280 180 br; P D 95 101 l; P E 215 101 r; L A B; L A C; L B C; H D E; cap D and E are mid-points: DE is parallel to BC and DE = ½ BC
- **Proof idea**: produce DE to F so that $EF = DE$ and join CF. $\triangle ADE \cong \triangle CFE$ (S.A.S.), so $CF = AD = DB$ and $CF \parallel AB$. Then DBCF is a parallelogram, so $DF \parallel BC$ and $DF = BC$, giving $DE = \dfrac{1}{2}BC$.
?eg In triangle ABC, D and E are the mid-points of AB and AC. If $BC = 14$ cm and $\angle B = 65^\circ$, find DE and $\angle ADE$.
= $DE = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 14$. Since $DE \parallel BC$, $\angle ADE = \angle B$ (corresponding angles).
=> $DE = 7$ cm, $\angle ADE = 65^\circ$

# Converse of the mid-point theorem
- **Theorem**: the line drawn through the **mid-point of one side** of a triangle, **parallel to another side**, **bisects the third side**.
- If D is the mid-point of AB and $DE \parallel BC$ meets AC at E, then E is the mid-point of AC, and $DE = \dfrac{1}{2}BC$.
?eg In triangle ABC, D is the mid-point of AB and DE is parallel to BC, meeting AC at E. If $AC = 9$ cm and $BC = 10$ cm, find AE and DE.
= E is the mid-point of AC.
=> $AE = 4.5$ cm, $DE = 5$ cm

# The triangle of mid-points
- Let D, E, F be the mid-points of BC, CA, AB of triangle ABC.
- $EF \parallel BC$, $FD \parallel CA$, $DE \parallel AB$, and each is half the side it is parallel to.
- The four triangles AFE, FBD, EDC and DEF are **congruent**. Each has $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the area of triangle ABC.
- Perimeter of triangle DEF $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times$ perimeter of triangle ABC.
- BDEF, DCEF and AFDE are parallelograms.
?eg The sides of a triangle are 8 cm, 10 cm and 12 cm. Find the perimeter of the triangle formed by joining the mid-points of its sides.
= The sides are 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm.
=> 15 cm

# Quadrilaterals
- **The figure formed by joining the mid-points of the sides of any quadrilateral, in order, is a parallelogram.**
- Reason: if P, Q, R, S are the mid-points of AB, BC, CD, DA, then PQ and SR are both parallel to AC and equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}AC$.
| Original quadrilateral | Mid-point figure |
| Any quadrilateral | Parallelogram |
| Rectangle | Rhombus |
| Rhombus | Rectangle |
| Square | Square |
| Diagonals equal | Rhombus |
| Diagonals perpendicular | Rectangle |
?eg The diagonals of a quadrilateral are 10 cm and 16 cm. Find the perimeter of the figure formed by joining the mid-points of its sides.
= Two sides are $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 10 = 5$ cm and two are $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 16 = 8$ cm.
=> 26 cm

# Trapezium
- In trapezium ABCD with $AB \parallel DC$, let E and F be the mid-points of the non-parallel sides AD and BC.
- $EF \parallel AB \parallel DC$ and $EF = \dfrac{1}{2}(AB + DC)$.
- The segment joining the mid-points of the **diagonals** is parallel to the parallel sides and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}(AB - DC)$, where $AB > DC$.
!fig w=320 h=180; P A 40 150 bl; P B 290 150 br; P C 220 40 tr; P D 110 40 tl; P E 75 95 l; P F 255 95 r; L A B; L B C; L C D; L D A; H E F; cap EF joins the mid-points of AD and BC: EF = ½ (AB + DC)
?eg In trapezium ABCD, $AB \parallel DC$, $AB = 18$ cm and $DC = 10$ cm. E and F are the mid-points of AD and BC. Find EF.
= $\dfrac{1}{2}(18 + 10)$
=> 14 cm
?eg In the same trapezium, find the distance between the mid-points of the diagonals.
= $\dfrac{1}{2}(18 - 10)$
=> 4 cm

# Equal intercept theorem
- **Theorem**: if a transversal makes **equal intercepts** on three or more parallel lines, then **any other transversal** cutting them also makes equal intercepts.
- If $l \parallel m \parallel n$ and one transversal has $AB = BC$, another transversal has $PQ = QR$.
- This theorem is used to divide a line segment into any number of equal parts.
?eg Three parallel lines cut a transversal at A, B, C with $AB = BC = 3$ cm. They cut another transversal at P, Q, R with $PQ = 4$ cm. Find PR.
= $QR = PQ = 4$ cm
=> $PR = 8$ cm
?eg ABCD is a parallelogram. E is the mid-point of AB and F is the mid-point of CD. Prove that AF and CE trisect the diagonal BD.
= Let AF and CE meet BD at P and Q. AECF is a parallelogram ($AE = CF$ and $AE \parallel CF$), so $AF \parallel EC$.
= In $\triangle ABP$, E is the mid-point of AB and $EQ \parallel AP$, so Q is the mid-point of BP: $BQ = QP$. Similarly in $\triangle DQC$, $DP = PQ$.
=> $DP = PQ = QB$

# Common mistakes
- Using the mid-point theorem when only one of the points is a mid-point. Then the converse is needed, together with a parallel line.
- Writing $DE = BC$ instead of $DE = \dfrac{1}{2}BC$.
- In a trapezium, taking the mid-points of the parallel sides instead of the non-parallel sides.
- Forgetting to state both parts of the theorem (parallel **and** half).
- Assuming the mid-point figure of a quadrilateral is a rectangle or rhombus without checking the diagonals.
