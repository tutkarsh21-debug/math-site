@summary The angle properties of a triangle, the conditions under which two triangles are congruent (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS) and how to use congruent triangles to prove that sides or angles are equal.

# Angles of a triangle
- The sum of the three angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$.
- An **exterior angle** equals the sum of the two **interior opposite angles**, so it is greater than either of them.
- A triangle can have at most one right angle or one obtuse angle.
?eg In triangle ABC, $\angle A = 50^\circ$ and the exterior angle at C is $120^\circ$. Find $\angle B$ and $\angle C$.
= $\angle B = 120^\circ - 50^\circ = 70^\circ$ and $\angle C = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$
=> $\angle B = 70^\circ$, $\angle C = 60^\circ$

# Congruent triangles
- Two triangles are **congruent** if they have exactly the same shape and size. One can be placed on the other so that it covers it exactly.
- We write $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$. The **order of the letters** shows which vertices correspond: A to D, B to E, C to F.
- **C.P.C.T.**: corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal. So $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$, $CA = FD$, $\angle A = \angle D$, $\angle B = \angle E$, $\angle C = \angle F$.

# Conditions for congruency
| Condition | What must be equal |
| S.S.S. | Three sides of one triangle and the three sides of the other |
| S.A.S. | Two sides and the **included** angle |
| A.S.A. | Two angles and the **included** side |
| A.A.S. | Two angles and a corresponding side (not included) |
| R.H.S. | In right-angled triangles: the hypotenuse and one side |
- **A.A.A. is not a condition**: equal angles give the same shape but not the same size.
- **S.S.A. is not a condition**: two sides and an angle that is not included do not fix the triangle (except in the R.H.S. case).
?eg In triangles ABC and PQR, $AB = PQ$, $\angle B = \angle Q$ and $BC = QR$. Are they congruent? If $\angle A = 65^\circ$, find $\angle P$.
= Two sides and the included angle are equal: S.A.S.
=> $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$, and $\angle P = \angle A = 65^\circ$ (C.P.C.T.)
?eg In triangles ABC and DEF, $\angle A = \angle D$, $\angle B = \angle E$ and $AB = EF$. Can we say they are congruent?
= AB lies between $\angle A$ and $\angle B$, but EF does not lie between $\angle D$ and $\angle E$. The equal sides are not corresponding.
=> No

# Writing a proof
- State the two triangles.
- List three equal pairs, each with its **reason** (given, common side, vertically opposite angles, alternate angles, radii of the same circle, and so on).
- State the congruency with the correct condition and the vertices in corresponding order.
- Draw the conclusion by C.P.C.T.
?eg AB and CD bisect each other at O. Prove that $AC = BD$ and that AC is parallel to BD.
= In $\triangle AOC$ and $\triangle BOD$: $AO = BO$ (given), $CO = DO$ (given), $\angle AOC = \angle BOD$ (vertically opposite).
= So $\triangle AOC \cong \triangle BOD$ (S.A.S.), and $AC = BD$ (C.P.C.T.).
=> Also $\angle OAC = \angle OBD$ (C.P.C.T.). These are alternate angles, so $AC \parallel BD$.
!fig w=300 h=170; P A 50 40 tl; P B 250 130 br; P C 60 130 bl; P D 240 40 tr; P O 150 85 t; L A B; L C D; L A C; L B D; cap AB and CD bisect each other at O
?eg In quadrilateral ABCD, $AB = AD$ and $CB = CD$. Prove that AC bisects $\angle BAD$.
= In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ADC$: $AB = AD$, $CB = CD$, $AC = AC$ (common).
= $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle ADC$ (S.S.S.)
=> $\angle BAC = \angle DAC$ (C.P.C.T.), so AC bisects $\angle BAD$

# Results proved by congruency
- **Perpendicular bisector**: every point on the perpendicular bisector of a line segment is **equidistant from its two ends**. Conversely, a point equidistant from the ends lies on the perpendicular bisector.
- **Angle bisector**: every point on the bisector of an angle is **equidistant from the two arms**. Conversely, a point equidistant from the arms lies on the bisector.
- The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord (R.H.S.).
- A diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles (A.S.A.).
?eg P is a point on the bisector of $\angle ABC$. PM and PN are perpendiculars from P to BA and BC. Prove that $PM = PN$.
= In $\triangle PMB$ and $\triangle PNB$: $\angle PMB = \angle PNB = 90^\circ$, $\angle PBM = \angle PBN$ (BP bisects the angle), $BP = BP$ (common).
=> $\triangle PMB \cong \triangle PNB$ (A.A.S.), so $PM = PN$
?eg In right triangles ABC and DEF, $\angle B = \angle E = 90^\circ$, $AC = DF$ and $BC = EF$. If $AB = 5$ cm, find DE.
= Hypotenuse and one side are equal: R.H.S.
=> $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, so $DE = AB = 5$ cm

# Finding unknowns
- Once two triangles are known to be congruent, equate the corresponding parts to form equations.
?eg $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$. $AB = 3x + 1$, $PQ = 16$, $\angle C = 2y^\circ$ and $\angle R = 70^\circ$. Find $x$ and $y$.
= $3x + 1 = 16$ and $2y = 70$
=> $x = 5$, $y = 35$

# Common mistakes
- Writing the vertices in the wrong order, so that corresponding parts do not match.
- Using S.A.S. when the angle is not between the two sides.
- Claiming congruency from three equal angles.
- Using R.H.S. without a right angle, or with two sides that do not include the hypotenuse.
- Using C.P.C.T. before the triangles have been proved congruent.
- Leaving out the reason for a step.

Q: Two angles of a triangle are $48^\circ$ and $67^\circ$. Find the third angle.
A: $65^\circ$
Q: An exterior angle of a triangle is $110^\circ$ and one interior opposite angle is $45^\circ$. Find the other two angles of the triangle.
A: $65^\circ$ and $70^\circ$
Q: Which condition proves congruency: $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$, $\angle B = \angle E$?
A: S.A.S.
Q: Which condition proves congruency: $\angle A = \angle D$, $\angle C = \angle F$, $AC = DF$?
A: A.S.A.
Q: $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle RQP$. Which side equals AB and which angle equals $\angle C$?
A: $AB = RQ$ and $\angle C = \angle P$
Q: Can two triangles with angles $50^\circ, 60^\circ, 70^\circ$ each be said to be congruent?
A: Not necessarily; equal angles do not fix the size
Q: In triangle ABC, AD is perpendicular to BC and $BD = DC$. Prove that $AB = AC$.
A: $\triangle ADB \cong \triangle ADC$ (S.A.S.: $BD = DC$, $\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90^\circ$, AD common), so $AB = AC$
Q: $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, $BC = 2x - 3$, $EF = 11$, $\angle A = 55^\circ$, $\angle D = (y + 15)^\circ$. Find $x$ and $y$.
A: $x = 7$, $y = 40$
Q: In right triangles PQR and XYZ, $\angle Q = \angle Y = 90^\circ$, $PR = XZ$ and $PQ = XY$. Name the condition and the angle equal to $\angle R$.
A: R.H.S.; $\angle Z$
Q: A point P is 5 cm from one end of a segment AB and lies on its perpendicular bisector. How far is P from the other end?
A: 5 cm
