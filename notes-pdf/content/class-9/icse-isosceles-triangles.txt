# The isosceles triangle
- A triangle with **two equal sides** is **isosceles**. The third side is the **base**, the angles on the base are the **base angles**, and the angle between the equal sides is the **vertical angle**.
- **Theorem 1**: if two sides of a triangle are equal, the **angles opposite to them are equal**.
- **Theorem 2 (converse)**: if two angles of a triangle are equal, the **sides opposite to them are equal**.
!fig w=300 h=200; P A 150 25 t; P B 70 165 bl; P C 230 165 br; L A B; L A C; L B C; A A B C x; A A C B x; cap AB = AC, so the base angles B and C are equal
- **Proof of Theorem 1**: draw AD, the bisector of $\angle A$, meeting BC at D. In $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle ACD$: $AB = AC$, $\angle BAD = \angle CAD$, $AD = AD$. So they are congruent (S.A.S.) and $\angle B = \angle C$.
- If the vertical angle is $v$, each base angle is $\dfrac{180^\circ - v}{2}$. If each base angle is $b$, the vertical angle is $180^\circ - 2b$.
?eg In triangle ABC, $AB = AC$ and $\angle A = 70^\circ$. Find $\angle B$ and $\angle C$.
= $\angle B = \angle C = \dfrac{180^\circ - 70^\circ}{2}$
=> $55^\circ$ each
?eg In triangle PQR, $\angle P = 40^\circ$ and $\angle Q = 100^\circ$. Which sides are equal?
= $\angle R = 180^\circ - 140^\circ = 40^\circ = \angle P$. Sides opposite equal angles are equal.
=> $QR = PQ$

# Properties of an isosceles triangle
- In triangle ABC with $AB = AC$, the following four lines from A are **the same line**: the bisector of $\angle A$, the median to BC, the altitude to BC, and the perpendicular bisector of BC.
- So the altitude from the vertex **bisects the base** and **bisects the vertical angle**.
- The medians to the equal sides are equal; so are the altitudes to the equal sides and the bisectors of the base angles.
?eg In triangle ABC, $AB = AC = 13$ cm and $BC = 10$ cm. Find the altitude from A.
= The altitude AD bisects BC, so $BD = 5$ cm. $AD^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 144$.
=> 12 cm
?eg Prove that the altitudes to the equal sides of an isosceles triangle are equal.
= Let $AB = AC$, with BE perpendicular to AC and CF perpendicular to AB. In $\triangle BEC$ and $\triangle CFB$: $\angle BEC = \angle CFB = 90^\circ$, $\angle ECB = \angle FBC$ (base angles), $BC = CB$.
=> The triangles are congruent (A.A.S.), so $BE = CF$

# Equilateral triangles
- A triangle with all three sides equal is **equilateral**. Each of its angles is $60^\circ$.
- Conversely, a triangle with all angles equal (each $60^\circ$) is equilateral.
- An isosceles triangle with one angle of $60^\circ$ is equilateral.
- An **isosceles right triangle** has angles $90^\circ, 45^\circ, 45^\circ$.
?eg In an isosceles triangle the vertical angle is twice a base angle. Find the angles.
= Let each base angle be $x$. Then $x + x + 2x = 180^\circ$.
=> $45^\circ, 45^\circ, 90^\circ$

# Angle-chasing
- Mark equal sides, then mark the equal angles opposite them.
- Use the angle sum of a triangle, the exterior angle property and angles on a straight line.
- Give each unknown angle a letter and form an equation.
?eg In triangle ABC, $AB = AC$. Side BC is produced to D and $\angle ACD = 115^\circ$. Find the angles of the triangle.
= $\angle ACB = 180^\circ - 115^\circ = 65^\circ = \angle ABC$
= $\angle A = 180^\circ - 130^\circ$
=> $65^\circ, 65^\circ, 50^\circ$
?eg In the figure, D is a point on BC such that $AB = AD = DC$ and $\angle B = 70^\circ$. Find $\angle C$.
= $AB = AD$ gives $\angle ADB = \angle B = 70^\circ$.
= $\angle ADB$ is an exterior angle of $\triangle ADC$, and $AD = DC$ gives $\angle DAC = \angle C$. So $2\angle C = 70^\circ$.
=> $\angle C = 35^\circ$
!fig w=320 h=180; P A 110 30 t; P B 50 150 bl; P D 170 150 b; P C 290 150 br; L A B; L A D; L A C; L B C; A A B D 70°; cap AB = AD = DC
?eg ABC is an equilateral triangle and BCDE is a square drawn outside it. Find $\angle BAE$.
= $\angle ABE = 60^\circ + 90^\circ = 150^\circ$, and $AB = BC = BE$, so triangle ABE is isosceles.
= $\angle BAE = \dfrac{180^\circ - 150^\circ}{2}$
=> $15^\circ$
?eg In triangle ABC, $AB = AC$ and the bisectors of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ meet at O. Prove that $OB = OC$.
= $\angle B = \angle C$, so $\dfrac{1}{2}\angle B = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle C$, that is $\angle OBC = \angle OCB$.
=> Sides opposite equal angles are equal, so $OB = OC$

# Common mistakes
- Marking the wrong angles as equal. The equal angles are **opposite** the equal sides.
- Assuming a triangle is isosceles because it looks so in the figure.
- Using the bisector, median and altitude as the same line in a triangle that is not isosceles.
- Forgetting that the result works both ways: equal sides give equal angles, and equal angles give equal sides.
- Taking the given angle as a base angle when it is the vertical angle, or the other way round.

@@formulas
Theorem 1 | Equal sides have equal opposite angles
Theorem 2 | Equal angles have equal opposite sides
Base angle from vertical angle $v$ | $\dfrac{180^\circ - v}{2}$
Vertical angle from base angle $b$ | $180^\circ - 2b$
Altitude from the vertex | Bisects the base and the vertical angle
Equilateral triangle | Each angle $60^\circ$
Isosceles right triangle | Angles $90^\circ, 45^\circ, 45^\circ$
Height of an equilateral triangle | $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times$ side
Altitude of an isosceles triangle | $\sqrt{a^2 - \left(\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^2}$, equal sides $a$, base $b$
Exterior angle | Sum of the two interior opposite angles
Equal segments | Altitudes, medians and angle bisectors to the equal sides are equal
