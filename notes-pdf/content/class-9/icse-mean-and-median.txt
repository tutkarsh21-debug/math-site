# Arithmetic mean
- The **mean** $\bar{x}$ of $n$ values $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ is
$$\bar{x} = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \ldots + x_n}{n} = \frac{\sum x}{n}$$
- So $\sum x = n\bar{x}$: the sum of the values is the mean times their number.
- The sum of the **deviations** of the values from their mean is zero: $\sum (x - \bar{x}) = 0$.
?eg Find the mean of 12, 15, 9, 20, 14.
= $\dfrac{70}{5}$
=> 14
?eg The mean of 5 numbers is 18. Four of them are 12, 20, 15 and 25. Find the fifth.
= Sum $= 90$; the four known numbers add up to 72.
=> 18

# Properties of the mean
- If every value is **increased** (or decreased) by $a$, the mean increases (or decreases) by $a$.
- If every value is **multiplied** by $a$, the mean is multiplied by $a$. If every value is **divided** by $a$ ($a \neq 0$), the mean is divided by $a$.
?eg The mean of 10 numbers is 25. Find the new mean if each number is (i) increased by 4 (ii) multiplied by 3 (iii) divided by 5.
=> (i) 29 (ii) 75 (iii) 5
?eg The mean of 8 values is 30. If each value is doubled and then 5 is subtracted, find the new mean.
= $2 \times 30 - 5$
=> 55

# Corrected and combined means
- **Wrong entry**: correct sum $=$ recorded sum $-$ wrong value $+$ correct value. Then divide by $n$.
- **Value added or removed**: adjust the sum and the number of values.
- **Combined mean** of two groups with $n_1$, $n_2$ values and means $\bar{x}_1$, $\bar{x}_2$:
$$\bar{x} = \frac{n_1\bar{x}_1 + n_2\bar{x}_2}{n_1 + n_2}$$
?eg The mean of 20 observations is 32. Later it is found that 46 was wrongly read as 64. Find the correct mean.
= Recorded sum $= 640$. Correct sum $= 640 - 64 + 46 = 622$.
=> 31.1
?eg The mean weight of 24 students is 40 kg. When the teacher's weight is included, the mean rises by 1 kg. Find the teacher's weight.
= New sum $= 25 \times 41 = 1025$; old sum $= 960$.
=> 65 kg
?eg The mean of 30 boys is 62 marks and the mean of 20 girls is 67 marks. Find the mean of the whole class.
= $\dfrac{30 \times 62 + 20 \times 67}{50} = \dfrac{3200}{50}$
=> 64
?eg The mean of 9 numbers is 15. One number is removed and the mean becomes 14. Find the number removed.
= $135 - 8 \times 14$
=> 23

# Mean of a frequency distribution
- If the values $x_1, x_2, \ldots$ occur with frequencies $f_1, f_2, \ldots$:
$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}$$
| $x$ | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | Total |
| $f$ | 4 | 6 | 10 | 7 | 3 | 30 |
| $fx$ | 20 | 60 | 150 | 140 | 75 | 445 |
?eg Find the mean of the distribution in the table.
= $\dfrac{445}{30}$
=> 14.83 (to two decimal places)
?eg The mean of the distribution below is 6. Find $p$. Values 2, 4, 6, 10, $p + 5$ with frequencies 3, 2, 3, 1, 2.
= $\sum f = 11$ and $\sum fx = 6 + 8 + 18 + 10 + 2p + 10 = 52 + 2p$.
= $52 + 2p = 66$
=> $p = 7$

# Median
- The **median** is the middle value when the data are arranged in ascending or descending order.
- For $n$ values in order:
| $n$ | Median |
| Odd | The $\left(\dfrac{n + 1}{2}\right)$th value |
| Even | The mean of the $\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)$th and $\left(\dfrac{n}{2} + 1\right)$th values |
- The median is not affected by very large or very small values at the ends. The mean is.
?eg Find the median of 17, 9, 24, 12, 30, 15, 21.
= In order: 9, 12, 15, 17, 21, 24, 30. The 4th value is the median.
=> 17
?eg Find the median of 8, 15, 3, 11, 20, 6, 13, 18.
= In order: 3, 6, 8, 11, 13, 15, 18, 20. The 4th and 5th values are 11 and 13.
=> 12
?eg The values $11, 13, 15, x + 1, x + 3, 30, 35, 40$ are in ascending order and their median is 22. Find $x$.
= The median is the mean of the 4th and 5th values: $\dfrac{(x + 1) + (x + 3)}{2} = x + 2$.
=> $x = 20$

# Median of a frequency distribution
- Write the cumulative frequencies. With total frequency $N$, find the position of the median as above, then read the value whose cumulative frequency first reaches that position.
| $x$ | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 11 |
| $f$ | 4 | 8 | 10 | 6 | 3 |
| Cumulative frequency | 4 | 12 | 22 | 28 | 31 |
?eg Find the median of the distribution in the table.
= $N = 31$ is odd, so the median is the 16th value. Values from the 13th to the 22nd are all 7.
=> 7

# Common mistakes
- Finding the median without arranging the data in order.
- For an even number of values, taking only one of the two middle values.
- Dividing $\sum fx$ by the number of different values instead of by $\sum f$.
- In a wrong-entry problem, subtracting the correct value and adding the wrong one.
- Forgetting to change the number of values when one is added or removed.
- Averaging two means directly when the groups have different sizes.
