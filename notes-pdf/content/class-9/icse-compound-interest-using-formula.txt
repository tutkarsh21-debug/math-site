@summary The compound interest formula and its variations: different rates, half-yearly compounding, fractional periods, finding the principal, rate or time, and applications to growth and depreciation.

# The formula
- If a principal $P$ is invested at $r\%$ per annum compound interest for $n$ years, compounded yearly, the amount $A$ is
$$A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)^n$$
- Compound interest $= A - P = P\left[\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^n - 1\right]$.
- Each year the amount is multiplied by the same factor $\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)$.
?eg Find the amount and the C.I. on ₹8000 for 3 years at 5% per annum.
= $A = 8000\left(\dfrac{21}{20}\right)^3 = 8000 \times \dfrac{9261}{8000}$
=> Amount ₹9261; C.I. ₹1261
?eg Find the C.I. on ₹25,000 for 2 years at 12% per annum.
= $A = 25000 \times (1.12)^2 = 25000 \times 1.2544 = 31360$
=> ₹6360

# Different rates in different years
- If the rates for successive years are $r_1\%$, $r_2\%$, $r_3\%$:
$$A = P\left(1 + \frac{r_1}{100}\right)\left(1 + \frac{r_2}{100}\right)\left(1 + \frac{r_3}{100}\right)$$
?eg Find the amount on ₹10,000 for 3 years if the rates are 5%, 8% and 10%.
= $10000 \times 1.05 \times 1.08 \times 1.10$
=> ₹12,474

# Other compounding periods
- **Half-yearly**: rate $\dfrac{r}{2}\%$ per period and $2n$ periods: $A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{200}\right)^{2n}$.
- **Quarterly**: $A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{400}\right)^{4n}$.
- **Time not a whole number of years** (compounded yearly), such as $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ years: use the formula for the whole years and simple interest for the remaining fraction.
$$A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{100}\right)^2\left(1 + \frac{\tfrac{1}{2} \times r}{100}\right)$$
?eg Find the amount on ₹16,000 for $1\tfrac{1}{2}$ years at 10% per annum, compounded half-yearly.
= $A = 16000\left(1 + \dfrac{5}{100}\right)^3 = 16000 \times \dfrac{9261}{8000}$
=> ₹18,522
?eg Find the amount on ₹20,000 for $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ years at 10% per annum, compounded yearly.
= $20000 \times (1.1)^2 \times \left(1 + \dfrac{5}{100}\right) = 24200 \times 1.05$
=> ₹25,410

# Finding P, r or n
- **Principal**: $P = A \div \left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^n$.
- **Rate**: find $\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^n = \dfrac{A}{P}$ and take the $n$th root.
- **Time**: write $\dfrac{A}{P}$ as a power of $\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)$ and compare the exponents.
?eg What sum amounts to ₹6655 in 3 years at 10% per annum compound interest?
= $P = 6655 \div (1.1)^3 = 6655 \div 1.331$
=> ₹5000
?eg At what rate will ₹4000 amount to ₹4840 in 2 years, compounded yearly?
= $\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^2 = \dfrac{4840}{4000} = \dfrac{121}{100} = \left(\dfrac{11}{10}\right)^2$
=> 10% per annum
?eg In how many years will ₹8000 amount to ₹9261 at 5% per annum?
= $\left(\dfrac{21}{20}\right)^n = \dfrac{9261}{8000} = \left(\dfrac{21}{20}\right)^3$
=> 3 years
?eg A sum becomes $\dfrac{27}{8}$ times itself in 3 years at compound interest. Find the rate.
= $\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^3 = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^3$, so $\dfrac{r}{100} = \dfrac{1}{2}$
=> 50% per annum

# C.I. and S.I. compared
- For **2 years**: C.I. $-$ S.I. $= P\left(\dfrac{r}{100}\right)^2$.
- For **3 years**: C.I. $-$ S.I. $= P\left(\dfrac{r}{100}\right)^2\left(3 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)$.
?eg The difference between the C.I. and the S.I. on a sum for 2 years at 5% is ₹20. Find the sum.
= $P \times \left(\dfrac{5}{100}\right)^2 = 20$, so $P = 20 \times 400$
=> ₹8000
?eg Find C.I. $-$ S.I. on ₹8000 for 3 years at 5%.
= $8000 \times \dfrac{1}{400} \times 3.05$
=> ₹61

# Growth and depreciation
- **Growth** at $r\%$ per year: value after $n$ years $= V_0\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^n$.
- **Depreciation** at $r\%$ per year: value after $n$ years $= V_0\left(1 - \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^n$.
- **Value $n$ years ago** (growth): $V_0 \div \left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^n$.
- Growth one year and decline the next: multiply the two factors.
?eg The population of a town is 64,000 and grows at 5% per year. Find the population after 3 years.
= $64000 \times \left(\dfrac{21}{20}\right)^3$
=> 74,088
?eg A machine depreciates at 10% per year and is now worth ₹72,900. What was it worth 3 years ago?
= $V \times (0.9)^3 = 72900$, so $V = 72900 \div 0.729$
=> ₹1,00,000
?eg The population of a village was 20,000. It rose by 10% in the first year and fell by 10% in the second. Find the population after 2 years.
= $20000 \times 1.1 \times 0.9$
=> 19,800

# Common mistakes
- Using $n$ instead of $2n$, or $r$ instead of $\dfrac{r}{2}$, for half-yearly compounding.
- Writing the factor as $\left(1 + r\right)$ instead of $\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)$.
- Using a plus sign for depreciation.
- Thinking that a rise of 10% followed by a fall of 10% gives the original value. It gives 99% of it.
- Reporting $A$ when C.I. is asked.
- For $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ years, using the power $2.5$ instead of two full years and simple interest for the half year.
