@title Solution of Right Triangles
@summary Finding unknown sides and angles of right-angled triangles using trigonometrical ratios of standard angles, including figures made of two or more right triangles.

# What "solving" means
- A triangle has six parts: three sides and three angles. To **solve** a right triangle is to find its unknown parts from the given ones.
- A right triangle can be solved when, besides the right angle, we know **one side and one acute angle**, or **two sides**.
- The two acute angles add up to $90^\circ$.
| To find | Known | Use |
| Perpendicular | Hypotenuse and angle | $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{perpendicular}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ |
| Base | Hypotenuse and angle | $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{base}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ |
| Perpendicular | Base and angle | $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{perpendicular}}{\text{base}}$ |
| Angle | Two sides | The ratio of those two sides |
- Choose the ratio that connects the **known side** and the **wanted side**.

# The two special triangles
- **30-60-90 triangle**: sides opposite $30^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$ are in the ratio $1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$. The side opposite $30^\circ$ is half the hypotenuse.
- **45-45-90 triangle**: sides are in the ratio $1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}$.

# One side and one angle given
?eg In triangle ABC, $\angle B = 90^\circ$, $\angle C = 30^\circ$ and $AC = 20$ cm. Find AB and BC.
= $AB = AC\sin 30^\circ = 20 \times \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $BC = AC\cos 30^\circ = 20 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
=> $AB = 10$ cm, $BC = 10\sqrt{3}$ cm
?eg In triangle PQR, $\angle Q = 90^\circ$, $\angle P = 60^\circ$ and $PQ = 8$ cm. Find QR and PR.
= $\tan 60^\circ = \dfrac{QR}{8}$ gives $QR = 8\sqrt{3}$. $\cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{8}{PR}$ gives $PR = 16$.
=> $QR = 8\sqrt{3}$ cm, $PR = 16$ cm
?eg The diagonal of a square is $10\sqrt{2}$ cm. Find its side.
= The diagonal makes $45^\circ$ with a side: side $= 10\sqrt{2}\cos 45^\circ$
=> 10 cm

# Two sides given
?eg In triangle ABC, $\angle B = 90^\circ$, $AB = 5$ cm and $AC = 10$ cm. Find $\angle A$, $\angle C$ and BC.
= $\cos A = \dfrac{5}{10} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\angle A = 60^\circ$ and $\angle C = 30^\circ$.
= $BC = 10\sin 60^\circ$
=> $\angle A = 60^\circ$, $\angle C = 30^\circ$, $BC = 5\sqrt{3}$ cm
?eg In triangle ABC, $\angle B = 90^\circ$ and $AB = BC = 7$ cm. Find the angles and AC.
= $\tan A = 1$, so $\angle A = \angle C = 45^\circ$. $AC = 7\sqrt{2}$.
=> $45^\circ, 45^\circ$; $7\sqrt{2}$ cm

# Triangles with an altitude
- Any triangle can be split into two right triangles by an altitude. Solve each part and combine.
- The altitude is the common side: find it from one triangle and use it in the other.
?eg In triangle ABC, AD is perpendicular to BC, $\angle B = 45^\circ$, $\angle C = 30^\circ$ and $AD = 6$ cm. Find BC.
= In triangle ABD: $\tan 45^\circ = \dfrac{6}{BD}$, so $BD = 6$ cm.
= In triangle ADC: $\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{6}{DC}$, so $DC = 6\sqrt{3}$ cm.
=> $BC = 6 + 6\sqrt{3} = 6(1 + \sqrt{3})$ cm, about 16.39 cm

?eg In triangle ABC, $AB = AC = 10$ cm and $\angle B = 30^\circ$. Find BC and the altitude from A.
= The altitude AD bisects BC. $AD = 10\sin 30^\circ = 5$ cm and $BD = 10\cos 30^\circ = 5\sqrt{3}$ cm.
=> $BC = 10\sqrt{3}$ cm; altitude 5 cm

# Two triangles on a common side
- When two right triangles share a side, set up an equation for the unknown length using the tangent in each.
?eg From a point C on the ground, the top A of a vertical pole AB is seen at $60^\circ$. From D, 20 m farther from the pole on the same line, it is seen at $30^\circ$. Find the height of the pole.
= Let $AB = h$ and $BC = x$. Then $h = x\sqrt{3}$ and $h = \dfrac{x + 20}{\sqrt{3}}$.
= $3x = x + 20$, so $x = 10$
=> $h = 10\sqrt{3}$ m, about 17.32 m
?eg In a trapezium ABCD, $AB \parallel DC$, $\angle A = \angle B = 60^\circ$, $DC = 8$ cm and the height is $5\sqrt{3}$ cm. Find AB.
= Drop perpendiculars from D and C. Each end triangle has base $\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{\tan 60^\circ} = 5$ cm.
=> $AB = 8 + 5 + 5 = 18$ cm

# Other figures
?eg A rhombus has side 12 cm and one angle $60^\circ$. Find its diagonals.
= The diagonals bisect the angles and meet at right angles. Half the shorter diagonal $= 12\sin 30^\circ = 6$ cm; half the longer $= 12\cos 30^\circ = 6\sqrt{3}$ cm.
=> 12 cm and $12\sqrt{3}$ cm
?eg A ladder 6 m long leans against a wall and makes $60^\circ$ with the ground. How high does it reach, and how far is its foot from the wall?
= Height $= 6\sin 60^\circ$; distance $= 6\cos 60^\circ$
=> $3\sqrt{3}$ m (about 5.2 m) and 3 m
?eg A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends $120^\circ$ at the centre. Find its length.
= The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord and the angle: half the chord $= 10\sin 60^\circ = 5\sqrt{3}$ cm.
=> $10\sqrt{3}$ cm

# Common mistakes
- Choosing a ratio that does not include the known side.
- Using $\sin$ where $\cos$ is needed, by mixing up the perpendicular and the base.
- Applying the ratios in a triangle that has no right angle. Draw an altitude first.
- Writing $\tan 30^\circ = \sqrt{3}$.
- Forgetting to add (or subtract) the two parts of the base at the end.
- Rounding too early: keep surds until the last step. Use $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$ and $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$.

@@formulas
Perpendicular | Hypotenuse $\times \sin\theta$
Base | Hypotenuse $\times \cos\theta$
Perpendicular from the base | Base $\times \tan\theta$
Hypotenuse | $\dfrac{\text{perpendicular}}{\sin\theta} = \dfrac{\text{base}}{\cos\theta}$
Acute angles | $\angle A + \angle C = 90^\circ$
30-60-90 triangle | $1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$
45-45-90 triangle | $1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}$
Side opposite $30^\circ$ | Half the hypotenuse
Diagonal of a square | Side $\times \sqrt{2}$
Altitude of an equilateral triangle | $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times$ side
Chord subtending $2\theta$ at the centre | $2r\sin\theta$
Surd values | $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$, $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$
