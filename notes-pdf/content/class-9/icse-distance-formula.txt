@title Distance Formula
@summary The formula for the distance between two points in the Cartesian plane, and its use to test for collinear points, identify triangles and quadrilaterals, and find points and circles.

# The formula
- The distance between $P(x_1, y_1)$ and $Q(x_2, y_2)$ is
$$PQ = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$
- It comes from Pythagoras theorem: the horizontal step is $x_2 - x_1$ and the vertical step is $y_2 - y_1$.
- The distance of $P(x, y)$ from the **origin** is $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$.
- The order of the points does not matter, because the differences are squared.

?eg Find the distance between $A(2, 3)$ and $B(6, 6)$.
= $\sqrt{(6 - 2)^2 + (6 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9}$
=> 5 units
?eg Find the distance between $P(-3, 4)$ and $Q(5, -2)$.
= $\sqrt{8^2 + (-6)^2} = \sqrt{100}$
=> 10 units
?eg Find the distance of $(-5, 12)$ from the origin.
=> 13 units

# Points on the axes
- Distance between $(x_1, 0)$ and $(x_2, 0)$ on the x-axis: $|x_2 - x_1|$.
- Distance between $(0, y_1)$ and $(0, y_2)$ on the y-axis: $|y_2 - y_1|$.
- Distance of $(x, y)$ from the x-axis is $|y|$, and from the y-axis is $|x|$.

# Finding an unknown co-ordinate
- Write the distance formula, square both sides and solve. There are often **two** answers.
?eg The distance between $A(2, -3)$ and $B(10, y)$ is 10 units. Find $y$.
= $64 + (y + 3)^2 = 100$, so $(y + 3)^2 = 36$ and $y + 3 = \pm 6$.
=> $y = 3$ or $y = -9$
?eg Find the point on the x-axis equidistant from $A(2, -5)$ and $B(-2, 9)$.
= Let the point be $P(x, 0)$. $PA^2 = PB^2$: $(x - 2)^2 + 25 = (x + 2)^2 + 81$.
= $-4x + 29 = 4x + 85$, so $8x = -56$.
=> $(-7, 0)$
?eg Find the point on the y-axis equidistant from $(6, 5)$ and $(-4, 3)$.
= Let it be $(0, y)$: $36 + (y - 5)^2 = 16 + (y - 3)^2$, so $61 - 10y = 25 - 6y$.
=> $(0, 9)$
?eg Find a relation between $x$ and $y$ if $P(x, y)$ is equidistant from $A(3, 6)$ and $B(-3, 4)$.
= $(x - 3)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = (x + 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2$
= $-6x - 12y + 45 = 6x - 8y + 25$
=> $3x + y = 5$

# Collinear points
- Three points A, B, C are **collinear** if the sum of two of the distances equals the third: for example $AB + BC = AC$.
?eg Show that $A(1, 2)$, $B(3, 4)$ and $C(6, 7)$ are collinear.
= $AB = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$, $BC = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$, $AC = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$.
=> $AB + BC = AC$, so the points are collinear

# Identifying triangles
| Triangle | Test |
| Scalene | All three sides different |
| Isosceles | Two sides equal |
| Equilateral | All three sides equal |
| Right-angled | (longest side)$^2 =$ sum of the squares of the other two |
| Right-angled isosceles | Two sides equal and Pythagoras holds |
- Work with the **squares** of the lengths to avoid square roots.
?eg Show that $A(3, 0)$, $B(6, 4)$ and $C(-1, 3)$ are the vertices of a right-angled isosceles triangle.
= $AB^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$, $AC^2 = 16 + 9 = 25$, $BC^2 = 49 + 1 = 50$.
=> $AB = AC$ and $AB^2 + AC^2 = BC^2$, so the triangle is isosceles with a right angle at A
?eg Show that $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(1, \sqrt{3})$ form an equilateral triangle.
= The three squared distances are $4$, $1 + 3 = 4$ and $1 + 3 = 4$.
=> All sides are 2 units

# Identifying quadrilaterals
- Find the four sides and the two diagonals.
| Quadrilateral | Sides | Diagonals |
| Parallelogram | Opposite sides equal | Not equal |
| Rectangle | Opposite sides equal | Equal |
| Rhombus | All sides equal | Not equal |
| Square | All sides equal | Equal |
?eg Show that $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 1)$, $C(4, 4)$ and $D(1, 4)$ are the vertices of a square.
= $AB = BC = CD = DA = 3$. $AC^2 = 9 + 9 = 18$ and $BD^2 = 9 + 9 = 18$.
=> All sides are equal and the diagonals are equal: a square
?eg Show that $P(-2, -1)$, $Q(1, 0)$, $R(4, 3)$ and $S(1, 2)$ form a parallelogram.
= $PQ^2 = 9 + 1 = 10$, $RS^2 = 9 + 1 = 10$; $QR^2 = 9 + 9 = 18$, $SP^2 = 9 + 9 = 18$.
=> Opposite sides are equal, so PQRS is a parallelogram

# Circles
- Every point on a circle is at distance $r$ from the centre. To check whether a point lies on a circle, compare its distance from the centre with the radius.
- The **circumcentre** of a triangle is equidistant from its three vertices.
?eg A circle has centre $(2, 1)$ and passes through $(5, 5)$. Find its radius. Does $(-1, 5)$ lie on it?
= $r = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$. Distance of $(-1, 5)$ from the centre $= \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$.
=> Radius 5; yes
?eg Find the centre of the circle through $(0, 0)$, $(6, 0)$ and $(0, 8)$.
= Let the centre be $(h, k)$: $h^2 + k^2 = (h - 6)^2 + k^2$ gives $h = 3$; $h^2 + k^2 = h^2 + (k - 8)^2$ gives $k = 4$.
=> Centre $(3, 4)$; radius 5

# Common mistakes
- Subtracting a negative co-ordinate wrongly: $5 - (-3) = 8$, not 2.
- Forgetting the square root, or forgetting to square the differences.
- Giving only one value when the equation gives two.
- Calling a quadrilateral a square after checking only the sides. A rhombus also has four equal sides; check the diagonals.
- Testing collinearity with two distances only.
- Writing a point on the x-axis as $(0, x)$.
