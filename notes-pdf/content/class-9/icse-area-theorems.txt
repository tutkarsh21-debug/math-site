@summary Theorems comparing the areas of parallelograms and triangles on the same base and between the same parallels, with the results on medians and their use in proofs.

# Basic ideas
- **Congruent figures have equal areas.** Figures with equal areas need not be congruent.
- If a figure is divided into parts that do not overlap, its area is the sum of the areas of the parts.
- Two figures are **on the same base and between the same parallels** if they share a base and the vertices (or sides) opposite the base lie on one line parallel to the base.
- Figures between the same parallels have the **same height**.

# The theorems
- **Theorem 1**: a parallelogram and a rectangle on the same base and between the same parallels are **equal in area**. So the area of a parallelogram $=$ base $\times$ height.
- **Theorem 2**: parallelograms on the same base (or equal bases) and between the same parallels are **equal in area**.
- **Theorem 3**: the area of a triangle is **half** the area of a parallelogram on the same base and between the same parallels. So the area of a triangle $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times$ base $\times$ height.
- **Theorem 4**: triangles on the same base (or equal bases) and between the same parallels are **equal in area**.
- **Converse of Theorem 4**: triangles with equal areas on the same base, and on the same side of it, lie between the same parallels.

# Medians and areas
- **A median divides a triangle into two triangles of equal area.** Both have equal bases and the same height.
- The three medians divide a triangle into **six triangles of equal area**.
- If G is the centroid of triangle ABC: area(GAB) $=$ area(GBC) $=$ area(GCA) $= \dfrac{1}{3}$ area(ABC).
- If D is a point on BC, then $\dfrac{\text{area}(ABD)}{\text{area}(ADC)} = \dfrac{BD}{DC}$, because the two triangles have the same height.
?eg AD is a median of triangle ABC and E is the mid-point of AD. If the area of triangle ABC is 48 sq cm, find the area of triangle BED.
= Area(ABD) $= 24$ sq cm. BE is a median of triangle ABD.
=> 12 sq cm
?eg D is a point on side BC of triangle ABC with $BD : DC = 2 : 3$. If the area of triangle ABC is 60 sq cm, find the area of triangle ABD.
= $\dfrac{2}{5} \times 60$
=> 24 sq cm

# Diagonals of quadrilaterals
- A diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two triangles of equal area.
- The two diagonals of a parallelogram divide it into **four triangles of equal area**.
- In a **trapezium** ABCD with $AB \parallel DC$ and diagonals meeting at O: area(AOD) $=$ area(BOC).
- Area of a rhombus $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2$.
- If the mid-points of the sides of a parallelogram are joined in order, the parallelogram formed has half the area.

=> 36 sq cm

# Using the theorems in proofs
- Look for a common base and a pair of parallel lines.
- Add or subtract a common area to pass from one pair of equal areas to another.
- To prove two lines parallel, show that two triangles on the same base have equal areas (converse).
?eg In triangle ABC, D and E are points on AB and AC such that area(DBC) $=$ area(EBC). Prove that $DE \parallel BC$.
= Triangles DBC and EBC are on the same base BC, on the same side of it, and have equal areas.
=> They lie between the same parallels, so $DE \parallel BC$
?eg ABCD is a parallelogram and P is any point inside it. Prove that area(APB) $+$ area(PCD) $= \dfrac{1}{2}$ area(ABCD).
= Through P draw a line parallel to AB, meeting AD at E and BC at F. Then ABFE and EFCD are parallelograms.
= Area(APB) $= \dfrac{1}{2}$ area(ABFE) and area(PCD) $= \dfrac{1}{2}$ area(EFCD).
=> Adding gives $\dfrac{1}{2}$ area(ABCD)
?eg D, E and F are the mid-points of sides BC, CA and AB of triangle ABC. Prove that area(DEF) $= \dfrac{1}{4}$ area(ABC).
= By the mid-point theorem, BDEF, DCEF and AFDE are parallelograms. A diagonal divides a parallelogram into two equal triangles.
= So triangles AFE, BDF, DCE and DEF all have equal areas.
=> Each is $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of area(ABC)
?eg In quadrilateral ABCD, a line through D parallel to AC meets BC produced at E. Prove that area(ABE) $=$ area(ABCD).
= Triangles ACD and ACE are on the same base AC and between the parallels AC and DE, so their areas are equal.
=> Adding area(ABC) to both: area(ABCD) $=$ area(ABE)

# Common mistakes
- Using a theorem when the figures are between the same parallels but **not** on the same or equal bases.
- Forgetting the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ between a triangle and a parallelogram.
- Assuming equal areas mean congruent triangles.
- Using a slant side as the height.
- In a trapezium, claiming that triangles AOB and COD have equal areas. It is AOD and BOC that are equal.
