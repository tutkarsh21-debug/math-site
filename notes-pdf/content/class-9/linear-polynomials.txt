@title Introduction to Linear Polynomials
@summary A linear polynomial has the form $ax + b$. This chapter shows how such expressions describe patterns that grow or fall by a fixed amount, and how they appear as straight lines on a graph.

# Algebraic expressions
- An **algebraic expression** combines numbers, variables and operation signs, for example $4x + 5y + 3$.
- The parts separated by $+$ or $-$ are the **terms**: here $4x$, $5y$ and $3$.
- The letters are the **variables** (earlier called letter-numbers): here $x$ and $y$.
- The number multiplying a variable is its **coefficient**: 4 is the coefficient of $x$ and 5 is the coefficient of $y$.
- A term with no variable is a **constant**: here 3.
?eg A rectangular plot is $l$ metres long and $w$ metres wide. Fencing costs ₹90 per metre and levelling costs ₹40 per square metre. Write an expression for the total cost.
= Perimeter $= 2l + 2w$, so fencing costs $90(2l + 2w) = 180l + 180w$.
= Area $= lw$, so levelling costs $40lw$.
=> Total cost $=$ ₹$(180l + 180w + 40lw)$

# Polynomials in one variable
- An expression in **one variable** whose powers are whole numbers is called a **polynomial** (a univariate polynomial).
- The **degree** is the highest power of the variable.
| Polynomial | Degree | Name |
| $8$ | 0 | Constant polynomial |
| $3z + 7$ | 1 | Linear polynomial |
| $x^2 + 5x + 1$ | 2 | Quadratic polynomial |
| $5y^3 + y^2 + 2y - 1$ | 3 | Cubic polynomial |
- A constant such as 8 has degree 0 because it can be written as $8x^0$.
- A **linear polynomial** has the form $ax + b$ with $a \neq 0$.
?eg For the polynomial $x^4 - 3x^3 + 6x^2 - 2x + 7$, write the degree, the coefficient of $x^3$ and the constant term.
= The highest power of $x$ is 4.
=> Degree 4; coefficient of $x^3$ is $-3$; constant term is 7

# Value of a polynomial and linear equations
- To find the **value** of a polynomial, substitute a number for the variable. The polynomial works like a machine: the number is the input and the value is the output.
- A polynomial seen in this way is a **function** of its variable. $2x + 3$ is a linear function and $10x - x^2$ is a quadratic function.
- When a linear polynomial is set equal to a number we get a **linear equation**, which can be solved for the variable.
?eg Find the value of $5x - 3$ at $x = -1$ and of $7s^2 - 4s + 6$ at $s = 4$.
= $5(-1) - 3 = -8$
= $7(16) - 4(4) + 6 = 112 - 16 + 6 = 102$
=> $-8$ and $102$
?eg Two numbers add up to 90, and one is 14 more than the other. Find them.
= Let the smaller number be $x$. Then the other is $x + 14$.
= $x + (x + 14) = 90$, so $2x = 76$ and $x = 38$.
=> The numbers are 38 and 52.
?eg A rectangle is 3 cm longer than twice its width, and its perimeter is 36 cm. Find its length and width.
= Let the width be $w$ cm. Then the length is $(2w + 3)$ cm.
= $2(w + 2w + 3) = 36$, so $6w + 6 = 36$ and $w = 5$.
=> Width 5 cm; length 13 cm

# Linear patterns
- A **linear pattern** is a sequence of numbers in which the difference between consecutive terms is always the same.
- Its $n$th term is a linear expression in $n$: (common difference) $\times\ n\ +$ (a constant).
- To find the rule, take the common difference as the coefficient of $n$, then adjust the constant so that $n = 1$ gives the first term.
?eg Triangles are made in a row with matchsticks, each new triangle sharing a side with the one before. The stages use 3, 5, 7, 9, ... matchsticks. Find the rule, the number needed at stage 15, and the stage that uses 61 matchsticks.
= The difference is 2, so the rule is $2n + c$. At $n = 1$: $2 + c = 3$, so $c = 1$.
= Stage 15: $2(15) + 1 = 31$
= $2n + 1 = 61$ gives $n = 30$.
=> Rule $2n + 1$; 31 matchsticks; stage 30
?eg Can 200 matchsticks make exactly one stage of this pattern?
= $2n + 1 = 200$ gives $n = 99.5$, which is not a whole number.
=> No. Every stage uses an odd number of matchsticks.

# Linear growth and linear decay
- **Linear growth**: a quantity **increases** by the same amount over equal intervals.
- **Linear decay**: a quantity **decreases** by the same amount over equal intervals.
- Both are described by (starting value) $+$ (change per interval) $\times$ (number of intervals). The change is positive for growth and negative for decay.
?eg A library charges a joining fee of ₹150 and ₹20 for each book borrowed. Write the cost of borrowing $n$ books. How many books were borrowed if the cost is ₹430?
= Cost $= 150 + 20n$. This is linear growth: each extra book adds ₹20.
= $150 + 20n = 430$ gives $n = 14$.
=> Cost $=$ ₹$(150 + 20n)$; 14 books
?eg A candle 24 cm tall burns down by 1.5 cm every hour. Write its height after $t$ hours. What is its height after 6 hours, and when does it burn out?
= Height $h = 24 - 1.5t$. This is linear decay.
= After 6 hours: $24 - 9 = 15$ cm. Burnt out when $24 - 1.5t = 0$, so $t = 16$.
=> $h = 24 - 1.5t$; 15 cm; after 16 hours

# Linear relationships
- Two quantities $x$ and $y$ have a **linear relationship** if $y = ax + b$ for fixed numbers $a$ and $b$.
- $a$ is the change in $y$ for each increase of 1 in $x$. $b$ is the value of $y$ when $x = 0$.
- To find $a$ and $b$ from two known pairs of values, substitute each pair into $y = ax + b$ and solve the two equations.
?eg A taxi charges a fixed amount plus a rate per kilometre. A 5 km ride costs ₹110 and a 12 km ride costs ₹215. Find the relation $y = ax + b$ between the fare $y$ and the distance $x$.
= $110 = 5a + b$ and $215 = 12a + b$
= Subtracting: $105 = 7a$, so $a = 15$. Then $b = 110 - 75 = 35$.
=> $y = 15x + 35$: a fixed charge of ₹35 and ₹15 per km

# Graph of a linear relationship
- The graph of $y = ax + b$ is a **straight line**. Two points are enough to draw it.
- A point lies on the line only if its coordinates satisfy the equation.
- $a$ is the **slope**: it tells how steep the line is. $b$ is the **$y$-intercept**: the line cuts the $y$-axis at $(0, b)$.
- $y = ax$ always passes through the origin. For $a > 1$ it is steeper than $y = x$, and for $0 < a < 1$ it is less steep.
- A **positive** slope means the line rises to the right (linear growth). A **negative** slope means it falls to the right (linear decay).
- Lines with the **same slope** and different $y$-intercepts are **parallel**.

?eg Write the slope and the $y$-intercept of $3y = 6x - 12$, and the point where the line cuts the $y$-axis.
= Divide by 3 to get the form $y = ax + b$: $y = 2x - 4$.
=> Slope 2; $y$-intercept $-4$; the line cuts the $y$-axis at $(0, -4)$
?eg The graph of a linear polynomial $p(x)$ passes through $(1, 4)$ and $(4, 13)$. Find $p(x)$ and the points where its graph cuts the axes.
= Let $p(x) = ax + b$. Then $a + b = 4$ and $4a + b = 13$.
= Subtracting: $3a = 9$, so $a = 3$ and $b = 1$.
= It cuts the $y$-axis at $(0, 1)$. It cuts the $x$-axis where $3x + 1 = 0$, that is $x = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.
=> $p(x) = 3x + 1$; the graph cuts the axes at $(0, 1)$ and $\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}, 0\right)$

# Common mistakes
- Calling $10x - x^2$ linear because it has an $x$ term. The degree is the **highest** power, here 2.
- Reading the slope from $2y = 4x + 7$ as 4. First divide by 2: $y = 2x + 3.5$, so the slope is 2.
- Forgetting the sign of the coefficient: in $x^4 - 3x^3$ the coefficient of $x^3$ is $-3$.
- Writing the $n$th term of 3, 5, 7, ... as $2n$. Check with $n = 1$: the rule must give 3, so it is $2n + 1$.
- Thinking a larger $y$-intercept makes a line steeper. Steepness depends only on the slope.

@@formulas
Linear polynomial | $ax + b$, with $a \neq 0$
Degree | The highest power of the variable
Names by degree | 0 constant, 1 linear, 2 quadratic, 3 cubic
Linear equation | A linear polynomial set equal to a number, such as $2x + 10 = 64$
Linear pattern, $n$th term | (common difference) $\times\ n\ +$ constant
Linear growth | Starting value $+$ (fixed increase) $\times$ (number of intervals)
Linear decay | Starting value $-$ (fixed decrease) $\times$ (number of intervals)
Linear relationship | $y = ax + b$
Slope | $a$ in $y = ax + b$
y-intercept | $b$ in $y = ax + b$; the line cuts the $y$-axis at $(0, b)$
Line through the origin | $y = ax$
Parallel lines | Same slope $a$, different $y$-intercepts $b$
Rising and falling lines | Slope positive: rises to the right. Slope negative: falls to the right.
