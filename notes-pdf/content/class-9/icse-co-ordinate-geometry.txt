# The Cartesian plane
- Two perpendicular number lines, the **x-axis** (horizontal) and the **y-axis** (vertical), meet at the **origin** $O(0, 0)$.
- The position of a point is given by an **ordered pair** $(x, y)$. The first number is the **abscissa** (x co-ordinate): its distance from the y-axis. The second is the **ordinate** (y co-ordinate): its distance from the x-axis.
- The order matters: $(2, 5)$ and $(5, 2)$ are different points.
- The axes divide the plane into four **quadrants**:
| Quadrant | Sign of $x$ | Sign of $y$ | Example |
| I | $+$ | $+$ | $(3, 4)$ |
| II | $-$ | $+$ | $(-3, 4)$ |
| III | $-$ | $-$ | $(-3, -4)$ |
| IV | $+$ | $-$ | $(3, -4)$ |
- A point on the **x-axis** has $y = 0$: $(a, 0)$. A point on the **y-axis** has $x = 0$: $(0, b)$.

?eg In which quadrant or on which axis do these points lie: $(-2, 5)$, $(4, -1)$, $(0, -3)$, $(-6, 0)$?
=> Quadrant II; quadrant IV; on the y-axis; on the x-axis
?eg Find $x$ and $y$ if $(2x - 1, y + 3) = (5, 7)$.
= Equal ordered pairs have equal co-ordinates: $2x - 1 = 5$ and $y + 3 = 7$.
=> $x = 3$, $y = 4$
?eg A(2, 1), B(7, 1) and C(7, 4) are three vertices of a rectangle ABCD. Find D and the area.
= D has the x co-ordinate of A and the y co-ordinate of C. $AB = 5$ and $BC = 3$.
=> $D(2, 4)$; area 15 square units

# Dependent and independent variables
- In $y = 3x + 2$, the value of $y$ depends on the value chosen for $x$. Here $x$ is the **independent variable** and $y$ the **dependent variable**.
- If the equation is solved for $x$, as in $x = \dfrac{y - 2}{3}$, the roles are reversed.

# Graph of a linear equation
- The graph of $ax + by + c = 0$ is a **straight line**. Every point on the line satisfies the equation, and every solution lies on the line.
- **To draw it**: make $y$ the subject; choose three convenient values of $x$ and find $y$; plot the three points; join them with a ruler. Two points fix the line; the third is a check.
| Equation | Graph |
| $x = 0$ | The y-axis |
| $y = 0$ | The x-axis |
| $x = a$ | A line parallel to the y-axis, $a$ units from it |
| $y = b$ | A line parallel to the x-axis, $b$ units from it |
| $y = mx$ | A line through the origin |
?eg Make a table of three points for the graph of $2x - y = 4$.
= $y = 2x - 4$. For $x = 0, 2, 3$: $y = -4, 0, 2$.
=> $(0, -4)$, $(2, 0)$, $(3, 2)$
?eg Does the point $(3, -1)$ lie on the line $2x + 3y = 3$?
= $2(3) + 3(-1) = 3$
=> Yes
?eg The point $(k, 4)$ lies on the line $3x - 2y = 1$. Find $k$.
= $3k - 8 = 1$
=> $k = 3$
?eg Find the area of the triangle formed by the line $3x + 4y = 12$ and the axes.
= The line meets the x-axis at $(4, 0)$ and the y-axis at $(0, 3)$.
=> $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3 = 6$ square units

# Inclination and slope
- The **inclination** $\theta$ of a line is the angle it makes with the **positive direction of the x-axis**, measured anticlockwise.
- The **slope** (gradient) is $m = \tan\theta$.
| Inclination | Slope |
| $0^\circ$ (parallel to the x-axis) | 0 |
| $30^\circ$ | $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ |
| $45^\circ$ | 1 |
| $60^\circ$ | $\sqrt{3}$ |
| $90^\circ$ (parallel to the y-axis) | Not defined |
- A line rising to the right has a positive slope. A line falling to the right has a negative slope.
?eg Find the slope of a line whose inclination is $60^\circ$, and the inclination of a line whose slope is 1.
=> $\sqrt{3}$; $45^\circ$

# The form y = mx + c
- When the equation of a line is written as $y = mx + c$:
- $m$ is the **slope**.
- $c$ is the **y-intercept**: the line cuts the y-axis at $(0, c)$.
- To find the slope and the y-intercept of any line, make $y$ the subject.
- The **x-intercept** is found by putting $y = 0$.
- Lines with the **same slope** are parallel.
?eg Find the slope and the y-intercept of $3x + 4y = 8$.
= $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x + 2$
=> Slope $-\dfrac{3}{4}$; y-intercept 2
?eg Write the equation of the line with slope 2 and y-intercept $-5$.
=> $y = 2x - 5$
?eg Find the equation of the line with inclination $45^\circ$ that cuts the y-axis at $(0, 3)$.
= $m = \tan 45^\circ = 1$ and $c = 3$
=> $y = x + 3$
?eg Are the lines $2x - y = 5$ and $4x - 2y = 3$ parallel?
= $y = 2x - 5$ and $y = 2x - \dfrac{3}{2}$: both have slope 2.
=> Yes
?eg The line $y = mx + c$ passes through $(0, 4)$ and $(2, 10)$. Find $m$ and $c$.
= From $(0, 4)$: $c = 4$. From $(2, 10)$: $10 = 2m + 4$.
=> $m = 3$, $c = 4$

# Common mistakes
- Plotting $(x, y)$ with the two numbers interchanged.
- Saying a point such as $(0, 5)$ lies on the x-axis. It is on the y-axis, since $x = 0$.
- Reading the slope from $ax + by + c = 0$ as $a$. Make $y$ the subject first: the slope is $-\dfrac{a}{b}$.
- Taking the sign of the intercept wrongly: in $y = 2x - 5$ the y-intercept is $-5$.
- Drawing $x = 3$ parallel to the x-axis. It is parallel to the y-axis.
- Joining plotted points freehand instead of with a ruler.
