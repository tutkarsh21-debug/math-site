T: The distance formula
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] $\triangle ABC$ has vertices $A(5, -6)$, $B(6, 4)$ and $C(0, 0)$. Find the length of its median $AD$. (a) $\sqrt{68}$ units  (b) $2\sqrt{15}$ units  (c) $\sqrt{101}$ units  (d) $10$ units
S: $D$ is the mid-point of $BC$: $D = \left(\dfrac{6 + 0}{2}, \dfrac{4 + 0}{2}\right) = (3, 2)$
S: $AD = \sqrt{(5 - 3)^2 + (-6 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 64} = \sqrt{68}$
S: **Answer: (a)**
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] The points $(3, -5)$ and $(x, -5)$ are 15 units apart. Find the possible values of $x$. (a) $12, -18$  (b) $-12, 18$  (c) $18, 5$  (d) $-9, -12$
S: Both points have the same $y$-coordinate, so the distance is $|x - 3| = 15$.
S: $x - 3 = 15$ or $x - 3 = -15$, so $x = 18$ or $x = -12$.
S: **Answer: (b)**

T: The section formula
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] A circle has centre $(2, 0)$, and one end of a diameter is $(6, 0)$. Find the other end. (a) $(0, 0)$  (b) $(4, 0)$  (c) $(-2, 0)$  (d) $(-6, 0)$
S: The centre is the mid-point of the diameter. Let the other end be $(x, y)$.
S: $\dfrac{6 + x}{2} = 2$ gives $x = -2$, and $\dfrac{0 + y}{2} = 0$ gives $y = 0$.
S: **Answer: (c)**
Q: [CBSE 2024, 3 marks] The point $\left(\dfrac{8}{5}, y\right)$ lies on the segment joining $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 3)$. In what ratio does it divide the segment? Find $y$ as well.
S: Let the ratio be $k : 1$. Then $\dfrac{2k + 1}{k + 1} = \dfrac{8}{5}$.
S: $10k + 5 = 8k + 8$, so $k = \dfrac{3}{2}$. The ratio is $3 : 2$.
S: $y = \dfrac{3 \times 3 + 2 \times 2}{3 + 2} = \dfrac{13}{5}$
S: **Answer: ratio $3 : 2$ and $y = \dfrac{13}{5}$**
Q: [CBSE 2024, 3 marks] $A(-1, -1)$, $B(-1, 6)$, $C(3, 6)$ and $D(3, -1)$ are the vertices of a rectangle. $P$, $Q$, $R$ and $S$ are the mid-points of $AB$, $BC$, $CD$ and $DA$. Show that the diagonals of $PQRS$ bisect each other.
S: $P = \left(-1, \dfrac{5}{2}\right)$, $Q = (1, 6)$, $R = \left(3, \dfrac{5}{2}\right)$, $S = (1, -1)$
S: Mid-point of diagonal $PR = \left(\dfrac{-1 + 3}{2}, \dfrac{5/2 + 5/2}{2}\right) = \left(1, \dfrac{5}{2}\right)$
S: Mid-point of diagonal $QS = \left(\dfrac{1 + 1}{2}, \dfrac{6 - 1}{2}\right) = \left(1, \dfrac{5}{2}\right)$
S: **The diagonals $PR$ and $QS$ have the same mid-point, so they bisect each other.**
