@summary This chapter is about tangents to a circle: what a tangent is, how many can be drawn from a point, and the two theorems that every question is built on.

# Lines and a circle
- A line and a circle in the same plane can meet in three ways.
| Position of the line | Points in common | Name |
| Does not meet the circle | 0 | Non-intersecting line |
| Cuts the circle | 2 | Secant |
| Touches the circle | 1 | Tangent |
- A **tangent** to a circle is a line that meets the circle at exactly one point. That point is the **point of contact**.
- A tangent is the limiting position of a secant when its two points of intersection come together.
- There is one and only one tangent at any given point of a circle.

# Number of tangents from a point
| Position of the point | Number of tangents |
| Inside the circle | 0 |
| On the circle | 1 |
| Outside the circle | 2 |
- The **length of a tangent** from an external point $P$ is the length of the segment from $P$ to the point of contact.
- A circle has infinitely many tangents, one at each of its points. It has exactly two tangents parallel to a given line.

# Theorem 1: tangent and radius
- **Theorem**: the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
- So if $PT$ is a tangent at $T$ to a circle with centre $O$, then $\angle OTP = 90°$ and $\triangle OTP$ is right-angled at $T$.
- For a point $P$ at distance $d$ from the centre of a circle of radius $r$, the length of the tangent is
$$PT = \sqrt{d^2 - r^2}$$
- The perpendicular to a tangent at its point of contact passes through the centre.
?eg A point $P$ is 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm. Find the length of the tangent from $P$ to the circle.
= $OT \perp PT$, so $\triangle OTP$ is right-angled at $T$ with $OP = 13$ cm and $OT = 5$ cm.
= $PT = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144}$
=> $PT = 12$ cm
?eg The length of a tangent from a point $A$ to a circle of radius 7 cm is 24 cm. Find the distance of $A$ from the centre.
= $OA^2 = OT^2 + AT^2 = 7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625$
=> $OA = 25$ cm
?eg Two concentric circles have radii 13 cm and 5 cm. Find the length of the chord of the larger circle that touches the smaller circle.
!fig w=300 h=240; P O 150 120 t; P A 70 180 bl; P B 230 180 br; P M 150 180 b; C O 100; C O 60; H A B; D O M; D O A; R O M B; cap Chord AB of the larger circle touches the smaller circle at M
= Let the chord $AB$ touch the smaller circle at $M$. Then $OM \perp AB$, and the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so $AM = MB$.
= $AM = \sqrt{OA^2 - OM^2} = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = 12$ cm
=> $AB = 2 \times 12 = 24$ cm
?eg Prove that the tangents drawn at the ends of a diameter of a circle are parallel.
= Let $AB$ be a diameter and let the tangents at $A$ and $B$ be $l$ and $m$.
= $OA \perp l$ and $OB \perp m$ (tangent is perpendicular to the radius), so both $l$ and $m$ are perpendicular to the line $AB$.
=> Two lines perpendicular to the same line are parallel, so $l \parallel m$.

# Theorem 2: tangents from an external point
- **Theorem**: the lengths of the tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
!fig w=340 h=220; P O 120 110 l; P P 290 110 r; P A 148.8 46.2 t; P B 148.8 173.8 b; C O 70; H P A; H P B; D O A; D O B; D O P; R O A P; R O B P; cap Tangents PA and PB from an external point P, each perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact
- If $PA$ and $PB$ are tangents from $P$ to a circle with centre $O$, then $PA = PB$. The proof uses $\triangle OAP \cong \triangle OBP$ by RHS.
- From the same congruence: $OP$ bisects $\angle APB$ and $\angle AOB$.
- $\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180°$, because the angles at $A$ and $B$ in quadrilateral $OAPB$ are both $90°$.
?eg $PA$ and $PB$ are tangents from $P$ to a circle with centre $O$, and $\angle APB = 50°$. Find $\angle AOB$ and $\angle OAB$.
= $\angle AOB = 180° - \angle APB = 180° - 50° = 130°$
= In $\triangle OAB$, $OA = OB$ (radii), so $\angle OAB = \angle OBA = \dfrac{180° - 130°}{2}$
=> $\angle AOB = 130°$ and $\angle OAB = 25°$
?eg A quadrilateral $ABCD$ is drawn to circumscribe a circle. If $AB = 8$ cm, $BC = 9$ cm and $CD = 6$ cm, find $AD$.
= Tangents from each vertex are equal, which gives $AB + CD = AD + BC$.
= $8 + 6 = AD + 9$
=> $AD = 5$ cm
?eg From an external point $P$, tangents $PA$ and $PB$ are drawn to a circle. A third tangent at a point $E$ of the circle meets $PA$ at $C$ and $PB$ at $D$. If $PA = 12$ cm, find the perimeter of $\triangle PCD$.
= $CA = CE$ and $DB = DE$ (tangents from an external point)
= Perimeter $= PC + CD + PD = PC + CE + ED + PD = (PC + CA) + (DB + PD) = PA + PB$
=> Perimeter $= 12 + 12 = 24$ cm
?eg The incircle of $\triangle ABC$ touches $BC$, $CA$ and $AB$ at $D$, $E$ and $F$. If $AB = 12$ cm, $BC = 8$ cm and $CA = 10$ cm, find $AF$, $BD$ and $CE$.
= Let $AF = AE = x$, $BD = BF = y$, $CD = CE = z$. Then $x + y = 12$, $y + z = 8$, $z + x = 10$.
= Adding: $2(x + y + z) = 30$, so $x + y + z = 15$.
=> $AF = x = 15 - 8 = 7$ cm, $BD = y = 15 - 10 = 5$ cm, $CE = z = 15 - 12 = 3$ cm

# Results worth remembering
- The centre of a circle lies on the bisector of the angle between two tangents drawn from an external point.
- A parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.
- Opposite sides of a quadrilateral circumscribing a circle subtend supplementary angles at the centre.
- In two concentric circles, a chord of the larger circle that touches the smaller circle is bisected at the point of contact.

# Common mistakes
- Forgetting to state the reason "tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact" when marking a right angle.
- Taking the hypotenuse wrongly: in $\triangle OTP$ the hypotenuse is $OP$, the line from the centre to the external point.
- Assuming $\angle APB = \angle AOB$. They add up to $180°$.
- Drawing a tangent from a point inside the circle. No tangent exists there.

@@formulas
Tangent and radius | Tangent $\perp$ radius at the point of contact
Tangents from an external point | $PA = PB$
Length of a tangent | $PT = \sqrt{d^2 - r^2}$, where $d$ is the distance of the point from the centre
Distance of the point from the centre | $d = \sqrt{PT^2 + r^2}$
Angle between two tangents | $\angle APB + \angle AOB = 180°$
Line from the point to the centre | $OP$ bisects $\angle APB$ and $\angle AOB$
Number of tangents from a point | Inside: 0;  on the circle: 1;  outside: 2
Quadrilateral circumscribing a circle | $AB + CD = AD + BC$
Chord touching the inner of two concentric circles | Length $= 2\sqrt{R^2 - r^2}$
Triangle with an incircle | $AF = AE = s - a$, $BD = BF = s - b$, $CD = CE = s - c$, where $s$ is the semi-perimeter
Radius of the incircle of a right-angled triangle | $r = \dfrac{a + b - c}{2}$, where $c$ is the hypotenuse

@@dpp
Q: How many tangents can a circle have? How many of them can be drawn from a point outside the circle?
A: Infinitely many; exactly 2 from an outside point
Q: A tangent $PQ$ at a point $P$ of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the centre $O$ at $Q$, with $OQ = 13$ cm. Find $PQ$.
A: 12 cm
Q: The length of a tangent from a point 10 cm from the centre of a circle is 8 cm. Find the radius.
A: 6 cm
Q: Two concentric circles have radii 10 cm and 6 cm. Find the length of the chord of the larger circle that touches the smaller circle.
A: 16 cm
Q: $PA$ and $PB$ are tangents to a circle with centre $O$. If $\angle APB = 70°$, find $\angle POA$.
A: $\angle AOB = 110°$, so $\angle POA = 55°$
Q: Tangents $PA$ and $PB$ from a point $P$ are inclined to each other at $60°$, and the radius of the circle is 5 cm. Find the length of each tangent.
A: $\angle APO = 30°$, so $PA = \dfrac{5}{\tan 30°} = 5\sqrt{3}$ cm
Q: A quadrilateral $ABCD$ circumscribes a circle. If $AB = 7$ cm, $BC = 10$ cm and $CD = 9$ cm, find $AD$.
A: 6 cm
Q: The incircle of $\triangle ABC$ touches $BC$, $CA$, $AB$ at $D$, $E$, $F$. If $AF = 4$ cm, $BD = 3$ cm and $CE = 5$ cm, find the perimeter of the triangle.
A: $2(4 + 3 + 5) = 24$ cm
Q: Prove that the perpendicular at the point of contact to a tangent of a circle passes through the centre.
A: The radius at the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent, and only one perpendicular can be drawn to a line at a given point, so the perpendicular is the line containing the radius.
Q: Prove that a parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.
A: $AB + CD = AD + BC$ and $AB = CD$, $AD = BC$ give $2AB = 2AD$, so all four sides are equal.
