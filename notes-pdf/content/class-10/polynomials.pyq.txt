T: Zeroes and coefficients of a quadratic polynomial
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] If the sum of zeroes of the polynomial $p(x) = 2x^2 - k\sqrt{2}\,x + 1$ is $\sqrt{2}$, then the value of $k$ is: (a) $\sqrt{2}$  (b) $2$  (c) $2\sqrt{2}$  (d) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
S: Sum of zeroes $= -\dfrac{b}{a} = \dfrac{k\sqrt{2}}{2}$
S: $\dfrac{k\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2}$ gives $k = 2$
S: **Answer: (b)**
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] The zeroes of a polynomial $x^2 + px + q$ are twice the zeroes of the polynomial $4x^2 - 5x - 6$. The value of $p$ is: (a) $-\dfrac{5}{2}$  (b) $\dfrac{5}{2}$  (c) $-5$  (d) $10$
S: Let the zeroes of $4x^2 - 5x - 6$ be $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Then $\alpha + \beta = \dfrac{5}{4}$.
S: The zeroes of $x^2 + px + q$ are $2\alpha$ and $2\beta$, with sum $2(\alpha + \beta) = \dfrac{5}{2}$.
S: Sum of zeroes $= -p$, so $p = -\dfrac{5}{2}$
S: **Answer: (a)**

T: Zeroes from the graph
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] Assertion (A): If the graph of a polynomial touches the $x$-axis at only one point, then the polynomial cannot be a quadratic polynomial. Reason (R): A polynomial of degree $n$ ($n > 1$) can have at most $n$ zeroes. Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A. (b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A. (c) A is true but R is false. (d) A is false but R is true.
S: A is false: the graph of $(x - 1)^2 = x^2 - 2x + 1$ touches the $x$-axis only at $x = 1$, and it is a quadratic polynomial.
S: R is true: a polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ zeroes.
S: **Answer: (d)**
