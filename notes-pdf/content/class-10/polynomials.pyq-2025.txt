T: Zeroes and coefficients of a quadratic polynomial
Q: [CBSE 2025, 1 mark] The zeroes $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of the polynomial $3x^2 + 6x + k$ satisfy $\alpha + \beta + \alpha\beta = -\dfrac{2}{3}$. Find $k$.
S: $\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{6}{3} = -2$ and $\alpha\beta = \dfrac{k}{3}$
S: $-2 + \dfrac{k}{3} = -\dfrac{2}{3}$, so $\dfrac{k}{3} = \dfrac{4}{3}$
S: **Answer: $k = 4$**
Q: [CBSE 2025, 2 marks] Find the zeroes of the polynomial $x^2 + \dfrac{4}{3}x - \dfrac{4}{3}$.
S: $x^2 + \dfrac{4}{3}x - \dfrac{4}{3} = \dfrac{1}{3}(3x^2 + 4x - 4)$
S: $3x^2 + 4x - 4 = 3x^2 + 6x - 2x - 4 = 3x(x + 2) - 2(x + 2) = (3x - 2)(x + 2)$
S: The polynomial is zero when $3x - 2 = 0$ or $x + 2 = 0$.
S: Check: sum $= \dfrac{2}{3} - 2 = -\dfrac{4}{3}$ and product $= \dfrac{2}{3} \times (-2) = -\dfrac{4}{3}$, which match the coefficients.
S: **Answer: $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $-2$**

T: Zeroes from the graph
Q: [CBSE 2025, 1 mark] The graphs of two quadratic polynomials are drawn on the same axes. One parabola opens upwards and the other opens downwards, and both cut the $x$-axis at the same two points. How many distinct zeroes do the two polynomials have between them?
S: The zeroes of a polynomial are the $x$-coordinates of the points where its graph meets the $x$-axis.
S: Both graphs meet the $x$-axis at the same two points, so they share the same two zeroes.
S: **Answer: 2**
