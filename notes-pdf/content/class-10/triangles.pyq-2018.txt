T: Right-angled triangles
Q: [CBSE 2018, 4 marks] $ABC$ is an equilateral triangle and $D$ is a point on $BC$ with $BD = \dfrac{1}{3}BC$. Prove that $9AD^2 = 7AB^2$.
!fig w=320 h=225; P A 160 20 t; P B 60 193.2 bl; P C 260 193.2 br; P D 126.7 193.2 b; P E 160 193.2 b; L A B; L B C; L C A; H A D; D A E; R A E C
S: Let each side be $a$. Draw $AE \perp BC$. In an equilateral triangle the altitude bisects the base, so $BE = \dfrac{a}{2}$.
S: $BD = \dfrac{a}{3}$, so $DE = BE - BD = \dfrac{a}{2} - \dfrac{a}{3} = \dfrac{a}{6}$.
S: In right triangle $AEB$: $AE^2 = a^2 - \dfrac{a^2}{4} = \dfrac{3a^2}{4}$
S: In right triangle $AED$: $AD^2 = AE^2 + DE^2 = \dfrac{3a^2}{4} + \dfrac{a^2}{36} = \dfrac{28a^2}{36} = \dfrac{7a^2}{9}$
S: **So $9AD^2 = 7a^2 = 7AB^2$. Proved.**
