T: Zeroes and coefficients of a quadratic polynomial
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] The zeroes of $p(x) = 2x^2 - k\sqrt{2}\,x + 1$ add up to $\sqrt{2}$. Find $k$. (a) $\sqrt{2}$  (b) $2$  (c) $2\sqrt{2}$  (d) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
S: Sum of zeroes $= -\dfrac{b}{a} = \dfrac{k\sqrt{2}}{2}$
S: $\dfrac{k\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2}$ gives $k = 2$
S: **Answer: (b)**
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] Each zero of $x^2 + px + q$ is double a zero of $4x^2 - 5x - 6$. Find $p$. (a) $-\dfrac{5}{2}$  (b) $\dfrac{5}{2}$  (c) $-5$  (d) $10$
S: Let the zeroes of $4x^2 - 5x - 6$ be $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Then $\alpha + \beta = \dfrac{5}{4}$.
S: The zeroes of $x^2 + px + q$ are $2\alpha$ and $2\beta$, with sum $2(\alpha + \beta) = \dfrac{5}{2}$.
S: Sum of zeroes $= -p$, so $p = -\dfrac{5}{2}$
S: **Answer: (a)**

T: Zeroes from the graph
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] Assertion (A): a polynomial whose graph touches the $x$-axis at exactly one point cannot be quadratic. Reason (R): a polynomial of degree $n$, with $n > 1$, has at most $n$ zeroes. Choose: (a) A and R are both true and R explains A. (b) A and R are both true but R does not explain A. (c) A is true and R is false. (d) A is false and R is true.
S: A is false: the graph of $(x - 1)^2 = x^2 - 2x + 1$ touches the $x$-axis only at $x = 1$, and it is a quadratic polynomial.
S: R is true: a polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ zeroes.
S: **Answer: (d)**
