@title Reflection
@summary Reflection gives the mirror image of a point or a figure in a line or in a point. This chapter covers reflection in the axes, in the origin and in lines parallel to the axes, and the idea of invariant points.

# Reflection in a line
- The **reflection (image)** of a point $P$ in a line $l$ is the point $P'$ such that $l$ is the perpendicular bisector of $PP'$.
- The line $l$ is the **mirror line** (line of reflection or axis of reflection).
- $P$ and $P'$ are at equal distances from the mirror line, on opposite sides of it.
- The notation $M_x$ means reflection in the $x$-axis, $M_y$ reflection in the $y$-axis and $M_o$ reflection in the origin.

# Reflection in the axes and in the origin
| Reflection in | Rule | What changes |
| The $x$-axis ($M_x$) | $(x, y) \rightarrow (x, -y)$ | Sign of the ordinate |
| The $y$-axis ($M_y$) | $(x, y) \rightarrow (-x, y)$ | Sign of the abscissa |
| The origin ($M_o$) | $(x, y) \rightarrow (-x, -y)$ | Signs of both |
?eg Find the reflection of $P(3, -4)$ in (i) the $x$-axis (ii) the $y$-axis (iii) the origin.
= (i) Change the sign of the ordinate: $(3, 4)$
= (ii) Change the sign of the abscissa: $(-3, -4)$
= (iii) Change both signs: $(-3, 4)$
=> (i) $(3, 4)$  (ii) $(-3, -4)$  (iii) $(-3, 4)$
?eg The point $P(2, 3)$ is reflected in the $x$-axis to $P'$, and $P'$ is reflected in the $y$-axis to $P''$. Find $P'$ and $P''$, and name a single transformation that maps $P$ to $P''$.
= $P' = (2, -3)$
= $P'' = (-2, -3)$
=> $P'' = (-2, -3)$, which is the reflection of $P$ in the origin.
!tip Reflection in the $x$-axis followed by reflection in the $y$-axis (in either order) is the same as reflection in the origin.

# Reflection in a line parallel to an axis
| Mirror line | Rule |
| $x = a$ (parallel to the $y$-axis) | $(x, y) \rightarrow (2a - x,\ y)$ |
| $y = b$ (parallel to the $x$-axis) | $(x, y) \rightarrow (x,\ 2b - y)$ |
- The mirror line passes through the mid-point of the segment joining a point and its image. This fact finds the mirror line when a point and its image are known.
?eg Find the reflection of $P(5, 3)$ in (i) the line $x = 2$ (ii) the line $y = -1$.
= (i) $(2 \times 2 - 5,\ 3) = (-1, 3)$
= (ii) $(5,\ 2 \times (-1) - 3) = (5, -5)$
=> (i) $(-1, 3)$  (ii) $(5, -5)$
?eg The image of $A(1, 2)$ in a line parallel to the $y$-axis is $A'(5, 2)$. Find the equation of the mirror line.
= The mirror line passes through the mid-point of $AA'$, which is $(3, 2)$, and is parallel to the $y$-axis.
=> The mirror line is $x = 3$.
?eg A point $P$ is reflected in the $x$-axis to $P'(4, -5)$. Find $P$, and the reflection of $P$ in the line $y = 2$.
= Reflection in the $x$-axis changes the sign of the ordinate, so $P = (4, 5)$.
= Reflection of $P$ in $y = 2$: $(4,\ 2 \times 2 - 5) = (4, -1)$
=> $P = (4, 5)$; its reflection in $y = 2$ is $(4, -1)$.

# Invariant points
- A point is **invariant** under a reflection if its image is the point itself.
- A point is invariant exactly when it lies **on the mirror line**.
| Reflection in | Invariant points |
| The $x$-axis | Points on the $x$-axis, $(x, 0)$ |
| The $y$-axis | Points on the $y$-axis, $(0, y)$ |
| The origin | Only the origin $(0, 0)$ |
| The line $x = a$ | Points with abscissa $a$ |
?eg The point $(k, 0)$ is invariant under reflection in a line $L$, and so is $(0, 0)$. Name the line $L$.
= Both points lie on the $x$-axis, and invariant points lie on the mirror line.
=> $L$ is the $x$-axis ($y = 0$).

# Reflection of figures
- To reflect a figure, reflect each of its vertices and join the images in the same order.
- Reflection keeps lengths and angles unchanged, so a figure and its image are **congruent**. The orientation is reversed, as in a mirror.
- A figure together with its reflection is symmetrical about the mirror line.
?eg $P(3, 4)$ is reflected in the $x$-axis to $Q$; $Q$ is reflected in the $y$-axis to $R$; and $P$ is reflected in the $y$-axis to $S$. Write the coordinates of $Q$, $R$ and $S$, name the figure $PQRS$ and find its area.
= $Q = (3, -4)$, $R = (-3, -4)$, $S = (-3, 4)$
= $PQ = 8$ units (vertical) and $QR = 6$ units (horizontal), with right angles at each vertex.
=> $PQRS$ is a rectangle with area $8 \times 6 = 48$ square units.

# Graph questions: how to answer
- Use the scale given, plot each point carefully and label it with its coordinates.
- Write the coordinates of every image asked for.
- Name the closed figure formed using the geometric name (kite, arrowhead, rectangle, isosceles trapezium, and so on).
- If asked, state the line of symmetry by its equation, such as $x = 0$ for the $y$-axis.

# Common mistakes
- Changing the wrong sign: reflection in the $x$-axis changes $y$, not $x$.
- Using $(a - x, y)$ for reflection in $x = a$. The rule is $(2a - x, y)$.
- Calling a point invariant when it is not on the mirror line.
- Joining the image points in a different order from the original figure.
