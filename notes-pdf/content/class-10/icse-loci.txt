@title Loci (Locus and its Constructions)
@summary A locus is the path traced by a point that moves so as to satisfy a given condition. This chapter covers the standard loci, the two locus theorems and the constructions based on them.

# What a locus is
- The **locus** of a point is the set of all points, and only those points, that satisfy a given condition. The plural is **loci**.
- To prove that a line or curve is a locus, two things must be shown: (i) every point on it satisfies the condition, and (ii) every point that satisfies the condition lies on it.
- A locus is usually a line, a pair of lines or a circle.

# The two locus theorems
- **Theorem 1**: the locus of a point equidistant from two given points is the **perpendicular bisector** of the line segment joining them.
- **Theorem 2**: the locus of a point equidistant from two intersecting lines is the pair of **bisectors of the angles** between the lines.
- Converses: every point on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ is equidistant from $A$ and $B$; every point on the bisector of an angle is equidistant from the arms of the angle.
?eg $P$ is a point on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. If $PA = 5$ cm, find $PB$. Give a reason.
= Every point on the perpendicular bisector of a segment is equidistant from its end points.
=> $PB = 5$ cm

# Standard loci
| Condition on the moving point | Locus |
| Equidistant from two fixed points $A$ and $B$ | Perpendicular bisector of $AB$ |
| Equidistant from two intersecting lines | The pair of bisectors of the angles between them |
| Equidistant from the two arms of an angle | The bisector of the angle |
| At a fixed distance $r$ from a fixed point $O$ | Circle with centre $O$ and radius $r$ |
| At a fixed distance $d$ from a given line | A pair of lines parallel to it, one on each side, at distance $d$ |
| Equidistant from two parallel lines | The line parallel to both and midway between them |
| Equidistant from two concentric circles | The concentric circle midway between them |
| Mid-points of equal chords of a circle | A concentric circle |
| Centres of circles touching a given line at a given point | The line perpendicular to the given line at that point |
?eg Describe the locus of (i) the centre of a wheel of radius 30 cm rolling along a straight road (ii) the tip of the minute hand of a clock.
= (i) The centre is always 30 cm above the road.
= (ii) The tip is always at a fixed distance (the length of the hand) from the centre of the clock.
=> (i) A line parallel to the road, 30 cm above it. (ii) A circle whose centre is the centre of the clock and whose radius is the length of the minute hand.
?eg Describe the locus of points that are 3 cm from a fixed line $AB$.
= A point at a fixed distance from a line can lie on either side of it.
=> A pair of lines parallel to $AB$, one on each side, each at a distance of 3 cm from it.

# Loci in a triangle
- The point equidistant from the three **vertices** of a triangle is the **circumcentre**. It is where the perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet.
- The point equidistant from the three **sides** of a triangle is the **incentre**. It is where the bisectors of the angles meet.
?eg In $\triangle ABC$, a point $P$ is equidistant from $A$ and $B$ and also equidistant from $AB$ and $BC$. How is $P$ located?
= Equidistant from $A$ and $B$: $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$.
= Equidistant from $AB$ and $BC$: $P$ lies on the bisector of $\angle ABC$.
=> $P$ is the point where the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ meets the bisector of $\angle ABC$.

# Construction questions
- Construct each locus separately, using ruler and compasses only, and leave the construction arcs visible.
- A point that satisfies two conditions is the **intersection** of the two loci.
- Measure and record whatever the question asks for at the end.
?eg Construct $\triangle ABC$ with $AB = 6$ cm, $BC = 5$ cm and $\angle ABC = 60°$. Locate the point $P$ inside the triangle that is equidistant from $B$ and $C$ and also equidistant from $AB$ and $BC$. Write the steps.
= Step 1: draw $BC = 5$ cm. At $B$ construct an angle of $60°$ and cut off $BA = 6$ cm. Join $AC$.
= Step 2: draw the perpendicular bisector of $BC$. This is the locus of points equidistant from $B$ and $C$.
= Step 3: draw the bisector of $\angle ABC$. This is the locus of points equidistant from $AB$ and $BC$.
=> Step 4: the point where the two loci meet is the required point $P$. Measure $PB$ if asked.
?eg Points $A$ and $B$ are 6 cm apart. Describe how to find the points that are 4 cm from $A$ and equidistant from $A$ and $B$.
= Locus 1: the circle with centre $A$ and radius 4 cm.
= Locus 2: the perpendicular bisector of $AB$.
=> The circle and the bisector meet at two points; these are the required points.

# Common mistakes
- Giving one parallel line instead of a **pair** for points at a fixed distance from a line.
- Giving one angle bisector instead of the **pair** for two intersecting lines.
- Confusing the circumcentre (perpendicular bisectors of sides) with the incentre (angle bisectors).
- Rubbing out the construction arcs.

@@formulas
Equidistant from two points | Perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them
Equidistant from two intersecting lines | The pair of angle bisectors
Fixed distance from a point | Circle with that point as centre
Fixed distance from a line | Pair of parallel lines, one on each side
Equidistant from two parallel lines | The parallel line midway between them
Circumcentre | Meeting point of the perpendicular bisectors of the sides; equidistant from the vertices
Incentre | Meeting point of the angle bisectors; equidistant from the sides
Point satisfying two conditions | Intersection of the two loci
Centre of a circle through two points | Lies on the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them
Centre of a circle touching two intersecting lines | Lies on a bisector of the angle between them

Q: State the locus of a point that moves so that it is always 5 cm from a fixed point $O$.
A: The circle with centre $O$ and radius 5 cm
Q: State the locus of a point equidistant from two fixed points $P$ and $Q$.
A: The perpendicular bisector of $PQ$
Q: State the locus of a point equidistant from two parallel lines 8 cm apart.
A: The line parallel to both, 4 cm from each
Q: State the locus of the centres of all circles that pass through two fixed points $A$ and $B$.
A: The perpendicular bisector of $AB$
Q: State the locus of a point inside $\angle ABC$ that is equidistant from $BA$ and $BC$.
A: The bisector of $\angle ABC$
Q: State the locus of the centre of a coin of radius 1 cm that rolls along the inside edge of a circular tray of radius 10 cm.
A: A circle of radius 9 cm concentric with the tray
Q: $Q$ lies on the bisector of $\angle ABC$. The perpendicular distance from $Q$ to $AB$ is 3.2 cm. Find its perpendicular distance from $BC$.
A: 3.2 cm
Q: Name the point that is equidistant from the three sides of a triangle, and say how it is constructed.
A: The incentre; the meeting point of the bisectors of any two angles
Q: Write the steps to locate a point equidistant from the three vertices of $\triangle ABC$.
A: Draw the perpendicular bisectors of any two sides; their meeting point is the circumcentre.
Q: $AB = 7$ cm. Describe how to find the points that are 5 cm from $B$ and 2 cm from the line $AB$.
A: Draw the circle with centre $B$ and radius 5 cm, and the two lines parallel to $AB$ at 2 cm; they meet in four points.
