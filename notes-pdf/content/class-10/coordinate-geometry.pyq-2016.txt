T: The distance formula
Q: [CBSE 2016, 2 marks] Show that $(3, 0)$, $(6, 4)$ and $(-1, 3)$ are the vertices of a right-angled isosceles triangle.
S: Call the points $A(3, 0)$, $B(6, 4)$ and $C(-1, 3)$.
S: $AB^2 = 3^2 + 4^2 = 25$, $AC^2 = (-4)^2 + 3^2 = 25$ and $BC^2 = (-7)^2 + (-1)^2 = 50$
S: $AB = AC$, so the triangle is isosceles. $AB^2 + AC^2 = 50 = BC^2$, so by the converse of the Pythagoras theorem $\angle A = 90°$.
S: **So the triangle is right-angled and isosceles. Proved.**
Q: [CBSE 2016, 3 marks] The point $P(x, y)$ is equidistant from $A(a + b, b - a)$ and $B(a - b, a + b)$. Prove that $bx = ay$.
S: $PA^2 = PB^2$: $(x - a - b)^2 + (y - b + a)^2 = (x - a + b)^2 + (y - a - b)^2$
S: Use $p^2 - q^2 = (p - q)(p + q)$ on the $x$ terms: $(x - a - b)^2 - (x - a + b)^2 = (-2b)(2x - 2a) = -4bx + 4ab$
S: On the $y$ terms: $(y - b + a)^2 - (y - a - b)^2 = (2a)(2y - 2b) = 4ay - 4ab$
S: Adding the two results gives 0: $-4bx + 4ab + 4ay - 4ab = 0$, so $4ay = 4bx$.
S: **So $bx = ay$. Proved.**

T: The section formula
Q: [CBSE 2016, 2 marks] $P$ and $Q$ are the points of trisection of the segment joining $A(2, -2)$ and $B(-7, 4)$, with $P$ nearer to $A$. Find $P$ and $Q$.
S: $P$ divides $AB$ in the ratio $1 : 2$: $P = \left(\dfrac{-7 + 4}{3}, \dfrac{4 - 4}{3}\right) = (-1, 0)$
S: $Q$ divides $AB$ in the ratio $2 : 1$: $Q = \left(\dfrac{-14 + 2}{3}, \dfrac{8 - 2}{3}\right) = (-4, 2)$
S: **Answer: $P(-1, 0)$ and $Q(-4, 2)$**
