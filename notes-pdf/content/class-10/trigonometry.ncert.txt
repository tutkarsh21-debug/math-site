Q: [Q1] In $\triangle ABC$, right-angled at $B$, $AB = 24$ cm and $BC = 7$ cm. Find (i) $\sin A$, $\cos A$ (ii) $\sin C$, $\cos C$.
S: $AC = \sqrt{24^2 + 7^2} = \sqrt{625} = 25$ cm.
S: (i) For angle $A$ the opposite side is $BC$ and the adjacent side is $AB$: $\sin A = \dfrac{7}{25}$, $\cos A = \dfrac{24}{25}$.
S: (ii) For angle $C$ the opposite side is $AB$ and the adjacent side is $BC$: $\sin C = \dfrac{24}{25}$, $\cos C = \dfrac{7}{25}$.
S: **Answer: (i) $\sin A = \dfrac{7}{25}$, $\cos A = \dfrac{24}{25}$ (ii) $\sin C = \dfrac{24}{25}$, $\cos C = \dfrac{7}{25}$**

Q: [Q3] If $\sin A = \dfrac{3}{4}$, find $\cos A$ and $\tan A$.
S: Take the opposite side as $3k$ and the hypotenuse as $4k$. The adjacent side is $\sqrt{16k^2 - 9k^2} = \sqrt{7}\,k$.

Q: [Q4] Given $15 \cot A = 8$, find $\sin A$ and $\sec A$.
S: $\cot A = \dfrac{8}{15}$, so take the adjacent side as $8k$ and the opposite side as $15k$. The hypotenuse is $\sqrt{64k^2 + 225k^2} = 17k$.
S: **Answer: $\sin A = \dfrac{15}{17}$ and $\sec A = \dfrac{17}{8}$**
Q: [Q5] Given $\sec\theta = \dfrac{13}{12}$, find all the other trigonometric ratios.
S: Take the hypotenuse as $13k$ and the adjacent side as $12k$. The opposite side is $\sqrt{169k^2 - 144k^2} = 5k$.
S: **Answer: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{5}{13}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{12}{13}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{5}{12}$, $\text{cosec}\,\theta = \dfrac{13}{5}$, $\cot\theta = \dfrac{12}{5}$**
Q: [Q6] $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are acute angles such that $\cos A = \cos B$. Show that $\angle A = \angle B$.
S: Take a triangle $ABC$ right-angled at $C$, so that $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are its acute angles. Then $\cos A = \dfrac{AC}{AB}$ and $\cos B = \dfrac{BC}{AB}$.
S: $\cos A = \cos B$ gives $AC = BC$.
S: Angles opposite equal sides of a triangle are equal, so $\angle B = \angle A$.
S: **Hence $\angle A = \angle B$.**

Q: [Q9] In $\triangle ABC$, right-angled at $B$, $\tan A = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. Find (i) $\sin A \cos C + \cos A \sin C$ (ii) $\cos A \cos C - \sin A \sin C$.

Q: [Q10] In $\triangle PQR$, right-angled at $Q$, $PR + QR = 25$ cm and $PQ = 5$ cm. Find $\sin P$, $\cos P$ and $\tan P$.
S: Let $QR = x$ cm, so $PR = (25 - x)$ cm. By the Pythagoras theorem, $(25 - x)^2 = 5^2 + x^2$.
S: $625 - 50x = 25$ gives $x = 12$. So $QR = 12$ cm and $PR = 13$ cm.
S: **Answer: $\sin P = \dfrac{12}{13}$, $\cos P = \dfrac{5}{13}$, $\tan P = \dfrac{12}{5}$**
Q: [Q11] State whether each is true or false, with a reason. (i) The value of $\tan A$ is always less than 1. (ii) $\sec A = \dfrac{12}{5}$ for some value of angle $A$. (iii) $\cos A$ is the abbreviation for the cosecant of angle $A$. (iv) $\cot A$ is the product of cot and $A$. (v) $\sin\theta = \dfrac{4}{3}$ for some angle $\theta$.
S: (i) False. For example, $\tan 60° = \sqrt{3} > 1$.
S: (ii) True. $\sec A$ is the hypotenuse divided by the adjacent side, which can be any number from 1 upwards, and $\dfrac{12}{5} > 1$.
S: (iii) False. $\cos A$ stands for the cosine of $A$. The cosecant is written $\text{cosec}\,A$.
S: (iv) False. $\cot A$ is one symbol, the cotangent of the angle $A$. "cot" alone has no meaning.
S: (v) False. $\sin\theta$ is the opposite side divided by the hypotenuse, and the hypotenuse is the longest side, so $\sin\theta$ cannot be more than 1.
S: **Answer: (i) False (ii) True (iii) False (iv) False (v) False**

S: (v) $\dfrac{5 \times \frac{1}{4} + 4 \times \frac{4}{3} - 1}{\frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{4}} = \dfrac{15 + 64 - 12}{12} = \dfrac{67}{12}$.
S: **Answer: (i) 1 (ii) 2 (iii) $\dfrac{3\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{8}$ (iv) $\dfrac{43 - 24\sqrt{3}}{11}$ (v) $\dfrac{67}{12}$**

Q: [Q1] Express $\sin A$, $\sec A$ and $\tan A$ in terms of $\cot A$.
S: $\text{cosec}^2 A = 1 + \cot^2 A$, so $\sin A = \dfrac{1}{\text{cosec}\,A} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \cot^2 A}}$.
S: $\tan A = \dfrac{1}{\cot A}$.
S: $\sec^2 A = 1 + \tan^2 A = 1 + \dfrac{1}{\cot^2 A}$, so $\sec A = \dfrac{\sqrt{1 + \cot^2 A}}{\cot A}$.
S: **Answer: $\sin A = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \cot^2 A}}$, $\sec A = \dfrac{\sqrt{1 + \cot^2 A}}{\cot A}$, $\tan A = \dfrac{1}{\cot A}$**
Q: [Q2] Write all the other trigonometric ratios of $\angle A$ in terms of $\sec A$.
S: $\cos A = \dfrac{1}{\sec A}$.
S: $\tan^2 A = \sec^2 A - 1$, so $\tan A = \sqrt{\sec^2 A - 1}$ and $\cot A = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\sec^2 A - 1}}$.
S: $\sin A = \tan A \times \cos A = \dfrac{\sqrt{\sec^2 A - 1}}{\sec A}$ and $\text{cosec}\,A = \dfrac{\sec A}{\sqrt{\sec^2 A - 1}}$.
S: **Answer: $\cos A = \dfrac{1}{\sec A}$, $\sin A = \dfrac{\sqrt{\sec^2 A - 1}}{\sec A}$, $\tan A = \sqrt{\sec^2 A - 1}$, $\cot A = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\sec^2 A - 1}}$, $\text{cosec}\,A = \dfrac{\sec A}{\sqrt{\sec^2 A - 1}}$**

Q: [Q4 (i)] Prove that $(\text{cosec}\,\theta - \cot\theta)^2 = \dfrac{1 - \cos\theta}{1 + \cos\theta}$.
S: LHS $= \left(\dfrac{1 - \cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\right)^2 = \dfrac{(1 - \cos\theta)^2}{1 - \cos^2\theta} = \dfrac{(1 - \cos\theta)^2}{(1 - \cos\theta)(1 + \cos\theta)}$
S: **$= \dfrac{1 - \cos\theta}{1 + \cos\theta} =$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (ii)] Prove that $\dfrac{\cos A}{1 + \sin A} + \dfrac{1 + \sin A}{\cos A} = 2\sec A$.
S: LHS $= \dfrac{\cos^2 A + (1 + \sin A)^2}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A} = \dfrac{\cos^2 A + \sin^2 A + 1 + 2\sin A}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A} = \dfrac{2(1 + \sin A)}{(1 + \sin A)\cos A}$
S: **$= \dfrac{2}{\cos A} = 2\sec A =$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (iii)] Prove that $\dfrac{\tan\theta}{1 - \cot\theta} + \dfrac{\cot\theta}{1 - \tan\theta} = 1 + \sec\theta\,\text{cosec}\,\theta$.
S: Write everything with $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$: LHS $= \dfrac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos\theta\,(\sin\theta - \cos\theta)} - \dfrac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta\,(\sin\theta - \cos\theta)} = \dfrac{\sin^3\theta - \cos^3\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta\,(\sin\theta - \cos\theta)}$
S: Using $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$: $= \dfrac{\sin^2\theta + \sin\theta\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} = \dfrac{1 + \sin\theta\cos\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}$
S: **$= \sec\theta\,\text{cosec}\,\theta + 1 =$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (iv)] Prove that $\dfrac{1 + \sec A}{\sec A} = \dfrac{\sin^2 A}{1 - \cos A}$.
S: LHS $= \dfrac{1}{\sec A} + 1 = \cos A + 1$.
S: RHS $= \dfrac{1 - \cos^2 A}{1 - \cos A} = \dfrac{(1 - \cos A)(1 + \cos A)}{1 - \cos A} = 1 + \cos A$.
S: **LHS $=$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (v)] Prove that $\dfrac{\cos A - \sin A + 1}{\cos A + \sin A - 1} = \text{cosec}\,A + \cot A$, using $\text{cosec}^2 A = 1 + \cot^2 A$.
S: Divide the numerator and the denominator by $\sin A$: LHS $= \dfrac{\cot A - 1 + \text{cosec}\,A}{\cot A + 1 - \text{cosec}\,A}$.
S: In the numerator replace 1 by $\text{cosec}^2 A - \cot^2 A$: $(\cot A + \text{cosec}\,A) - (\text{cosec}\,A - \cot A)(\text{cosec}\,A + \cot A) = (\cot A + \text{cosec}\,A)(1 - \text{cosec}\,A + \cot A)$.
S: **So LHS $= \dfrac{(\cot A + \text{cosec}\,A)(\cot A + 1 - \text{cosec}\,A)}{\cot A + 1 - \text{cosec}\,A} = \text{cosec}\,A + \cot A =$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (vi)] Prove that $\sqrt{\dfrac{1 + \sin A}{1 - \sin A}} = \sec A + \tan A$.
S: Multiply the numerator and the denominator inside the root by $(1 + \sin A)$: LHS $= \sqrt{\dfrac{(1 + \sin A)^2}{1 - \sin^2 A}} = \dfrac{1 + \sin A}{\cos A}$
S: **$= \dfrac{1}{\cos A} + \dfrac{\sin A}{\cos A} = \sec A + \tan A =$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (vii)] Prove that $\dfrac{\sin\theta - 2\sin^3\theta}{2\cos^3\theta - \cos\theta} = \tan\theta$.
S: LHS $= \dfrac{\sin\theta\,(1 - 2\sin^2\theta)}{\cos\theta\,(2\cos^2\theta - 1)}$.
S: Using $1 = \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta$: $1 - 2\sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta$ and $2\cos^2\theta - 1 = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta$.
S: **So LHS $= \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \tan\theta =$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (viii)] Prove that $(\sin A + \text{cosec}\,A)^2 + (\cos A + \sec A)^2 = 7 + \tan^2 A + \cot^2 A$.
S: LHS $= \sin^2 A + \text{cosec}^2 A + 2 + \cos^2 A + \sec^2 A + 2$, since $\sin A \times \text{cosec}\,A = 1$ and $\cos A \times \sec A = 1$.
S: $= (\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A) + 4 + (1 + \cot^2 A) + (1 + \tan^2 A)$
S: **$= 7 + \tan^2 A + \cot^2 A =$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (ix)] Prove that $(\text{cosec}\,A - \sin A)(\sec A - \cos A) = \dfrac{1}{\tan A + \cot A}$.
S: LHS $= \dfrac{1 - \sin^2 A}{\sin A} \times \dfrac{1 - \cos^2 A}{\cos A} = \dfrac{\cos^2 A \sin^2 A}{\sin A \cos A} = \sin A \cos A$.
S: RHS $= \dfrac{1}{\frac{\sin A}{\cos A} + \frac{\cos A}{\sin A}} = \dfrac{\sin A \cos A}{\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A} = \sin A \cos A$.
S: **LHS $=$ RHS**
Q: [Q4 (x)] Prove that $\dfrac{1 + \tan^2 A}{1 + \cot^2 A} = \left(\dfrac{1 - \tan A}{1 - \cot A}\right)^2 = \tan^2 A$.
S: First expression: $\dfrac{\sec^2 A}{\text{cosec}^2 A} = \dfrac{\sin^2 A}{\cos^2 A} = \tan^2 A$.
S: Second expression: $1 - \cot A = \dfrac{\tan A - 1}{\tan A}$, so $\dfrac{1 - \tan A}{1 - \cot A} = \dfrac{(1 - \tan A)\tan A}{\tan A - 1} = -\tan A$, and its square is $\tan^2 A$.
S: **Both expressions equal $\tan^2 A$.**
