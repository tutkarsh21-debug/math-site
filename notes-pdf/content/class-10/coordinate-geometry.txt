# Coordinates: a quick recap
- A point in the plane is written $(x, y)$. $x$ is the **abscissa** (distance from the $y$-axis) and $y$ is the **ordinate** (distance from the $x$-axis).
- A point on the $x$-axis has the form $(x, 0)$. A point on the $y$-axis has the form $(0, y)$. The origin is $(0, 0)$.
- Signs in the quadrants: I $(+, +)$, II $(-, +)$, III $(-, -)$, IV $(+, -)$.

# The distance formula
- The distance between $P(x_1, y_1)$ and $Q(x_2, y_2)$ is
$$PQ = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$
- The distance of $P(x, y)$ from the origin is $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$.
- The formula comes from the Pythagoras theorem: the horizontal gap is $x_2 - x_1$ and the vertical gap is $y_2 - y_1$.
?eg Find the distance between $A(3, -4)$ and $B(-1, -1)$.
= $AB = \sqrt{(-1 - 3)^2 + (-1 - (-4))^2} = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9}$
=> $AB = 5$ units
?eg Find the values of $k$ if the distance between $A(3, k)$ and $B(-1, 2)$ is 5 units.
= $\sqrt{(-1 - 3)^2 + (2 - k)^2} = 5$, so $16 + (2 - k)^2 = 25$
= $(2 - k)^2 = 9$, so $2 - k = 3$ or $2 - k = -3$
=> $k = -1$ or $k = 5$
?eg Find the point on the $x$-axis that is equidistant from $A(3, 4)$ and $B(7, 6)$.
= Let the point be $P(x, 0)$. Then $PA^2 = PB^2$: $(x - 3)^2 + 16 = (x - 7)^2 + 36$
= $x^2 - 6x + 25 = x^2 - 14x + 85$, so $8x = 60$ and $x = 7.5$
=> The point is $(7.5, 0)$.
!tip When two distances are equal, equate their **squares**. This removes the square roots at once.

# Using distances to identify figures
- **Collinear points**: three points $A$, $B$, $C$ lie on one line if the sum of two of the distances $AB$, $BC$, $CA$ equals the third.
- **Triangle**: isosceles if two sides are equal; equilateral if all three are equal; right-angled if the squares of two sides add up to the square of the third.
| Quadrilateral | What to show |
| Parallelogram | Opposite sides are equal |
| Rectangle | Opposite sides are equal and the diagonals are equal |
| Rhombus | All four sides are equal |
| Square | All four sides are equal and the diagonals are equal |
?eg Show that the points $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 5)$ and $C(7, 9)$ are collinear.
= $AB = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, $BC = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, $AC = \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10$
= $AB + BC = 5 + 5 = 10 = AC$
=> The points are collinear.
?eg Show that $A(1, 2)$, $B(4, 6)$ and $C(8, 3)$ are the vertices of an isosceles right-angled triangle.
= $AB^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$, $BC^2 = 16 + 9 = 25$, $AC^2 = 49 + 1 = 50$
= $AB = BC$, so the triangle is isosceles.
= $AB^2 + BC^2 = 50 = AC^2$, so by the converse of the Pythagoras theorem the angle at $B$ is $90°$.
=> $\triangle ABC$ is an isosceles right-angled triangle, right-angled at $B$.

# The section formula
- The point $P$ that divides the line segment joining $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ **internally** in the ratio $m_1 : m_2$ is
$$P = \left(\frac{m_1x_2 + m_2x_1}{m_1 + m_2},\ \frac{m_1y_2 + m_2y_1}{m_1 + m_2}\right)$$
- $m_1$ goes with the **far** point: $m_1$ multiplies $(x_2, y_2)$ and $m_2$ multiplies $(x_1, y_1)$.
- **Mid-point** (ratio $1 : 1$):
$$M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$$
- To **find the ratio** in which a point divides a segment, take the ratio as $k : 1$.
?eg Find the point that divides the segment joining $A(1, -2)$ and $B(6, 8)$ in the ratio $2 : 3$.
= $x = \dfrac{2 \times 6 + 3 \times 1}{2 + 3} = \dfrac{15}{5} = 3$
= $y = \dfrac{2 \times 8 + 3 \times (-2)}{2 + 3} = \dfrac{10}{5} = 2$
=> The point is $(3, 2)$.
?eg In what ratio does the $x$-axis divide the segment joining $A(2, -3)$ and $B(5, 6)$? Find the point of division.
= Let the ratio be $k : 1$. On the $x$-axis the $y$-coordinate is 0: $\dfrac{6k - 3}{k + 1} = 0$, so $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
= The ratio is $1 : 2$. Then $x = \dfrac{1 \times 5 + 2 \times 2}{1 + 2} = 3$.
=> Ratio $1 : 2$; the point is $(3, 0)$.
?eg Find the points of trisection of the segment joining $A(1, 2)$ and $B(7, 11)$.
= The points of trisection divide $AB$ in the ratios $1 : 2$ and $2 : 1$.
= $P = \left(\dfrac{7 + 2}{3}, \dfrac{11 + 4}{3}\right) = (3, 5)$ and $Q = \left(\dfrac{14 + 1}{3}, \dfrac{22 + 2}{3}\right) = (5, 8)$
=> The points are $(3, 5)$ and $(5, 8)$.
?eg Three vertices of a parallelogram $ABCD$, taken in order, are $A(1, 2)$, $B(4, 3)$ and $C(6, 6)$. Find $D$.
= The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so $AC$ and $BD$ have the same mid-point.
= Mid-point of $AC = \left(\dfrac{7}{2}, 4\right)$. Let $D = (x, y)$: $\dfrac{4 + x}{2} = \dfrac{7}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3 + y}{2} = 4$.
=> $D = (3, 5)$

# Common mistakes
- Subtracting in different orders for $x$ and $y$. Use $x_2 - x_1$ and $y_2 - y_1$, with the same point first each time.
- Sign errors with negative coordinates: $-1 - (-4) = 3$, not $-5$.
- Pairing $m_1$ with $(x_1, y_1)$ in the section formula.
- Forgetting to write "units" after a distance.
- Claiming a square after showing only four equal sides. That shows a rhombus; the diagonals must also be equal.

@@formulas
Distance between two points | $\sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$
Distance from the origin | $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$
Section formula (internal, $m_1 : m_2$) | $\left(\dfrac{m_1x_2 + m_2x_1}{m_1 + m_2},\ \dfrac{m_1y_2 + m_2y_1}{m_1 + m_2}\right)$
Section formula (ratio $k : 1$) | $\left(\dfrac{kx_2 + x_1}{k + 1},\ \dfrac{ky_2 + y_1}{k + 1}\right)$
Mid-point | $\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$
Centroid of a triangle | $\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3},\ \dfrac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3}\right)$
Point on the x-axis | $(x, 0)$
Point on the y-axis | $(0, y)$
Collinear points | $AB + BC = AC$ for some order of the three points
Parallelogram | Diagonals have the same mid-point
Points of trisection | Divide the segment in the ratios $1 : 2$ and $2 : 1$
