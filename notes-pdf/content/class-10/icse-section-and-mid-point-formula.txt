@title Section and Mid-Point Formula
@summary These formulas give the coordinates of a point that divides a line segment in a given ratio. The mid-point and the centroid of a triangle are special cases.

# The section formula
- Let $P(x, y)$ divide the line segment joining $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ in the ratio $m_1 : m_2$, so that $AP : PB = m_1 : m_2$. Then
$$x = \frac{m_1x_2 + m_2x_1}{m_1 + m_2} \qquad y = \frac{m_1y_2 + m_2y_1}{m_1 + m_2}$$
- Each part of the ratio multiplies the coordinates of the **opposite** end: $m_1$ goes with $B$ and $m_2$ goes with $A$.
?eg Find the coordinates of the point that divides the segment joining $A(2, -1)$ and $B(7, 9)$ in the ratio $3 : 2$.
= $x = \dfrac{3 \times 7 + 2 \times 2}{3 + 2} = \dfrac{25}{5} = 5$
= $y = \dfrac{3 \times 9 + 2 \times (-1)}{3 + 2} = \dfrac{25}{5} = 5$
=> The point is $(5, 5)$.

# Finding the ratio
- To find the ratio in which a point divides a segment, take the ratio as $k : 1$ and use one coordinate to find $k$.
- A point on the $x$-axis has ordinate 0. A point on the $y$-axis has abscissa 0.
- If $k$ comes out negative, the point divides the segment externally.
?eg In what ratio does the point $P(3, 4)$ divide the segment joining $A(1, 2)$ and $B(6, 7)$?
= Let the ratio be $k : 1$. Then $\dfrac{6k + 1}{k + 1} = 3$, so $6k + 1 = 3k + 3$ and $k = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
= Check with $y$: $\dfrac{7k + 2}{k + 1} = \dfrac{\frac{14}{3} + 2}{\frac{5}{3}} = 4$. Correct.
=> The ratio is $2 : 3$.
?eg In what ratio does the $y$-axis divide the segment joining $A(-4, 5)$ and $B(6, 0)$? Find the point of intersection.
= Let the ratio be $k : 1$. On the $y$-axis the abscissa is 0: $\dfrac{6k - 4}{k + 1} = 0$, so $k = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
= The ratio is $2 : 3$. Ordinate $= \dfrac{2 \times 0 + 3 \times 5}{2 + 3} = 3$.
=> Ratio $2 : 3$; the point is $(0, 3)$.

# Points of trisection
- The points of trisection divide a segment into three equal parts. They divide it in the ratios $1 : 2$ and $2 : 1$.
?eg Find the points of trisection of the segment joining $A(2, 1)$ and $B(8, 10)$.
= Ratio $1 : 2$: $P = \left(\dfrac{8 + 4}{3}, \dfrac{10 + 2}{3}\right) = (4, 4)$
= Ratio $2 : 1$: $Q = \left(\dfrac{16 + 2}{3}, \dfrac{20 + 1}{3}\right) = (6, 7)$
=> The points are $(4, 4)$ and $(6, 7)$.

# The mid-point formula
- The mid-point of the segment joining $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is
$$\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$$
- If one end and the mid-point are known, the other end can be found.
- The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so they have the same mid-point.
?eg The mid-point of the segment joining $A(3, -2)$ and $B$ is $M(1, 4)$. Find $B$.
= Let $B = (x, y)$. Then $\dfrac{3 + x}{2} = 1$ and $\dfrac{-2 + y}{2} = 4$.
=> $B = (-1, 10)$
?eg $A(2, 3)$, $B(6, 5)$ and $C(8, 9)$ are three vertices of a parallelogram $ABCD$. Find $D$.
= Mid-point of $AC = (5, 6)$. This is also the mid-point of $BD$.
= Let $D = (x, y)$: $\dfrac{6 + x}{2} = 5$ and $\dfrac{5 + y}{2} = 6$
=> $D = (4, 7)$
?eg $AB$ is a diameter of a circle with centre $C(2, -3)$. If $A = (5, 1)$, find $B$.
= The centre is the mid-point of the diameter: $\dfrac{5 + x}{2} = 2$ and $\dfrac{1 + y}{2} = -3$.
=> $B = (-1, -7)$

# Centroid of a triangle
- A **median** joins a vertex to the mid-point of the opposite side. The three medians meet at the **centroid** $G$.
- The centroid divides each median in the ratio $2 : 1$, measured from the vertex.
$$G = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3}\right)$$
?eg Find the centroid of the triangle with vertices $A(1, 4)$, $B(-2, -1)$ and $C(7, 3)$.
= $G = \left(\dfrac{1 - 2 + 7}{3},\ \dfrac{4 - 1 + 3}{3}\right)$
=> $G = (2, 2)$
?eg The centroid of a triangle is $(1, 3)$ and two of its vertices are $(2, 5)$ and $(-4, 1)$. Find the third vertex.
= $\dfrac{2 - 4 + x}{3} = 1$ gives $x = 5$, and $\dfrac{5 + 1 + y}{3} = 3$ gives $y = 3$.
=> The third vertex is $(5, 3)$.

# Common mistakes
- Multiplying $m_1$ by $x_1$. It multiplies $x_2$.
- Writing the ratio the wrong way round: $AP : PB$ is not the same as $PB : AP$.
- Forgetting that a point on the $x$-axis has $y = 0$, not $x = 0$.
- Using the mid-point formula with a subtraction instead of an addition.

@@formulas
Section formula ($m_1 : m_2$) | $\left(\dfrac{m_1x_2 + m_2x_1}{m_1 + m_2},\ \dfrac{m_1y_2 + m_2y_1}{m_1 + m_2}\right)$
Section formula ($k : 1$) | $\left(\dfrac{kx_2 + x_1}{k + 1},\ \dfrac{ky_2 + y_1}{k + 1}\right)$
Mid-point formula | $\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$
Centroid of a triangle | $\left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2 + x_3}{3},\ \dfrac{y_1 + y_2 + y_3}{3}\right)$
Centroid on a median | Divides it in the ratio $2 : 1$ from the vertex
Points of trisection | Ratios $1 : 2$ and $2 : 1$
Point on the x-axis | Ordinate $= 0$
Point on the y-axis | Abscissa $= 0$
Parallelogram | The diagonals have the same mid-point
Centre of a circle | Mid-point of any diameter
