T: Complementary events
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] If the probability of a player winning a game is 0.79, then the probability of his losing the same game is: (a) 1.79  (b) 0.31  (c) 0.21%  (d) 0.21
S: $P(\text{losing}) = 1 - P(\text{winning}) = 1 - 0.79 = 0.21$
S: **Answer: (d)**

T: Theoretical probability
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] From the data 1, 4, 7, 9, 16, 21, 25, if all the even numbers are removed, then the probability of getting at random a prime number from the remaining is: (a) $\dfrac{2}{5}$  (b) $\dfrac{1}{5}$  (c) $\dfrac{1}{7}$  (d) $\dfrac{2}{7}$
S: Removing the even numbers 4 and 16 leaves 1, 7, 9, 21, 25: five numbers.
S: The only prime among them is 7 (1 is not prime; $9 = 3^2$, $21 = 3 \times 7$, $25 = 5^2$).
S: Probability $= \dfrac{1}{5}$
S: **Answer: (b)**

T: Two dice
Q: [CBSE 2024, 1 mark] Two dice are rolled together. The probability of getting sum of numbers on the two dice as 2, 3 or 5, is: (a) $\dfrac{7}{36}$  (b) $\dfrac{11}{36}$  (c) $\dfrac{5}{36}$  (d) $\dfrac{4}{9}$
S: Sum 2: $(1,1)$. Sum 3: $(1,2), (2,1)$. Sum 5: $(1,4), (2,3), (3,2), (4,1)$.
S: Favourable outcomes $= 1 + 2 + 4 = 7$ out of 36.
S: **Answer: (a)**

T: Playing cards
Q: [CBSE 2024, 2 marks] In a pack of 52 playing cards one card is lost. From the remaining cards, a card is drawn at random. Find the probability that the drawn card is queen of heart, if the lost card is a black card.
S: One black card is lost, so 51 cards remain.
S: The queen of hearts is a red card, so it is still in the pack.
S: **Answer: $\dfrac{1}{51}$**
