@title Arithmetic Progressions
@summary An arithmetic progression is a list of numbers that goes up or down by the same amount each time. Two formulas do most of the work: one for the nth term and one for the sum of the first n terms.

# What is an AP?
- An **arithmetic progression (AP)** is a list of numbers in which each term is obtained by adding a fixed number to the term before it.
- The fixed number is the **common difference** $d$. It can be positive, negative or zero.
$$d = a_2 - a_1 = a_3 - a_2 = \dots = a_{n} - a_{n-1}$$
- With first term $a$, the general form of an AP is $a,\ a + d,\ a + 2d,\ a + 3d,\ \dots$
- A **finite** AP has a last term; an **infinite** AP does not.
- To test whether a list is an AP, check that the differences between consecutive terms are all equal.
?eg Which of these are APs? (i) $4, 10, 16, 22, \dots$  (ii) $1, 3, 9, 27, \dots$  (iii) $5, 5, 5, 5, \dots$
= (i) Differences: $6, 6, 6$. Equal, so it is an AP with $d = 6$.
= (ii) Differences: $2, 6, 18$. Not equal.
= (iii) Differences: $0, 0, 0$. Equal, so it is an AP with $d = 0$.
=> (i) and (iii) are APs; (ii) is not.

# The nth term
- The $n$th term (general term) of an AP with first term $a$ and common difference $d$ is
$$a_n = a + (n - 1)d$$
- If the AP has a last term $l$, the $n$th term **from the end** is $l - (n - 1)d$.
?eg Find the 20th term of the AP $7, 11, 15, \dots$
= $a = 7$, $d = 4$, $n = 20$
= $a_{20} = 7 + (20 - 1) \times 4 = 7 + 76$
=> $a_{20} = 83$
?eg Which term of the AP $5, 9, 13, \dots$ is 125?
= $a = 5$, $d = 4$. Let $a_n = 125$: $5 + (n - 1) \times 4 = 125$
= $(n - 1) \times 4 = 120$, so $n - 1 = 30$
=> 125 is the 31st term.
?eg Is 100 a term of the AP $3, 7, 11, \dots$?
= $3 + (n - 1) \times 4 = 100$ gives $n - 1 = \dfrac{97}{4}$, which is not a whole number.
=> No, 100 is not a term of this AP.
?eg The 4th term of an AP is 11 and its 9th term is 26. Find the AP.
= $a + 3d = 11$ and $a + 8d = 26$
= Subtract: $5d = 15$, so $d = 3$ and $a = 11 - 9 = 2$
=> The AP is $2, 5, 8, 11, \dots$
?eg How many three-digit numbers are divisible by 7?
= The numbers are $105, 112, 119, \dots, 994$: an AP with $a = 105$, $d = 7$, $l = 994$.
= $105 + (n - 1) \times 7 = 994$, so $n - 1 = \dfrac{889}{7} = 127$
=> There are 128 such numbers.
?eg Find the 8th term from the end of the AP $7, 10, 13, \dots, 184$.
= $l = 184$, $d = 3$
= 8th term from the end $= 184 - (8 - 1) \times 3 = 184 - 21$
=> 163
!tip $n$ is a count of terms, so it must be a positive whole number. If you get a fraction or a negative value for $n$, the number is not a term of the AP.

# Sum of the first n terms
- The sum of the first $n$ terms of an AP is
$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}\,[\,2a + (n - 1)d\,]$$
- If the last term $l$ is known, use the shorter form
$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}\,(a + l)$$
- The $n$th term can be found from the sums: $a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$.
- The sum of the first $n$ positive integers is $\dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2}$.
?eg Find the sum of the first 20 terms of the AP $5, 8, 11, \dots$
= $a = 5$, $d = 3$, $n = 20$
= $S_{20} = \dfrac{20}{2}\,[\,2 \times 5 + 19 \times 3\,] = 10 \times 67$
=> $S_{20} = 670$
?eg Find the sum of all multiples of 6 between 1 and 200.
= The multiples are $6, 12, 18, \dots, 198$, with $a = 6$, $d = 6$, $l = 198$.
= Number of terms: $6 + (n - 1) \times 6 = 198$ gives $n = 33$.
= $S_{33} = \dfrac{33}{2}\,(6 + 198) = 33 \times 102$
=> The sum is 3366.
?eg How many terms of the AP $3, 7, 11, \dots$ must be added to get a sum of 210?
= $\dfrac{n}{2}\,[\,6 + (n - 1) \times 4\,] = 210$, so $n(2n + 1) = 210$ and $2n^2 + n - 210 = 0$
= $n = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 1680}}{4} = \dfrac{-1 \pm 41}{4}$, so $n = 10$ or $n = -\dfrac{21}{2}$
= $n$ must be a positive whole number.
=> 10 terms.
?eg The sum of the first $n$ terms of an AP is $S_n = 3n^2 + 5n$. Find its $n$th term and the common difference.
= $a_n = S_n - S_{n-1} = (3n^2 + 5n) - [\,3(n - 1)^2 + 5(n - 1)\,]$
= $= 3n^2 + 5n - 3n^2 + 6n - 3 - 5n + 5 = 6n + 2$
= $a_1 = 8$, $a_2 = 14$, so $d = 6$
=> $a_n = 6n + 2$ and $d = 6$

# Word problems
- Decide first whether the question asks for a **term** (use $a_n$) or a **total** (use $S_n$).
- Write down $a$, $d$ and $n$ before using a formula.
?eg Rekha saves ₹50 in the first week and increases her weekly saving by ₹15 every week. How much does she save in total in 12 weeks?
= The savings form an AP with $a = 50$, $d = 15$, $n = 12$.
= $S_{12} = \dfrac{12}{2}\,[\,2 \times 50 + 11 \times 15\,] = 6 \times 265$
=> She saves ₹1590 in 12 weeks.
?eg The first term of an AP is 5, the last term is 45 and the sum of all its terms is 400. Find the number of terms and the common difference.
= $S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}\,(a + l)$ gives $400 = \dfrac{n}{2} \times 50$, so $n = 16$
= $a_{16} = 45$: $5 + 15d = 45$, so $d = \dfrac{8}{3}$
=> 16 terms; $d = \dfrac{8}{3}$

# Useful ideas
- Three numbers in AP: take them as $a - d$, $a$, $a + d$. Their sum is $3a$.
- Four numbers in AP: $a - 3d$, $a - d$, $a + d$, $a + 3d$.
- $a$, $b$, $c$ are in AP if and only if $2b = a + c$. Then $b$ is the **arithmetic mean** of $a$ and $c$.
- If every term of an AP is increased, decreased, multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number, the result is still an AP.

# Common mistakes
- Using $n$ instead of $n - 1$ in $a_n = a + (n - 1)d$.
- Finding $d$ as $a_1 - a_2$. It is $a_2 - a_1$.
- Mixing up $a_n$ (one term) with $S_n$ (a total).
- Accepting a negative or fractional value of $n$.
- Forgetting that a decreasing AP has a negative $d$.

@@formulas
General form of an AP | $a,\ a + d,\ a + 2d,\ a + 3d,\ \dots$
Common difference | $d = a_{n} - a_{n-1}$
nth term | $a_n = a + (n - 1)d$
nth term from the end | $l - (n - 1)d$
Number of terms | $n = \dfrac{l - a}{d} + 1$
Sum of the first n terms | $S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}\,[\,2a + (n - 1)d\,]$
Sum when the last term is known | $S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}\,(a + l)$
nth term from the sums | $a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$
Sum of the first n positive integers | $1 + 2 + \dots + n = \dfrac{n(n + 1)}{2}$
Sum of the first n odd numbers | $1 + 3 + 5 + \dots + (2n - 1) = n^2$
Sum of the first n even numbers | $2 + 4 + \dots + 2n = n(n + 1)$
Three terms in AP | $a - d,\ a,\ a + d$
Condition for an AP | $a$, $b$, $c$ are in AP if and only if $2b = a + c$
Arithmetic mean of a and c | $b = \dfrac{a + c}{2}$

Q: Find the common difference and the next two terms of the AP $-5, -1, 3, 7, \dots$
A: $d = 4$; next terms 11 and 15
Q: Find the 25th term of the AP $12, 7, 2, \dots$
A: $12 + 24 \times (-5) = -108$
Q: Which term of the AP $4, 9, 14, \dots$ is 254?
A: The 51st term
Q: The 5th term of an AP is 20 and the 12th term is 48. Find the first term and the common difference.
A: $a = 4$, $d = 4$
Q: How many two-digit numbers are divisible by 4?
A: $12, 16, \dots, 96$: 22 numbers
Q: Find the 6th term from the end of the AP $8, 15, 22, \dots, 148$.
A: $148 - 5 \times 7 = 113$
Q: Find the sum of the first 30 terms of the AP $2, 6, 10, \dots$
A: $15\,[\,4 + 116\,] = 1800$
Q: Find the sum of all odd numbers between 0 and 60.
A: $1 + 3 + \dots + 59 = 30^2 = 900$
Q: How many terms of the AP $20, 18, 16, \dots$ must be added to get a sum of 110? Explain the two answers.
A: $n = 10$ or $n = 11$; the 11th term is 0, so both sums are 110.
Q: A sum of ₹1000 is to be paid in 10 instalments that form an AP. If the first instalment is ₹55, find the common difference and the last instalment.
A: $d = 10$; last instalment ₹145
