@title Some Applications of Trigonometry
@summary Trigonometric ratios let us find heights and distances that cannot be measured directly, using an angle of elevation or depression and one known length.

# Line of sight and the two angles
- The **line of sight** is the line drawn from the eye of the observer to the point of the object being viewed.
- The **angle of elevation** is the angle between the horizontal and the line of sight when the object is **above** the horizontal level. The observer looks up.
- The **angle of depression** is the angle between the horizontal and the line of sight when the object is **below** the horizontal level. The observer looks down.
- The angle of depression of a point $B$ seen from $A$ equals the angle of elevation of $A$ seen from $B$, because they are alternate angles between parallel horizontal lines.
!tip Both angles are measured from the **horizontal**, never from the vertical.

# Choosing the ratio
- Draw a figure. Mark the right angle, the given angle and the known length.
- Name the side you know and the side you want, relative to the given angle.
| Sides involved | Ratio to use |
| Opposite and adjacent (height and ground distance) | $\tan$ |
| Opposite and hypotenuse (height and slant length) | $\sin$ |
| Adjacent and hypotenuse (ground distance and slant length) | $\cos$ |
- Values used most often: $\tan 30° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, $\tan 45° = 1$, $\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}$. Take $\sqrt{3} = 1.732$ unless told otherwise.

# One right-angled triangle
?eg The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground 20 m from its foot is $60°$. Find the height of the tower.
= Let the height be $h$ m. The height is opposite to the angle and the 20 m is adjacent.
= $\tan 60° = \dfrac{h}{20}$, so $h = 20\sqrt{3}$
=> Height $= 20\sqrt{3} \approx 34.64$ m
?eg A ladder 10 m long leans against a wall and makes an angle of $60°$ with the ground. Find the height it reaches on the wall and the distance of its foot from the wall.
= The ladder is the hypotenuse. Height: $\sin 60° = \dfrac{h}{10}$, so $h = 10 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}$ m
= Distance: $\cos 60° = \dfrac{d}{10}$, so $d = 10 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 5$ m
=> Height $= 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.66$ m; distance $= 5$ m
?eg From the top of a building 60 m high, the angle of depression of a car on the ground is $30°$. Find the distance of the car from the foot of the building.
= The angle of elevation of the top of the building from the car is also $30°$ (alternate angles).
= $\tan 30° = \dfrac{60}{d}$, so $d = 60\sqrt{3}$
=> Distance $= 60\sqrt{3} \approx 103.92$ m

# Two right-angled triangles
- Many problems have two triangles that share a side. Take the unknown shared side (or the unknown height) as $x$ or $h$, write one equation from each triangle and solve.
?eg The shadow of a tower on level ground is 30 m longer when the sun's altitude is $30°$ than when it is $60°$. Find the height of the tower.
= Let the height be $h$ m and the shorter shadow be $x$ m.
= At $60°$: $\tan 60° = \dfrac{h}{x}$, so $h = x\sqrt{3}$
= At $30°$: $\tan 30° = \dfrac{h}{x + 30}$, so $x + 30 = h\sqrt{3} = 3x$
= $x = 15$, so $h = 15\sqrt{3}$
=> Height $= 15\sqrt{3} \approx 25.98$ m
?eg From the top of a building 10 m high, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is $45°$ and the angle of depression of its foot is $30°$. Find the height of the tower.
= Let the horizontal distance between the building and the tower be $d$ m.
= Depression of the foot: $\tan 30° = \dfrac{10}{d}$, so $d = 10\sqrt{3}$ m
= Elevation of the top: the tower rises $y$ m above the level of the roof, where $\tan 45° = \dfrac{y}{d}$, so $y = 10\sqrt{3}$ m
= Height of the tower $= 10 + 10\sqrt{3} = 10(1 + \sqrt{3})$
=> Height $\approx 27.32$ m
?eg The angles of elevation of the top of a tower from two points on the same side of it, 5 m and 20 m from its base and in the same straight line with it, are complementary. Find the height of the tower.
= Let the height be $h$ m and the angles be $\theta$ and $90° - \theta$.
= From the nearer point: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{h}{5}$
= From the farther point: $\tan(90° - \theta) = \cot\theta = \dfrac{h}{20}$
= Multiply: $\tan\theta \times \cot\theta = \dfrac{h^2}{100}$, so $h^2 = 100$
=> Height $= 10$ m
?eg A boy 1.5 m tall stands 28.5 m from a building 30 m high. Find the angle of elevation of the top of the building from his eyes.
= Height of the top above eye level $= 30 - 1.5 = 28.5$ m
= $\tan\theta = \dfrac{28.5}{28.5} = 1$
=> The angle of elevation is $45°$.

# Steps for every problem
- Read the problem and draw a neat figure. Label every point.
- Mark the angles of elevation and depression from the horizontal.
- Choose the unknowns and write a trigonometric equation from each right-angled triangle.
- Solve, give the answer with its unit, and round off only at the end.

# Common mistakes
- Measuring the angle of depression from the vertical instead of the horizontal.
- Forgetting the height of the observer's eyes: subtract it before using the ratio, or add it back at the end.
- Using $\tan$ with the slant length. The slant length is the hypotenuse, so use $\sin$ or $\cos$.
- Rationalising too early and making arithmetic slips; keep $\sqrt{3}$ as it is until the last step.

@@formulas
Angle of elevation | Angle between the horizontal and the line of sight, object above eye level
Angle of depression | Angle between the horizontal and the line of sight, object below eye level
Elevation and depression | Angle of depression of $B$ from $A$ = angle of elevation of $A$ from $B$
Height and ground distance | $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{distance}}$, so $\text{height} = \text{distance} \times \tan\theta$
Height and slant length | $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{slant length}}$
Ground distance and slant length | $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{slant length}}$
tan of standard angles | $\tan 30° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, $\tan 45° = 1$, $\tan 60° = \sqrt{3}$
sin of standard angles | $\sin 30° = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $\sin 45° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, $\sin 60° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
cos of standard angles | $\cos 30° = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\cos 45° = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, $\cos 60° = \dfrac{1}{2}$
Complementary angles | $\tan(90° - \theta) = \cot\theta$, so $\tan\theta \times \tan(90° - \theta) = 1$
Useful values | $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$, $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$

@@dpp
Q: A tower stands on level ground. From a point 30 m from its foot, the angle of elevation of its top is $30°$. Find the height of the tower.
A: $\dfrac{30}{\sqrt{3}} = 10\sqrt{3} \approx 17.32$ m
Q: A kite is flying at a height of 75 m above the ground. Its string makes an angle of $60°$ with the ground. Find the length of the string, assuming there is no slack.
A: $\dfrac{75}{\sin 60°} = 50\sqrt{3} \approx 86.6$ m
Q: A pole 6 m high casts a shadow $2\sqrt{3}$ m long. Find the sun's angle of elevation.
A: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{6}{2\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}$, so $\theta = 60°$
Q: A tree breaks and its top touches the ground 8 m from its foot, making an angle of $30°$ with the ground. Find the original height of the tree.
A: $\dfrac{8}{\sqrt{3}} + \dfrac{16}{\sqrt{3}} = 8\sqrt{3} \approx 13.86$ m
Q: From the top of a cliff 75 m high, the angle of depression of a boat is $60°$. Find the distance of the boat from the foot of the cliff.
A: $\dfrac{75}{\sqrt{3}} = 25\sqrt{3} \approx 43.3$ m
Q: An observer 1.5 m tall is 20.5 m from a tower 22 m high. Find the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from his eyes.
A: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{20.5}{20.5} = 1$, so $\theta = 45°$
Q: From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of a flagstaff fixed on a building 20 m high are $45°$ and $60°$. Find the height of the flagstaff.
A: $20\sqrt{3} - 20 = 20(\sqrt{3} - 1) \approx 14.64$ m
Q: Two poles of equal height stand on opposite sides of a road 100 m wide. From a point on the road between them, the angles of elevation of their tops are $60°$ and $30°$. Find the height of the poles and the distances of the point from them.
A: Height $25\sqrt{3} \approx 43.3$ m; distances 25 m and 75 m
Q: From the top of a 12 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is $60°$ and the angle of depression of its foot is $45°$. Find the height of the tower.
A: $12(1 + \sqrt{3}) \approx 32.78$ m
Q: The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground is $30°$. On walking 20 m towards the tower, the angle becomes $60°$. Find the height of the tower.
A: $10\sqrt{3} \approx 17.32$ m
