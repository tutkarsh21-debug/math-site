T: Theoretical probability
Q: [CBSE 2025, 1 mark] Assertion (A): When one number is picked at random from the numbers 1 to 20, the probability that it is a number from 1 to 20 is 1. Reason (R): An event $E$ with $P(E) = 1$ is a sure event. Choose: (a) both true and R explains A  (b) both true but R does not explain A  (c) A true, R false  (d) A false, R true
S: Every possible outcome is a number from 1 to 20, so the event is certain to happen and its probability is $\dfrac{20}{20} = 1$. A is true.
S: R is true, and it is exactly the reason for A.
S: **Answer: (a)**

T: Two dice
Q: [CBSE 2025, 3 marks] Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the two numbers differ by 2.
S: There are $6 \times 6 = 36$ equally likely outcomes.
S: Pairs that differ by 2: $(1,3), (3,1), (2,4), (4,2), (3,5), (5,3), (4,6), (6,4)$, which is 8 outcomes.
S: Probability $= \dfrac{8}{36}$
S: **Answer: $\dfrac{2}{9}$**

T: Playing cards
Q: [CBSE 2025, 1 mark] One card is drawn at random from a well-shuffled pack of 52 cards. Find the probability that it is a red face card.
S: The face cards are the jack, queen and king. The two red suits (hearts and diamonds) have $2 \times 3 = 6$ of them.
S: Probability $= \dfrac{6}{52}$
S: **Answer: $\dfrac{3}{26}$**
