@title Arithmetic Progression
@summary An arithmetic progression is a sequence in which each term differs from the one before it by a fixed number. This chapter covers its general term, the sum of its terms and the arithmetic mean.

# Sequence, series and A.P.
- A **sequence** is a list of numbers written in a definite order by a rule. When the terms are joined by plus signs, it is a **series**.
- An **arithmetic progression (A.P.)** is a sequence in which the difference between any term and the term before it is constant.
- The constant is the **common difference** $d$, and the first term is $a$.
$$d = t_2 - t_1 = t_3 - t_2 = \dots \qquad \text{General A.P.: } a,\ a + d,\ a + 2d,\ a + 3d,\ \dots$$
- $d$ can be positive (terms increase), negative (terms decrease) or zero (all terms equal).
?eg Is the sequence $-8, -5, -2, 1, \dots$ an A.P.? If so, find its common difference and the next term.
= $-5 - (-8) = 3$, $-2 - (-5) = 3$, $1 - (-2) = 3$. The differences are equal.
=> Yes, it is an A.P. with $d = 3$; the next term is 4.

# The general term
- The $n$th term (general term) of an A.P. is
$$t_n = a + (n - 1)d$$
- If the last term is $l$, the $n$th term from the end is $l - (n - 1)d$.
- A sequence is an A.P. exactly when its $n$th term is a linear expression in $n$, such as $t_n = 5n - 2$.
?eg Find the 15th term of the A.P. $6, 10, 14, \dots$
= $a = 6$, $d = 4$
= $t_{15} = 6 + 14 \times 4$
=> $t_{15} = 62$
?eg Which term of the A.P. $3, 8, 13, \dots$ is 148?
= $3 + (n - 1) \times 5 = 148$, so $n - 1 = 29$
=> The 30th term.
?eg The 6th term of an A.P. is 19 and its 16th term is 59. Find the first term, the common difference and the general term.
= $a + 5d = 19$ and $a + 15d = 59$
= Subtract: $10d = 40$, so $d = 4$ and $a = 19 - 20 = -1$
= $t_n = -1 + (n - 1) \times 4 = 4n - 5$
=> $a = -1$, $d = 4$, $t_n = 4n - 5$
?eg The $n$th term of a sequence is $t_n = 7 - 3n$. Show that it is an A.P. and find its common difference.
= $t_{n} - t_{n-1} = (7 - 3n) - [\,7 - 3(n - 1)\,] = -3$, which is constant.
=> It is an A.P. with $d = -3$.

# Sum of n terms
$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}\,[\,2a + (n - 1)d\,] \qquad S_n = \frac{n}{2}\,(a + l)$$
- Use the second form when the last term $l$ is known.
- $t_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$.
?eg Find the sum of the first 25 terms of the A.P. $4, 7, 10, \dots$
= $a = 4$, $d = 3$, $n = 25$
= $S_{25} = \dfrac{25}{2}\,[\,8 + 24 \times 3\,] = \dfrac{25}{2} \times 80$
=> $S_{25} = 1000$
?eg Find the sum of all two-digit numbers that are divisible by 5.
= The numbers are $10, 15, 20, \dots, 95$: $a = 10$, $d = 5$, $l = 95$.
= $10 + (n - 1) \times 5 = 95$ gives $n = 18$
= $S_{18} = \dfrac{18}{2}\,(10 + 95) = 9 \times 105$
=> The sum is 945.
?eg How many terms of the A.P. $27, 24, 21, \dots$ must be taken so that their sum is 0?
= $\dfrac{n}{2}\,[\,54 + (n - 1)(-3)\,] = 0$, so $n(57 - 3n) = 0$
= $n = 0$ is rejected.
=> $n = 19$ terms.

# Arithmetic mean
- If $a$, $A$, $b$ are in A.P., then $A$ is the **arithmetic mean** of $a$ and $b$.
$$A = \frac{a + b}{2}$$
- Three numbers $a$, $b$, $c$ are in A.P. if and only if $2b = a + c$.
?eg Find the value of $k$ for which $2k + 1$, $13$ and $5k - 3$ are three consecutive terms of an A.P.
= $2 \times 13 = (2k + 1) + (5k - 3)$
= $26 = 7k - 2$
=> $k = 4$

# Choosing terms conveniently
| Number of terms | Take them as |
| 3 | $a - d,\ a,\ a + d$ |
| 4 | $a - 3d,\ a - d,\ a + d,\ a + 3d$ |
| 5 | $a - 2d,\ a - d,\ a,\ a + d,\ a + 2d$ |
?eg The sum of three numbers in A.P. is 24 and their product is 440. Find the numbers.
= Let the numbers be $a - d$, $a$, $a + d$. Their sum is $3a = 24$, so $a = 8$.
= Product: $8(8 - d)(8 + d) = 440$, so $64 - d^2 = 55$ and $d = \pm 3$
=> The numbers are 5, 8 and 11.

# Common mistakes
- Using $n$ in place of $n - 1$ in the general term.
- Taking $d$ as (first term $-$ second term).
- Accepting a value of $n$ that is negative, zero or a fraction.
- Confusing $t_n$ (a term) with $S_n$ (a sum).

Q: Find the common difference and the 10th term of the A.P. $11, 7, 3, \dots$
A: $d = -4$; $t_{10} = 11 - 36 = -25$
Q: Which term of the A.P. $2, 9, 16, \dots$ is 149?
A: The 22nd term
Q: Is 68 a term of the A.P. $7, 10, 13, \dots$?
A: $n - 1 = \dfrac{61}{3}$, not a whole number; no
Q: The 3rd term of an A.P. is 12 and the 8th term is 37. Find the 20th term.
A: $d = 5$, $a = 2$; $t_{20} = 97$
Q: Find the general term of the A.P. $9, 14, 19, \dots$
A: $t_n = 5n + 4$
Q: Find the sum of the first 16 terms of the A.P. $10, 6, 2, \dots$
A: $8\,[\,20 - 60\,] = -320$
Q: Find the sum of all natural numbers between 100 and 200 that are divisible by 9.
A: $108 + 117 + \dots + 198$: 11 terms, sum $= 1683$
Q: The sum of the first $n$ terms of an A.P. is $2n^2 + 3n$. Find its 10th term.
A: $S_{10} - S_9 = 230 - 189 = 41$
Q: Find the arithmetic mean of 14 and 38, and the value of $x$ if $x + 2$, $3x$, $4x + 1$ are in A.P.
A: 26; $6x = 5x + 3$, so $x = 3$
Q: The sum of three numbers in A.P. is 30 and the sum of their squares is 318. Find the numbers.
A: $a = 10$, $2d^2 = 18$; 7, 10, 13
